## Description
A **statue of a golden dragon** standing on a large plinth at the centre of
[[the-golden-courtyard|the golden courtyard]], beneath [[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]].

- **Size:** about **twelve to fourteen feet** from the base of the plinth to the top, and roughly
  **eighteen feet** in overall length — the neck sinuously extended, the tail curled around the body.
  Far too large and heavy for the party to carry, though the magic makes it turn easily.
- **Depiction:** **naturalistic and true to life**, of very fine work — the sort of likeness made when
  dragons were still commonly depicted. (Depictions were plentiful until comparatively recently; see
  [[the-disappearance-of-dragons]].)
- **Material:** it **appears to be gold**. [[mags|Mags]] tested it with a fingernail (Arcana 19) and
  felt a moment of soft-metal **give** before a **protective enchantment engaged** and the surface
  hardened. She took no harm; the effect simply protects the statue.
- **Condition:** completely **perfect and very shiny**, while the courtyard around it has decayed.

## The inscription
Around the base runs an inscription in **Draconic script** — not written quite the way modern Draconic
is, but close enough to read. [[trevick|Trevick]] parses it through his
[[glasses-of-comprehend-languages|Comprehend Languages glasses]] (S2.33):

---

**The Lord of Light greets the dawn of a new age.**

---

**Nobody recognises the phrase.** Every history check the party made fell short; who the "Lord of
Light" is, and what age was being greeted, are **unknown**.

## Its magic (S2.33)
[[caspian-talon|Caspian]]'s **Detect Magic** plus an Arcana check establishes three things:

- **Abjuration**, powerful and actively working. The party takes this to be what holds the crushing
  rock off the courtyard, though what would happen if it failed is beyond them. Crucially, the ward on
  the hidden **flagstone is bound to this one** — the DM warned that dispelling one would take the
  other with it, which is why the party did not risk **Dispel Magic**.
- **Evocation** — a capacity to **emit** magical energy.
- Separately, a capacity to **absorb** magical energy — the kind of signature Caspian knows from
  objects that drink magic rather than shed it.

The absorption is real and indiscriminate. Cantrip **Light** visibly diminishes where it touches the
statue; **Sorcerous Burst** (fire, then every damage type it offers in turn), **Vicious Mockery**,
and everything else the party threw at it were **absorbed without effect or mark**. Magic does not
generally dissipate, so the statue **may** be storing what it takes — but the party saw **no evidence**
of accumulation, and repeated blasts did it no damage.

## The mechanism
The **upper part of the plinth rotates**, though the base does not (so there are no drag-marks on the
floor). [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] spotted both the rotation and **signs that it has been rotated
before** — the magic distorts any sense of how recently. Turning it is easy for two or three people
working together; the weight is barely apparent, and the motion feels **designed**.

Rotation alone does nothing: the party swung it 180°, then a full circle clockwise, then a full circle
anticlockwise, with Detect Magic up, and observed **no change** at all.

What worked (S2.33) was **radiant magic delivered while the dragon faced the courtyard's original
east** — the direction of **dawn** in the courtyard's original construction and position, recovered by
[[mags|Mags]] from the **weathering on the tiles**, which is also the direction of the hidden
flagstone.
Mags's **Guiding Bolt** was absorbed like everything else, but the statue then **glowed from within**
and threw a **beam of light onto the flagstone**, which lifted freely by hand for as long as the beam
lasted. Beneath it lay the [[the-draconic-regalia|regalia cache]].

**Both halves matter.** Radiant magic alone is not the key: the facing must be **east relative to the
courtyard's original construction and position**, not east by present compass. That is why the party's
earlier rotations — tracked against Caspian's compass — achieved nothing, and why Mags's reading of the
tile-weathering was the step that unlocked it.

## History
Unknown. It stands at the centre of [[the-golden-courtyard|a courtyard]] that is demonstrably **no
longer in its original position or orientation**, and the party takes it to be both that courtyard's
defence and its lock — a reading of the evidence, not something established. The party left it **restored to its
original facing**.
