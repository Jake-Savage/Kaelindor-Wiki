## Overview
**Grommar's Path** is the name the [[the-gleaners|gleaners]] give to the deep beyond the
[[the-custodian|gate]] at the western face of [[the-stone-river|the stone river]] — where, they say,
"the bigger folk" and the priests closer to "God" dwell, and where they themselves never go (S2.31). It
is named for the legendary dwarven smith [[grommar|Grommar]]. Both the gleaners and the gate itself
call it **deadly**: the face at the gate reckoned the party would "fall and fail," and let them pass
only as a **waste** it was curious enough to permit (S2.33).

The party entered on **30 Dusklin 1568**, at the end of S2.33, and the stone **closed behind them**.

## What is known of it
- **The way in is a dwarven sacred way.** Beyond the gate the passage is deliberately **hewn**, not raw
  rock, and slopes **steeply downward**, going much deeper into the earth. [[mags|Mags]],
  [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] and [[rell-aetris|Rell]] each recognise the style: the sort of **narrow
  ceremonial passageway that leads into the more seriously sacred and secretive dwarven temples of
  [[calvyr|Calvyr]]**. It is unlit.
- **No way back this way.** The gate said plainly that the party would not return by this route, and
  the opening sealed behind them.
- **Other watchers below.** The gate spoke of **"the eyes of God below"** as its **brothers**, and
  doubted they would pass news of the party back up to it (S2.33).
- **The gleaners' account (S2.31, unverified):** priests, wise men and holy men who commune with "God"
  more directly live down here, and a place called **Dunton** is rumoured deeper still, where "godless
  who found a place" live.

## The trapped corridor (S2.34)
Beyond the entrance the way is a **stone corridor**, cleanly dressed, on a **gentle downward slope**,
running some **90 to 100 feet** before it turns. It is unlit, and the **stink** grows steadily worse the
deeper it goes. A survival check finds nothing acutely poisonous in the air — it is **unpleasant rather
than dangerous**, though not air to breathe indefinitely. A flame held in it burns with a **faint
bluish tinge at the edges**, from which [[rell-aetris|Rell]] infers a natural build-up of **methane**
and has the party douse open flames.

**It is a Calvyr temple.** The walls carry **devotional bas-reliefs of the
[[the-seven-saints-of-calvyr|seven classical saints of Calvyr]]** — all seven, one relief each —
alternating from side to side every ten to fifteen feet. Its metalwork is treated against corrosion in the way religious dwarven spaces
normally are. The reliefs are in a **different style from the engravings at the complex's entrance
above** (DM, confirmed) — two builds, not one.

**And it is comprehensively trapped** — the trap-ports are cut **into and around** the reliefs, so the
trapping came **after** the devotional work. The party got through the following:

- **Needles or gas**, beside Orvan Smokeglass's relief: a row of very small holes with wires inside.
  Already **triggered** — recently, and almost certainly by [[mags|Mags]] and [[caspian-talon|Caspian]]
  walking up to the carving without noticing — and its mechanism no longer operable. What set it off
  was never determined.
- **A ceiling hammer**: a huge war-hammer-shaped weight on a chain, over a patch of floor **broken and
  mended more than once**. The party edged round it and then deliberately triggered it from a safe
  distance by leaning on [[mags|Mags]]'s staff, which the hammer **destroyed**. It cracks the floor
  heavily and stays down. The space above it is small, holding a **gear** the chain winds around and
  little else — [[mags|Mags]] had hoped for a maintenance passage running past the traps, on the
  reasoning that Calvyrans believe in repairs and would have had a protocol for them. There is room to
  climb up, and not much to find.
- **A spike drop**: a narrow rectangular opening in the ceiling, with a line of impact points about
  eight inches apart in the floor beneath it. The party simply **jumped the line**, Rell marking the
  safe standing spot with a chalk **X**.
- **Two portcullises and a sweeping axe**, at Vero's relief — the trap sprung when Rell moved in to prise
  out the relief's **green gem eyes**. Grates fall about 20 feet apart, sealing the party between them,
  while a very large, slender **axe-blade** sweeps across the corridor from a wall slit roughly **every
  30 to 40 seconds**. As it runs, small **rectangular gaps open along the floor** on both sides where
  tiles subtly sink — noticed by [[trevick|Trevick]], and still unexplained. Rell defeated it with a
  **pulley**: two pitons, a rope run from the far portcullis and pulled taut across the axe's arc, so
  that the axe's own momentum **hauled the grate up**. The DM confirmed afterwards that the trap would
  have **escalated on its seventh swing**, and that Rell arrested it on the sixth.
- **Warded stones**, at Sargrim Hexward's relief: some but not all of the stones of the floor and the
  wall beneath the carving carry very faint marks — **not carvings, more like shadows on the stone** —
  which read as the residue of magic cast upon them. Rell picked a path between them and **chalked it
  out** for the others rather than talking them through it.

The relief of the **brewing saint** was the one at which the party found **no trap**. Beside it, cut
into the floor, is the Dwarvish inscription **[[khazar-vuk-varag|"Khazar Vuk Varag — I defy you"]]**, carved
later than the corridor and with care.

Left behind, deliberately: the **potent runes** on Sargrim Hexward's relief, which would be valuable and
possibly useful if chiselled out, but would take time the party judged it did not have. Taken: the
**emeralds** from Vero's eyes, plus one **darker, purplish stone** nobody has identified.

## The stair and below
At its end the corridor turns a **right angle to the right** onto a flight of stone steps descending in
an irregular, almost square-spiral about **50 feet**, then opening into one very steep flight down to
**[[the-foul-pool|the foul pool]]** — a wide chamber of warm, filthy water where **construct spiders**
nest over the only apparent way on. The stairs themselves proved to be a **trap**: they **snapped flat
into a slope** and pitched the party into the water at the end of S2.34.

In S2.35 the party fought its way out of that pool — past the **five-headed hydra** living in it —
destroyed the spiders with an upcast **Shatter**, and climbed the blade-trapped carving into
**[[the-ancient-forge|an ancient dwarven forge]]**, which [[rell-aetris|Rell]] judges to be the
**temple complex proper**. The corridor, the pool and the forge were all originally **one structure**,
since crushed out of shape. Two doors lead deeper from the forge.

## DM notes
- The Calvyr-temple styling recognised at the gate in S2.33 is borne out: the corridor's reliefs are
  Calvyran devotional work, and S2.35 puts the party in the temple complex proper. Whose temple it was,
  and how [[grommar|Grommar]] relates to it, are still open.
- The party carries the gate's **"acknowledgement and blessing"** to show below — which [[mags|Mags]]
  read (natural-20 Insight) as something the creature **has no actual power to grant**.
- **S2.34 additions.** The entrance passage opens into a **Calvyr temple corridor** — which upgrades
  the S2.33 recognition of *architectural type* into the corridor's own **devotional content**. Whose
  temple it was, who built it, and why it sits inside the [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] are still
  open.
- **The traps are later than the temple**, being cut into and around the reliefs — so the trapper and
  the builder are two different hands. **Who laid them is unknown.** The party assumes
  [[grommar|Grommar]], on the strength of the legend calling him trap-happy and the path bearing his
  name; the DM's only comment was to wonder how far a man insane by the end of his life felt bound by
  Calvyran propriety.
- The **thing in the water** and the **construct spiders** are two separate unknowns, and were first
  met separately — the spiders by [[theodore|Theo]] under the canopy, the thing in the water by
  dropping rations in from the ledge.
