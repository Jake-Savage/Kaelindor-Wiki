## Overview
[[the-witch|The witch]] built this road when she fled Thal'Mirrith, so that she could live **in**
[[the-stonehunger|the Stonehunger]] without being **trapped** in it. In S2.44 she let the party and the
seventeen survivors of [[dunton|Dunton]] use it, in return for their word not to hunt
[[the-high-priest|Lae'ethorian]]. She had **refused it to him**.

## The garden (the Stonehunger end)
- From outside, **a shimmering mirage of a building and a green space** on [[the-plain|the plain]],
  west past [[the-keep|the Keep]], with a magical light that comes and goes. The Keep's patrols can see
  it from far off, but it is **gone whenever they close in**.
- **It is an illusion**, a good one spread thin over a wide area, and so easier to see through — but
  **crossing its edge needs her permission**. Without it, it seems, the illusion dissolves and you are
  somewhere else.
- Inside: **a high hedge with an Elvish gate**, grander than but in the same style as
  [[derwyth|Derwyth]]'s; **a single building** of one proper floor, its roof half covered and half a
  **roof garden**; a flourishing garden full of **illusory birds and insects**. The illusion has been
  cast so often for so long that it has **taken root** and maintains itself. What lies beneath it, the
  party could not see.
- She keeps **servants of her house**; it is not a place built for visitors.

## The road
- **An anchored crossing.** Travellers once fixed a point in the Feywild so they could reach it
  quickly; nobody does it now. The gate is **anchored** and needs little fresh magic to open. You cannot
  see what you are stepping into.
- **The Feywild beyond** is a **pinkish-purple sky** over **grey, powdery, ash-like uthrel** stretching
  to the horizon, the air thick with spores. The natural hills remain but everything that grew is
  flattened. **Nothing else lives there.** To Detect Magic it is **a blank**.
- **The path is clear but bare.** The same necromancy that holds the [[the-uthrel|uthrel]] off seems to
  suppress all life on it.
- **The wards.** Invisible; about **one every couple of hundred metres**. She first laid it with
  **necromantically charged stones** she brought from Thal'Mirrith. They faded, and
  **Lae'ethorian supplied new ones**, which are **considerably more potent** than the old. At one or two
  points the wards have weakened and the path narrows. She had not walked it herself **for three
  years**, and could not promise it was intact.
- **About a day's walk.** Don't leave the path, don't stop to sleep, and bring **no trace of the
  uthrel** out.
- **The black towers.** Far to the left of the path, on the horizon, stand **large martial buildings of
  black stone** — towers, two joined by what could be a fort wall. The witch never mentioned them.

## The far anchor
- **Just west of the Ridgeway Pass**, in the **foothills of the Stonemarch** in Kalredor, in woods away
  from any town; [[mooncross|Mooncross]] is the nearest city. It is the way she came when she fled.
- **Invisible** once you are through, and undetectable by Detect Magic; nothing on the Material side
  marks it. The far gate shows only to those who entered by hers.
- **Nearly one-way.** Only the witch can open it, or hold it open from this end. But a mage with enough
  power and skill **who knew roughly where to look could find the anchor and unpick it**. It is a small
  risk, but the DM is clear that it is a risk: **an unauthorised weakness** in the barrier.
- Caspian mapped what he could, but the road **compresses geography** and cannot be used to teleport
  back to it.

## DM notes / secrets
- **Forbidden.** Opening private channels to the Feywild is not authorised, for fear the uthrel comes
  through. Those who guard against the uthrel would be horrified to know it exists.
- **The party now knows of a hidden crossing near the war frontier**, and promised the witch to say
  nothing of Lae'ethorian. Nothing was asked about the road itself.
- **What are the black towers?** Caspian means to ask the witch by Sending.
- **The road is also her way out.** She told the party plainly that they could destroy her wards and
  trap her below.
