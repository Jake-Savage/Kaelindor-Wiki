## Overview
**Unvisited, and barely seen.** The Keep is named by [[the-provost|the Provost]] in S2.38 as what lies
beyond [[the-plain|the plain]], which in turn lies beyond [[dunton|Dunton]]'s palisade.

What is established:

- **It stands above what the Provost understands are the lower levels of this place**, and it
  **guards** them. She has **never seen them and does not wish to**.
- The villagers **know which direction it lies in**, and that direction has stayed consistent in their
  experience — but **most of them have never gone far enough to see it**.
- It is **not the bottom**. Asked whether the Keep is the lowest point, the Provost says no — it guards
  the lower levels, and she does not know how many more there are beneath it.
  [[the-high-priest|Greyface]] says there are **at least three or four levels below this one, and
  possibly many more he has never considered the existence of**.

## Why it matters
After hearing about it, [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]]'s conclusion for the party is simply: **"we've got
to get to this keep."** It is the first concrete destination the party has had since reaching
[[dunton|Dunton]], and the route to it crosses a dark, shifting plain full of large hostile creatures.

## What is there (S2.39)
- **Soldiers.** There are soldiers at the Keep. As far as the villagers of [[dunton|Dunton]] are
  concerned, **those soldiers are undead** — their judgement, not a confirmed fact.
- **Red-robed priests** come up from the lower levels past Dunton from time to time, which is part of
  what the palisade watches for. [[the-provost|The Provost]] says there are **"very, very many"** of
  them below.
- **It is more than a day's walk** across [[the-plain|the plain]], and visible from the palisade.
  The villagers' flat position is that **you cannot go there** — you would be set upon by the plain's
  creatures the whole way and never arrive.
- **A witch lives beyond it.** Dunton's townsfolk have told the party of a **witch beyond "the castle"**
  — the Keep. Who she is, and whether she would help, the party does not know.
- **Past it is a honeycomb of tunnels.** [[the-provost|The Provost]] has heard stories of **another
  intact area** beyond the Keep, and believes it might be possible to **pass under the plain rather
  than over it**. She knows the start of those paths and that **they lead up**.

## Its patrols (S2.40)
On the plain the party sees **a patrol of six**, unlit and stomping openly along its route.
[[the-provost|The Provost]] says such patrols are **soldiers from the Keep, or priests**. What she
knows of them, she has from [[the-high-priest|Greyface]]:

- They think of the villagers as **parasites inhabiting the body of God**.
- They tolerate [[dunton|Dunton]] only because **Greyface made an arrangement with them — convinced them
  he was one of them**.
- On the plain, they **will kill** villagers they find.
- They **never leave their patrol routes**, so the hunters find them easy to avoid.

## Why the party cares
Two reasons, both raised in S2.39:

- **[[fabian-perennius|Fabian]]'s** reasoning: the Keep is the hinge between this level and the ones
  below, so **if there is a way back up anywhere, it is likely to be there**.
- **[[rell-aetris|Rell]]'s**: it holds **people**, and that makes it a bargaining chip — his proposal
  is to offer [[the-high-priest|Greyface]] access to the Keep's inhabitants as **an alternative supply**
  in exchange for letting [[dunton|Dunton]] go. See [[evacuate-dunton]].

## DM notes
- **Nothing about the Keep itself is established** — not its builders, its age, its occupants, its
  condition, or what "guarding" the lower levels means in practice. Everything above is the Provost's
  secondhand account.
- [[the-high-priest|Greyface]] warns that when the Stonehunger **submerges again — days, a week
  perhaps** — things on the plain **may move around far more profoundly**. Whether the Keep's direction
  survives that is unknown.
