## Overview
Kethran's Fold (also heard "Cethan's Fold"/"Catherine's Fold"/"Keffin's Fold") is a village in
[[the-mirrowen|the Mirrowen]], reached from [[greywatch|Greywatch]] over **about two days** on foot
with a guide (the [[the-furnace-brood|Brood]]'s orders gave the distance as ~40 miles south). It sits
in a **fold** in the broken landscape — a steep, sharp valley wide enough to have supported building
and a little cultivation, the flattest point in country otherwise creased into gullies. About **ten
weeks ago** it was **destroyed**: a great slide of stone came down (or, per [[mags|Mags]]'s reading,
partly welled *up*) and buried the whole village. It is the destination the [[the-furnace-brood|Furnace
Brood]] assigned to its agent [[soren-dragan|Soren]], and the party reaches and enters it in S2.30
(see [[the-korrathian-conspiracy]]).

## The site (S2.30)
Seen from above, the collapse reads superficially as an avalanche: a teardrop of loose stone
spreading from a point high on the valley's north side, down and across the village, scooping a
little up the far slope. The village proper is completely buried; the valley on either side is
mostly clear. Weeds have barely begun to reclaim the stone.

- **Not a natural landslide.** [[mags|Mags]], an expert on rock, judges (high survival check,
  DM-confirmed) that this was **not natural** — at least some of the stone **welled up from beneath**
  rather than tumbling from above. The landslip appearance is a *consequence*, not the origin. What
  caused the upheaval is not established on site. (The guide [[cressan-weller|Cressan]] attributes it
  to the [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]]; [[nan-prentic|Nan]] calls that superstition — see the lore
  entry. Do not treat the folk-cause as confirmed.)
- **The site has been deliberately, systematically excavated.** At the rubble's edge there is **far
  less village detritus than there should be** — crushed building material, not just valuables, has
  been fairly systematically **removed**. Scratches left by nailed/spiked boots mark the rocks; the
  feet are **small (gnome-sized), not human-sized**, and there is heavy traffic (many people, not one).
  Most marks date to soon after the collapse; a few are fresher. Tracks converge and lead down from
  higher in the slide to a **deliberately opened gap** into the rubble near the old village centre.
- **The foreign nail.** Among the scratches the party recovers a small **silver-coloured nail that is
  in fact very high-quality steel** — too fine and valuable for the job, bearing no maker's mark, and
  of a quality that **could not be made in the Mirrowen**. Its like comes from large southern
  manufacturers — [[crownspire|Crownspire]], Orentis, Velbrance — not from here or from Mooncross. It
  has been crudely repurposed as a boot-grip, as if whoever used it did not know (or care about) its
  worth. Its presence is anomalous and unexplained.

## Underground (S2.30)
Following the tracks into the opened gap, the party descends a **created passage** — not tunnelled
but arranged, rocks shifted and balanced to leave a way through, sized comfortably for small folk
(Mags walks; the others stoop or crawl). A dropped ball-bearing (Rell) falls naturally through the
rubble — **not a portal**. The passage reaches, unexpectedly, a **grand decorative marble staircase**:
broad as a formal entranceway, finely shaped, of a style [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] associates with
grand buildings — its edges still buried in rubble, the walkable strip excavated through it. The party
is judged to be **still above the level where the village stood**, so the stair belongs to something
that was here beneath or within the site. There are **no lamp niches** anywhere — the excavators see
in the dark.

The stair leads down to a plain, barn- or storehouse-like **wooden door**, planked and metal-banded,
with a lock but **poorly sealed** (it has cracks and can simply swing open; Rell finds no trap, alarm,
or engaged locking mechanism). Behind it:

- a **faint, constant, pale-whitish glow** — not flickering, not bright enough to see by, noticed only
  in full darkness. [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] does not recognise it.
- from **far below** (not just beyond the door), the sounds of **voices** and of **digging** — stone
  being shifted and broken, the occasional tap of a hammer.

The session ends with the party at this door: Rell links the group telepathically, and Rell and
[[trevick|Trevick]] prepare to go through while the others hold back. **They have not yet passed
through.**

## The pillar chamber (S2.31)
Beyond the buried wooden door is a long hall (~40 ft by ~25 ft, ~15 ft high) that is the source of
the pale glow — an antechamber of the older complex, in a style unrelated to the marble stair:

- **The [[the-broken-map|broken map]].** The whole left wall is a faintly-glowing **mural-map**,
  smashed and reformed countless times and preserved by strong **abjuration** magic. It shows this
  region in a **far older configuration** — fewer mountains, a vaster Branweald, settlements where
  none stand now — its names in an **unknown script** resembling but not Draconic.
  [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] copies it.
- **The [[the-glass-smoke|glass smoke]].** Six pillars of dark glass line the hall (three a side).
  Each **shelters** a bound **undead spirit** — smoke moving within the glass — reading as necrotic,
  dual-natured (predatory hunger and lonely longing), able to emerge but tethered to its pillar. The
  gleaners call them "glass smoke" and say they have killed gleaners who came too close.
- **The gargoyle.** At the far end stands a ~10-ft **stone gargoyle**, wings wrapped around itself,
  repaired many times from mismatched stone, carrying an **enchantment aura** and showing evidence
  of past movement — the party takes it for a **golem/guardian**. Under its wings it clutches a
  **large key**, laid loose in its hand. It does **not** react to being examined or to the door
  opening.
- Fortress-like **heraldic tiled floor**; a blank dressed-stone opposite wall; and, in the far
  corner, the **heavy stone door** (below) — a frequently-used, complex but pickable lock, no magic
  or trap.

## Deeper (S2.31)
[[rell-aetris|Rell]] shrouds the gargoyle end in **magical Darkness**, [[theodore|Theodore]] perches
on the gargoyle to guard the key, and Rell picks the corner **stone door** (second attempt). It
opens onto a **~25–30 ft drop** and a spiral, library-style metal **staircase** down into an
inhabited complex:

- **The gleaners' "moor."** A **dwarvish** machine-room with mining machinery (water/air pumping,
  the sound familiar from the [[nerrin-mine]]) and climbable pipes, where the party finds two
  **[[the-gleaners|gleaners]]** ([[turley|Turley]] and [[glorta|Glorta]]) at rest. A further archway
  leads down to a **ramshackle** room of ~14–15 battered **wooden crates that stink of dead things** —
  which prove to be the resting place of **the sleepers**, the creatures the party must later creep
  past (below). The two gleaners seen wear **soft leather shoes** rather than the spiked boots that
  left the S2.30 tracks (which stopped at the marble stair) — but the party takes the gleaners to be
  those surface excavators, gleaning being exactly that scavenging (presumably other gleaners wore the
  boots).
- **The route deeper** (per Turley): past **the sleepers** — creatures resting **among the stinking
  crates** (above), asleep but liable to **wake at a scent** — down to the **bridges** spanning the
  **chasm**, a "river" that is in fact **endlessly churning stone**
  — where a "dozen or two" gleaners work and "forty, fifty" more are near the boss [[tuffin|Tuffin]];
  then on to the [[the-custodian|custodian]]'s **gate** onto **Grommer's Path**, the deadly deep.
- The party (via Theodore) glimpses the **web of bridges** over the churning-stone chasm and the
  many gleaners on bridges and shore. The session ends with the party debating how to get past ~50
  gleaners — and resolving to take time to devise a plan (see [[the-korrathian-conspiracy]]).

## DM notes
- **The complex is pre-existing; the [[the-gleaners|gleaners]] are squatters.** Turley: "There were
  many folks here before us, but we've made it ours." The marble stair, the dwarvish machine-room,
  the pillar chamber, the map and the gargoyle predate them; who built it is unknown.
- **Working identification:** the party takes the gleaners to be the S2.30 **surface excavators** —
  gleaning a kill's leavings is exactly their trade — though the two seen wore soft shoes rather than
  the spiked/foreign-steel boots that left the tracks (presumably other gleaners); the **foreign
  steel's origin** stays unexplained. Still genuinely open, and not to be assumed answered: whether
  any of this is the [[the-furnace-brood|Furnace Brood]]'s object, what the [[the-glass-smoke|glass
  smoke]] are, what the [[the-broken-map|map]] records, and who built the complex before the gleaners
  squatted it. The [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]]'s nature is corroborated as real but not explained.


## What is known
From the instructions [[jorrith-potter|Jorrith]] passes to the disguised [[rell-aetris|Rell]]
(S2.29):

- There are **stories of caves, passages, or holes** by which one can get underground into the
  buried village.
- There are **stories of people heard singing or crying** somewhere underneath. Whether anyone
  truly heard this is unestablished.
- The Brood want their agent to go **as deep as is safe**, find anything **strange that "does not
  belong"** to such a village (treasure, or anything anomalous), and bring it back to Jorrith to
  report — so the Brood can judge whether to send more people.
- It lies in unstable, dangerous country; a local **guide** is needed to reach it safely.
