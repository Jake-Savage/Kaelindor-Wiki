## Overview
First heard in S2.36 as **a bright, unseen voice** at the dead end below [[the-ancient-forge|the ancient
forge]], welcoming the party and pressing someone *else* to work the stone open. In S2.37 he walks up
behind [[mags|Mags]] at the lip of the arena pit — squelching footsteps, not invisible — and the two
are confirmed to be the same being: the intonation matches, and asked outright whether he was the one
who let them through, he **inclines his head and says yes**.

> *"You looked to be in something of a difficult situation where you were, and I thought this might be
> an interesting opportunity. And so it proved."*

## Appearance and manner
- **Heavy robes, red and trimmed with gold**, with an elaborate hood that conceals most of his face. He
  carries a **staff**, held without aggression.
- Enough flesh shows to read as **undead**. Asked to lower the hood (he does, with a flicker of
  impatience at the sidetracking), he is **clearly undead but relatively fresh** — not a skeleton, all
  his flesh present, a deathly colour.
- His face is **definitely human, but it has been altered to look more elvish**. Nobody present knows
  of a people who do that.
- **"Greyface"** is what people in these parts call him. He finds it *"a little rude"* — *"one does
  what one can with what one has, but it keeps me humble."* He gives no other name.
- Dry, patient, faintly amused, and unbothered by being argued with. He becomes clipped when a question
  he has asked twice goes unanswered.

## The experiment
He admits the minotaur was his.

- The axe, he says, **"was used in this little experiment,"** and he demands it back before the
  conversation **"might get a little less amenable."**
- Asked why he set the creature on them: *"certain qualities of the circumstance, of this artifact, of
  your abilities, interested me. Visitors of any caliber are rare down here."* Pressed by
  [[rell-aetris|Rell]], who was nearly killed: *"It wouldn't have been my preferred outcome if it ended
  any one of you, but of course the possibility was there. I thought you actually handled it rather
  cleverly."* Rell's parting advice — not to disembowel the next lot if he wants them to last — earns a
  long flat look nobody can read.
- He examines the recovered axe with **curiosity rather than triumph**: [[mags|Mags]]'s read is that it
  is a **relatively unfamiliar object to him in its physical immediacy**, interesting but not something
  he treats as precious. He confirms it is a **dwarven axe of some considerable antiquity**.

## What he says about "God"
He says **"God"** with audible air quotes and makes no secret of it (Fabian's insight). What he tells
the party — his own account, not independently confirmed:

- The [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] is **"a substance… fascinating but not particularly pleasant."**
- It is **not indifferent**. Told it seemed not to care for the dwarven architecture, he corrects them:
  *"it does, in its way… very much. But what it doesn't like are things that it cannot reshape and make
  its own."*
- Of [[the-ancient-forge|the forge complex]]: **"that area was resistant for quite some time. It had a
  certain sort of energy which gives protection"** — which bears directly on why the shrine chamber
  stood while the rooms around it came down.
- **"There are quite a number of places within its substance which haven't yet really been absorbed and
  may never be."** And then, pointedly: *"unless, of course, some intrepid adventurers decide to
  disrupt things — then things can change."* He credits the party with having done exactly that. His
  attribution, not an established cause.
- **[[the-buried-arena|The arena]] "is very much to God's liking. He has picked and chosen the elements
  that please him, and this is the result. I dare say it will change, but not immediately."**
- **"When God was more expansive in its habits, there were several dwarven sites which came under its
  attention."** He has seen some deeper, but says he has not explored much beyond this point.
- On the dwarven traps: *"the dwarves are rather thorough in their idea of security — not, in the end,
  that it did them any good whatsoever."*

He has also **been up into the forge complex**: *"Yes, I've been there. And I think there won't be very
much to find the next time I go that way. It's a good thing I recovered what I did."*

## The elemental
He opens the way out by addressing **the empty air at an outline of an archway**: *"Well, you heard me,
open the way quickly now."* Sounds come from the stone and nothing moves. He **raises the staff in a
reasonably threatening way** — at the wall, not at the party — the shaping immediately quickens, and he
lowers it: *"That's better. Let's not get any ideas."* He later calls the thing **"my captive"**:
*"should my captive give you any trouble, I would make it regret it."* See
[[the-arena-elemental]].

## Parting instructions
He sends the party down to **[[dunton|Dunton]]** — *"it's not far"* — and does not come.

- *"My presence in the town causes something of a stir. I don't wish to alarm or distress people
  unnecessarily. I shall return to the Ossuary"* — which he describes as **another region of at least
  relative stability**.
- They are to say that **in his capacity as High Priest he has discerned that God would prefer that
  their sacrifice wait for a more appropriate time**. *"They're not always welcoming to outsiders, but
  you can tell them I've sent you… I don't think they will disbelieve. And besides, even tired as you
  are, I doubt any of them would really be a match for you. They're a rather sorry bunch."*
- **[[the-provost|The Provost]] can direct them to the Ossuary** if they want him again, though he
  doubts she would be keen to accompany them.

## DM notes / secrets
- **What he is willing to do.** [[mags|Mags]]'s Insight (21): **no aggressive intent and no interest in
  murdering them imminently** — but the distinct impression that **if he thought it would be
  interesting or profitable to do so, he would 100% do it**. Separately, he is **sincere** about the
  settlement and the rest; he believes what he is telling them.
- **He is not himself — he is [[undead-puppetry|a puppet]].** From [[codex-mortis|Kiona's infernal
  notes]] [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] recalls a magic by which a practitioner makes an **undead puppet**
  and channels their awareness and part of their power through it, to act in the world while staying
  safe and hidden, and concludes this figure is one. **The fiction has not confirmed it** — it is
  Caspian's reading, and the party has never tested it — but it is the **working truth** of this entry,
  and everything the figure does should be read as something done at a remove by whoever is holding
  him.
  **He said nothing at the time**, worried about being asked how he knew, and shared the content the
  next morning without naming the book.
- **Who the puppeteer is, and where they are, is entirely unknown** — and is now the live question. The
  range on this magic is limited unless relay points have been prepared, so the operator is either
  close or has made arrangements. Note also that he is a **fresh** undead in an ancient complex, and
  that everything the party found interesting about him — the curiosity, the experiments, the appetite
  for visitors of any caliber — belongs to **them**, not to the corpse.
- **He gives no name of his own** — only the nickname and the office. Whoever he was before he was
  killed is unknown, as is who killed him.
- **Why he improved Dunton's lot.** [[varen|Varen]] says that before this High Priest came, a healer in
  these parts *"would have changed everything"*, and that they now lack little in that regard. His
  motive for that is unestablished, as is what "sacrifice" means in Dunton.
