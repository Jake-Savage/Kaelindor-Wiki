## Who he was
**Grommar** (spelling confirmed by the DM; heard variously at the table as "Gromar"/"Grommer") is a
**semi-legendary, semi-historical dwarven figure** — a **smith of very great skill**, known to
[[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] from stories (S2.32, history check with advantage).

In his later years he set himself a single project: to **forge a weapon that could kill death** — a
thing that would kill Death itself when it came for him. He **disappeared before he could do so**.

Opinion in the stories divides on what that project was:

- **Madness.** Some hold that he simply went mad in old age, chasing an impossible thing.
- **Occult insight.** Others hold that, as very skilled dwarven smiths often are, he was also a mage
  of sorts — at least in **runecraft** and related arts — and that he had **understood something
  about the nature of death** that others had not.

Either way, the legend agrees on one detail: in those late years he became **extremely paranoid**,
and laid **elaborate and dastardly traps** for anyone who tried to approach him.

## Grommar's Path
The deep beneath [[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]], beyond the [[the-custodian|custodian]]'s gate, is
called **[[grommars-path|Grommar's Path]]**. The [[the-gleaners|gleaners]] describe it as deadly and
not their place — where "the bigger folk" and the priests closer to "God" dwell (S2.31) — and the gate
itself calls sending anyone down it a **waste**, reckoning they will "fall and fail" (S2.33). The party
entered it at the end of S2.33.

Its entrance passage is a **hewn ceremonial way** of the kind that fronts the more sacred and secretive
dwarven temples of **[[calvyr|Calvyr]]** (Mags, Caspian and Rell, S2.33). In S2.34 that passage opens
into a genuine **Calvyr temple corridor**, devotional reliefs of the
[[the-seven-saints-of-calvyr|seven saints]] and all, which has been **comprehensively trapped after the
fact** — the trap-ports are cut into and around the carvings, so the trapper came after the builder.

**Two things found in that corridor touch the legend directly:**

- **The traps themselves.** The legend says Grommar became paranoid and laid elaborate, dastardly traps;
  the corridor is elaborately and dastardly trapped, in a devotional space where (the DM noted) putting
  a trap at the saint of hospitality would be considered poor form. Asked about that, the DM observed
  only that on the stories the party knows Grommar was **insane by the end of his life**, and wondered
  how far he felt such constraints applied to him.
- **The inscription.** Cut into the floor beside the brewing saint's relief, later than the rest of the
  work and with evident care: **[[khazar-vuk-varag|"Khazar Vuk Varag — I defy you"]]** — a proper name meaning
  roughly *oldest enemy* or *ancient hatred*. Read in the context of Grommar, whose adversary was
  death, the party would **almost certainly take it for death** (DM) — though the phrase belongs to no
  dwarvish or Calvyran idiom for death, which is what keeps [[mags|Mags]] arguing.

## Open questions
- **Why is the deep named for him?** Whether Grommar went down there, ended there, worked there, or
  merely gave his name to a road is **not established**.
- **Does his unfinished weapon exist**, and is it down that path? Unknown — nothing in the fiction
  says so; this is only the obvious question raised by the naming.
- **Are the traps of the legend the traps of the path?** The gleaners call the deep deadly; the legend
  calls Grommar trap-happy; S2.34 shows the temple corridor trapped after it was built. Three pieces
  that fit, joined so far only by the party.
- **Did he come through alive?** [[rell-aetris|Rell]]'s reading of the inscription is that Grommar cut
  it having survived the trap-run — his declaration that he had defied death. [[mags|Mags]]'s objection
  stands: why patiently carve a private defiance where others will read it?
- **Was he ever actually here?** [[rell-aetris|Rell]]'s argument that he was: the
  [[the-gleaners|gleaners]] know Grommar's name while knowing nothing of any other structure inside the
  [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]], so the name reached them from somewhere.
- **Why does a dwarven sacred way to [[calvyr|Calvyr]] front the path?** Grommar was a dwarf and, in one
  telling, a runecrafter; Calvyr is the god of thought and craft. Suggestive, and so far only that.
