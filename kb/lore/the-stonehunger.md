## What it is
The **Stonehunger** is a name used by some folk of [[the-mirrowen|the Mirrowen]] for the events in
which a village, a house, or a stretch of road is suddenly **swallowed underground** — the rock
brought down, or (as it is sometimes told) welling *up* from below, burying the place and its people.
The party first hears the name from the guide [[cressan-weller|Cressan Weller]] (S2.30), describing
what took [[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]].

Whether the Stonehunger is a real thing — a creature, a force, or the land itself — or merely a
folk-name for natural collapse is **openly disputed at the table of the two guides**, and is
recorded here as **unconfirmed**.

## What's known (as told)
Reported by [[cressan-weller|Cressan]] and, he says, common town stories — **attributed, not
confirmed**:

- It "lives underground" and comes to take a place from below; there is "no space for it" above.
- It takes **everything** — not part of a settlement like an ordinary landslip, but the whole of it,
  and mostly everyone with it.
- It recurs — perhaps **once a decade**, sometimes more, sometimes less — and after it feeds, the
  land is left "still hungry" and unsafe for a good while.
- People pulled out from beneath the stone "aren't right" afterward; some, so the stories go, went
  **back** under the rubble (one tale: a man who had been dug out took his children back under).
- **Singing, knocking, and voices** are said to be heard underground days or weeks after a place is
  taken. (This overlaps the "singing/crying below" rumour attached to Kethran's Fold since S2.29 —
  the two are kept as the same body of folklore, and both remain unverified.)

## The sceptic's account
[[nan-prentic|Nan Prentic]] rejects the Stonehunger outright. In her telling, Kethran's Fold was
simply **a bad landslip** — these parts are prone to them because "there's too much magic here" —
and the frightening tales are nonsense "made up to frighten people." Her prescription is only
watchfulness and care, not fear of a monster.

## Open questions
- Is the Stonehunger a real agent, or a folk-name for magically-driven collapse? Unresolved.
- **Countervailing evidence (S2.30):** at Kethran's Fold, [[mags|Mags]] (an expert on rock)
  determines the collapse was **not a natural landslide** — at least some stone welled up from
  underneath rather than tumbling from above — which is consistent with *something* acting from
  below, but does **not** confirm the folkloric Stonehunger. Separately, the party finds the site has
  been **deliberately excavated** by living hands (see [[kethrans-fold]]); this is a distinct matter
  and must not be conflated with the folklore. Keep the three threads — the folk-name, the unnatural
  origin of the collapse, and the present-day diggers — separate until the fiction links them.
