## What they are
In **dwarvish [[calvyr|Calvyr]] worship** there are **seven classical saints** (DM, S2.34 — confirmed).
They are all **either real people or supposedly real people**, each an **exemplar of a particular
craft**. All seven are **ancient** by the standards of the present era, which is why [[mags|Mags]]'s
idea of dating a temple by which of them appear in it is only **potentially** workable — the DM allowed
it as a method without the party ever carrying it out.

Their depictions are **bas-reliefs**, roughly eight to ten feet wide and six feet high, stylised, each
saint shown in a workshop surrounded by the typical symbols and tools of their craft. They are **quite
reverent portraits, definitely intended as devotional works** (DM). Most are sparingly decorated, with
small accents picked out in gold.

## The saints identified so far
The corridor at the head of [[grommars-path|Grommar's Path]] carries **all seven** — one relief per
saint — alternating from side to side about every ten to fifteen feet (S2.34). The party stopped to
identify **five** of them:

- **Orvan Smokeglass**, the **alchemist** — shown holding a flask with vapour rising from it, another
  flask on a table before him. Known familiarly as "Old Smokeglass." Identified by [[mags|Mags]].
- **Keldra the Golden-Faced**, saint of **fine precision work** — goldsmithing, silversmithing — shown
  masked, with the small hammer and tongs of delicate work. In his youth he crafted a **golden mask**
  and wore it until his old age. Whether it was a true likeness of his young face, a wish-fulfilment,
  or a covering for injuries taken in the pursuit of his craft is **not settled even in the tradition**.
  Identified by [[caspian-talon|Caspian]].
- **Vero**, saint of **jewels, lenses and glassware** — of cutting, faceting, and the precision grinding
  that makes a lens. Vero's relief is the one exception to the plain decoration: the dwarf's **eyes are
  set with green gems**. [[mags|Mags]] is relatively familiar with this saint through the trade in
  diamonds of different sorts for different divine spells.
- **Sargrim Hexward**, the **runecrafter** — equally proficient with **curses (hexes)** and with
  **wards against curses**. Conventionally depicted with **five runes**: two for his hex-setting, two
  for his ward-setting, and one for **keeping the two forces in balance**. Which particular runes vary
  from depiction to depiction. A carver may or may not trouble to make the depicted runes genuinely
  **potent** — on this relief the carver **did**.
- **The saint of brewing** — the one of the seven **most celebrated outside dwarven culture**, shown
  with vats and tankards, and generally taken as a symbol of **hospitality and respite**. His name was
  not clearly caught at the table and is **not recorded here** rather than guessed at.

The remaining **two reliefs were passed without being identified**, so two of the seven are still
unnamed here.

## What's known
- **Placing a trap at the brewing saint would be considered poor form**, given what he stands for
  (DM) — which is the party's ground for reading the corridor's trapping as the work of someone not
  bound by ordinary Calvyran propriety. On the stories the party knows, [[grommar|Grommar]] was
  **insane by the end of his life**, and the DM's only comment was to wonder how far he felt such
  constraints applied to him.

## Open questions
- **Who are the remaining two saints?** Both are carved in the corridor; the party walked past them
  without stopping to read them.
- **Does the set of saints present in a temple date it?** The method was allowed as workable and never
  used — and this temple carries all seven, so it would date it only as later than the last of them.
- **Whose temple is this?** The reliefs are standard Calvyran devotional work. Who built the corridor at
  the head of [[grommars-path|Grommar's Path]], and why it sits inside the
  [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]], is open.
- **Who trapped it, and when?** The traps are cut into and around the reliefs — later additions to a
  devotional space, not part of its design.
