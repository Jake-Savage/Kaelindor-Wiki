## Recap
The session picks up where S2.33 ended, a little way inside [[grommars-path|Grommar's Path]], with the
gate sealed behind the party. [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] takes a **hooded lantern** rather than casting
Light, to avoid spoiling the others' darkvision, and [[mags|Mags]] and [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] walk
ahead to the first carving on the wall.

**The corridor is a temple.** The way beyond the gate is a cleanly-dressed stone corridor on a gentle
downward slope, and its walls carry **devotional bas-reliefs**, alternating side to side every ten to
fifteen feet. Mags identifies the first (religion 19): **Orvan Smokeglass**, the alchemist, one of the
**seven classical saints** of dwarvish [[calvyr|Calvyr]] worship — real or supposedly real people, each
an exemplar of a craft, all of them ancient (see [[the-seven-saints-of-calvyr]]). The carvings are
reverent work, plainly devotional, and — a detail the DM confirms when Fabian asks — they are in a
**different style from the engravings at the complex's entrance above**. Mags floats dating the temple by which
saints appear in it; the DM allows that it could be done, and the party never does it.

**The traps.** [[rell-aetris|Rell]] interrupts the art history to point out that this is Grommar's Path
and that they should be checking for traps, and is immediately vindicated. Beside Smokeglass's relief
he finds a row of small holes with wires inside — a needle or gas trap that has been **triggered
recently**, almost certainly by Mags and Caspian walking up to the carving, and whose mechanism no
longer works. What set it off is never determined.

From there the corridor is a sequence:

- **A ceiling hammer.** Rell spots a patch of floor **broken and mended more than once**. The party
  edges around it, then springs it from a safe distance by leaning on Mags's staff — [[trevick|Trevick]]
  and Fabian adding their weight. A huge war-hammer-shaped weight drops on a chain, **destroys the
  staff**, cracks the floor and stays down. Rell climbs the chain to look: a small space, a gear the
  chain winds around, little else. Mags had been hoping for a maintenance passage running past the
  traps, on the reasoning that Calvyrans believe in repairs and would have had a protocol for them.
- **A spike drop.** A narrow ceiling opening with a line of impact points in the floor beneath it. The
  party simply **jumps the line**, Rell marking the safe standing spot with a chalk **X** and calling
  back instructions.
- **Portcullises and an axe.** At the relief of **Vero** — saint of jewels, lenses and glassware, and
  the one carving decorated beyond plain accents, its dwarf's **eyes set with green gems** — Rell asks
  whether anyone objects to prising the gems out. Mags objects on principle to despoiling temples; Rell
  answers that this temple has been eaten and is not a temple any more. He takes them (sleight of hand
  16, scratching them slightly): a handful of **emeralds** and one **darker, purplish stone** nobody can
  identify. Two **portcullises** crash down twenty feet apart, sealing the party between them, and a
  large slender **axe-blade** begins sweeping the corridor from a wall slit every thirty to forty
  seconds. Trevick, checking the far side, notices small **rectangular gaps opening along the floor** as
  the cycle runs, still unexplained. Mags reads the swings as building toward something and offers to
  arrest the axe with Thorn Whip; Rell instead builds a **pulley** — two pitons, a rope run from the far
  portcullis and drawn taut across the axe's arc — so that the axe's own momentum **hauls the grate
  up**. It works, the party ducks through (Fabian's natural 20 scooping up Mags), and the DM confirms
  afterwards that on the **seventh swing** the trap would have escalated. Rell stopped it on the sixth.
- **Warded stones.** At the relief of **Sargrim Hexward**, the runecrafter — depicted conventionally
  with five runes, two for hexes, two for wards and one holding the two in balance, and carved here by
  someone who troubled to make them **genuinely potent** — Rell finds that some of the floor and wall
  stones carry very faint marks, not carved but like **shadows on the stone**, which read as the residue
  of magic cast on them. He picks a path between them and **chalks it out** rather than talking the
  party through it; everyone follows. Caspian points out the runes would be worth money and is talked
  into leaving them for the way out.

**The inscription.** Past the relief of the **brewing saint** — the one of the seven most celebrated
outside dwarven culture, a figure of hospitality and respite, and the one relief at which the party
finds no trap — Rell spots a line of **Dwarvish cut into the floor**. It does not match the coherence of
the rest of the work and was **definitely carved later**, not scratched in haste but cut with care and
time. He reads it aloud:

---

**Khazar Vuk Varag — I defy you.**

---

**Khazar Vuk Varag** is presented by every linguistic marker as a **proper name**, meaning something like
*ancient hatred*, *the oldest of hatreds*, or *oldest enemy*. The DM establishes (history 15+) that
anyone meeting this line in the context of [[grommar|Grommar]] — whose classic adversary was death —
would **almost certainly read it as death**, while being clear that the phrase belongs to **no
traditional dwarvish idiom for death, and no Calvyran one**. Mags's religion check (19) turns up no
tradition carrying the name.

The party argues it out without resolving it. Rell's reading is that this is Grommar himself, having
come through the trap-run alive, announcing that he has **defied death** — and he notes that the
[[the-gleaners|gleaners]] know Grommar's name while knowing nothing about any of the other structures
inside the [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]], which he takes to imply Grommar was really here. Mags keeps
pressing two objections: that *"oldest adversary"* is not the same claim as *"death,"* and that nobody
patiently carves a private defiance unless they **want others to draw an inference from it**. Asked
whether trapping the saint of hospitality would be improper, the DM allows that it would traditionally
be considered poor form — and observes only that on the stories the party knows, Grommar was **insane by
the end of his life**. See [[khazar-vuk-varag]].

**The air.** Throughout, the stink grows worse as the corridor descends. Mags's survival check finds
nothing acutely poisonous — unpleasant rather than dangerous, though not air to breathe indefinitely.
Rell studies a flame and finds a **faint bluish tinge at its edges**, from which he infers a natural
build-up of **methane**, and asks Fabian to put out his lantern flame on the strength of it. Further
down, the party begins to hear water — not running water, but an
**irregular slap**, every now and again.

**The pool.** The corridor turns a right angle onto steps descending about fifty feet in an irregular,
almost square-spiral, then opening into a natural chamber holding **[[the-foul-pool|a pit of water some
seventy feet across]]**. A damaged ledge runs the whole way round it, thirty feet above a warm, filthy
surface with oily patches, floating matter and rising gas — the source of the slapping sound. About a
third of the way round to the left, a **fifteen-foot strip of carved wall** runs from the water up into
the ceiling and beyond, like a **chimney**, in very much the style of the corridor's reliefs but **not
by the same hand**, and marred by **many narrow openings added after the carving was done**. A built
**canopy** of four pillars and a sloping roof shelters it — the work of whoever made the chamber, the DM
clarifies explicitly, with the current occupants moved in under it. It is the **only apparent way on**.

[[caspian-talon|Caspian]] scouts it all with [[theodore|Theo]], then sends him under the canopy, where
something comes at him **almost at once**. He pulls the familiar out of existence in time. What came was
**arachnid**, moving with the **clatter of metal**: spiders **either fully construct or heavily modified
by artifice** (DM), dog-sized, very quick, immediately hostile, descending on what looks like a **steel
cable** rather than scuttling, with **no webs anywhere** — which Rell reads as confirmation they are not
biological. The ceiling under the canopy is lumpy with them.

The party then spends a long time on the stairs rather than on the ledge. Fabian's **unseen servant**
makes noise two-thirds of the way round, waves and throws a pebble (no reaction), and then drops
rations into the water along an arc. Trevick spots a shift in the surface that the thrown stone cannot
account for, and with the rations it becomes clear that **something in the water follows the drops** —
concentrating near them, spread over a wide area, never seen. The party cannot agree where it is; the DM
rules that this is because the movement is genuinely **confusing**, not because anyone is being
magically compromised. Mags raises the idea of authenticating themselves to the spiders the way Calvyran
automata would expect — and, asked directly, the table confirms this is the **player's** reasoning
rather than anything Mags knows, leaving the party without an in-character way to hail them.

Rell finally proposes a test: his [[amulet-of-proof-against-detection|amulet of proof against
detection]] may hide him if the spiders sense by divination magic. Fabian's Arcana check can only say
that the theory is intuitively sound and that nobody knows how these things are built. Rell steps off
the stairs onto the ledge — and **the rest of the staircase snaps flat into a steep slope**, pitching
everyone else thirty feet into the pool. Rell keeps his footing, grabs for Mags as she goes past, and
fails to hold her without being dragged in himself. The session ends with the party in the water, Rell
alone above it, and the spiders between them and the only exit.

## Key facts learned
- **Grommar's Path opens into a [[calvyr|Calvyr]] temple.** Not merely a passage in the Calvyr style
  (S2.33) but a corridor of **devotional reliefs of the [[the-seven-saints-of-calvyr|seven classical
  saints]]**. Whose temple it is, who built it, and why it lies inside the
  [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] are the questions it raises.
- **Dwarvish Calvyr worship has seven classical saints**, real or supposedly real exemplars of crafts,
  all ancient (DM, confirmed). The corridor carries **all seven, one relief each**. The party identified
  five: **Orvan Smokeglass** (alchemy), **Keldra the Golden-Faced** (fine precision metalwork), **Vero**
  (jewels, lenses, glassware), **Sargrim Hexward** (runecraft, hexes and wards alike), and an unnamed
  **saint of brewing** — the one most celebrated outside dwarven culture, a figure of hospitality and
  respite. The other two reliefs were passed without being read.
- **The reliefs are in a different style from the engravings at the complex's entrance above** (DM,
  confirmed) — two separate builds, whatever their relation.
- **The temple was trapped after it was built.** The trap-ports are cut **into and around** the
  carvings, so the trapper and the builder are different hands. **Who laid the traps is unknown.** The
  party assumes [[grommar|Grommar]]; the DM's only comment was that a man insane by the end of his life
  may not have felt bound by the propriety that makes trapping the saint of hospitality poor form.
- **[[khazar-vuk-varag|"Khazar Vuk Varag — I defy you"]]**, cut carefully into the floor **later** than the rest
  of the corridor. The name means roughly *oldest enemy* or *ancient hatred*. In Grommar's context the
  party would almost certainly read it as **death** — though the phrase belongs to no dwarvish or
  Calvyran idiom for death, and Mags's religion check turns up no tradition carrying the name.
- **The axe escalates on the seventh swing** (DM, after the fact). Rell stopped it on the sixth.
- **Dwarven religious metalwork is treated against corrosion** as a matter of course, so uncorroded
  metal in such a place is ordinary rather than a sign of enchantment (DM).
- **The air is foul but not acutely poisonous** (survival, confirmed twice) — though not air to breathe
  indefinitely. The **faint blue edge** on a flame is what Rell read as **methane**; his own inference.
- **[[the-foul-pool|The pool]]:** a natural chamber, ~70 feet across, warm water giving off heat, filthy
  and gassy, ringed by a damaged ledge 30 feet up. Climbing out is difficult except at the carving.
- **The carved chimney is the only apparent exit**, its carvings in the corridor's style but by a
  different hand, and pierced by many openings **added afterwards**. Its sheltering canopy is the work
  of whoever made the chamber — the spiders moved into it (DM, explicit).
- **The spiders are constructs** — or things very significantly modified by artifice (DM). Dog-sized,
  metallic, webless, very fast, immediately hostile, and they lower themselves on **steel cable**. Who
  built them is open.
- **Something unidentified is in the water, and it follows objects dropped into it.** Never seen. The
  party could not agree where it was; the DM ruled the movement simply hard to read, rather than any
  effect working on them.
- **The staircase is itself a trap.** It snapped flat without warning as Rell stepped off it. What
  tripped it is open.

## DM notes
- The DM intends a **battle map** for the pool chamber next session, and was frank afterwards about
  having waited a long time for someone to step off the stairs.
- **Names.** The inscription is **Khazar Vuk Varag**, three words (DM-confirmed after the session; the
  recording garbled it into one). *Keldra the Golden-Faced* and *Orvan Smokeglass* came through the
  recording inconsistently and remain unconfirmed. The **brewing saint's name was not recoverable** and
  is left unrecorded rather than guessed.
- **Seven is not a significant number in Calvyr worship** (DM-confirmed after the session).
  [[mags|Mags]]'s remark to that effect during the axe trap was a guess, and its agreement with the
  trap's seventh-swing escalation was coincidence.
- **The count of reliefs.** There are as many reliefs as there are saints — **seven**. The table may
  have handwaved one of them mechanically, so a strict beat-by-beat reading of the session could come
  up one short; seven is correct (DM, after the session).
- Mags's "Calvyran automata with authentication protocols" was explicitly disclaimed at the table as the
  player's idea rather than the character's knowledge, and is recorded that way in [[the-foul-pool]] and
  [[constructs]].
