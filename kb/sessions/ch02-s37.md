## Recap

**Finishing the minotaur.** The session opens where S2.36 ended: the creature turned and cowering in
the pit, the party sealed into [[the-buried-arena|the arena]] by the invisible wall, and about a minute
of the turning left. They spend it getting into position rather than attacking.

[[rell-aetris|Rell]] picks his way across the mud on the dry ground he already knows and looks down the
pit: **fifteen to twenty feet deep**, with enough stone protruding to climb easily, and a space opening
off the northern side which is **a den rather than a corridor** — it cannot go far, since the minotaur
would have fled down it if it did. **There is no way out this way.** He shouts that back, then searches
the far wall of the arena — twice, a quick look and then a full study — for buttons, switches, levers,
runes, glyphs or sigils, touching nothing. **Nothing shows on a visual inspection.** His conclusion
sets the party's course: *"I see no mechanism or way forward here. I think perhaps killing the beast is
our best route. I would assume the voice we heard is looking for some kind of entertainment."*

[[caspian-talon|Caspian]], who has held **Haste** since before the last session ended, re-targets it
onto [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]]. The party rings the pit and holds actions.

**The fight.** Caspian opens with **Shatter** into the pit, which hurts the creature and knocks a good
many stone slabs off the inside walls — surface damage, not structural, but enough to make the climb
awkward afterwards — and everyone else releases on it.

What wins the fight is [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] at the lip with **Thunder Wave**, held each round
for the moment the minotaur starts climbing. It comes up with real aggression and he blasts it back
down; the second time he waits until it is **right at the top** so that it takes the fall as well, at
the cost of leaving himself wide open. A third attempt half-lands and does not push it back.

[[mags|Mags]] holds **Thorn Whip** to try to pull it off the wall, lands a late **Toll the Dead**, and
spits in its face to pull it off Fabian — which works: it turns and gores at her instead.

It reaches the top once and **claws [[rell-aetris|Rell]] from near-full health to unconscious in a
single round**. Mags upcasts **Healing Word**; Rell stands, takes the flanking position beside her, and
**lands the blow that drops it**. It falls backwards with a crash into the pit.

**What was keeping it alive.** Mags had already caught the pattern with a medicine check: it had
**regenerated while it sat in its den** between bouts, and was **not regenerating** while it was out in
the open. With it down, [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] watches the pit closely and sees the battering
starting to fade again, so Mags holds it under **Chill Touch** — which stops it regaining hit points —
and stands over it while the others climb down.

The den holds **old gnawed bones** and debris, and nothing else. Caspian burns his **last spell** on
**Identify**, aimed at the axe in the creature's chest:

- It **defies ready identification**.
- It is **strongly magical**.
- He is **confident it is the cause of the minotaur's regenerating**.

Caspian and Fabian each try to wrench it free and each fail. [[rell-aetris|Rell]] **cuts it out**
instead, working around tough undead sinew. Almost as soon as it comes loose the body runs through the
stages of decomposition **in about twenty seconds** and collapses to almost nothing, leaving a
**pristine, shining** large axe in his hands.

**The High Priest.** [[mags|Mags]], alone at the lip, hears **squelching footsteps** behind her. She
fakes a turn and throws up dust with **Prestidigitation** to catch anything invisible — and finds
something that is not invisible at all: a humanoid figure in **heavy red robes trimmed with gold**,
with an elaborate hood and a staff, enough flesh showing to read as **undead**.

She stalls, loudly, to bring the others up. It gives her a name of sorts: **people in these parts call
him "Greyface"**, which he finds *"a little rude"* — *"one does what one can with what one has, but
it keeps me humble."* Talked into lowering his hood (she claims to be half deaf and in need of
lip-reading), he proves **relatively fresh undead** — not a skeleton, all his flesh present, a deathly
colour — with a face that is **definitely human but has been altered to look more elvish**. Nobody
recognises the practice.

Asked outright by [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] whether he was the one who let them through the door, he
**inclines his head and says yes**: *"You looked to be in something of a difficult situation where you
were, and I thought this might be an interesting opportunity. And so it proved."* **He is the voice
from S2.36.**

**The axe, returned.** He wants it back — *"the axe, which was used in this little experiment"* — before
the conversation *"might get a little less amenable."* Fabian tries to leave it in the pit on the
grounds that it is too heavy to lift out, and is not believed. The figure climbs down without using the
rope, holds out a hand, and **[[rell-aetris|Rell]] gives it up rather than resist**, arguing the point
as he does: the creature nearly disembowelled him, and only his companions kept him alive.

> *"It wouldn't have been my preferred outcome if it ended any one of you, but of course the
> possibility was there. I thought you actually handled it rather cleverly."*

He confirms it is **a dwarven axe of some considerable antiquity**, says it is an object he is studying
and intends to go on studying, and turns it over in his hands with what [[mags|Mags]] reads as
**curiosity rather than triumph** — an unfamiliar thing, interesting, but not treated as precious.

**What he tells them.** He says **"God"** with audible air quotes and makes no secret of it:

- The [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] is **"a substance… fascinating but not particularly pleasant."**
- It is **not indifferent**: *"it does care, in its way… very much. But what it doesn't like are things
  that it cannot reshape and make its own."*
- Of [[the-ancient-forge|the forge]]: *"that area was resistant for quite some time. It had a certain
  sort of energy which gives protection."* **There are many such places inside it** that have not been
  absorbed and may never be — *"unless, of course, some intrepid adventurers decide to disrupt
  things."* He credits the party with having done exactly that.
- Of [[the-buried-arena|the arena]]: *"This place is very much to God's liking. He has picked and
  chosen the elements that please him, and this is the result. I dare say it will change, but not
  immediately."*
- **"When God was more expansive in its habits, there were several dwarven sites which came under its
  attention."** He has seen some deeper down and says he has not explored much past this point. On the
  trapped temple way: *"the dwarves are rather thorough in their idea of security — not, in the end,
  that it did them any good whatsoever."*
- **He has been up into the forge:** *"Yes, I've been there. And I think there won't be very much to
  find the next time I go that way. It's a good thing I recovered what I did."*

**The way out.** He turns to **empty air at the outline of an archway** and orders it open. Sounds come
from the stone and nothing moves; he **raises the staff threateningly** at the wall and the shaping
immediately quickens. *"That's better. Let's not get any ideas."* He later calls the thing **"my
captive."** The archway opens in the same form as the one that let them in.

He sends them to **[[dunton|Dunton]]** and does not come — *"my presence in the town causes something
of a stir"* — saying he will return to **[[the-ossuary|the Ossuary]]**, another region of at least
relative stability, to which **[[the-provost|the Provost]]** can direct them. They are to say that **in
his capacity as High Priest he has discerned that God would prefer their sacrifice wait for a more
appropriate time.** [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] finally buys their exit by **offering Caspian up** as a
scholar of axes and dwarven things, which the figure accepts **completely sincerely**.

**Afterwards.** Out of earshot, [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] half-recognises what they have just met and
says nothing. [[mags|Mags]], thinking about the stone, establishes that the thing working it is **an
earth elemental being forced to obey by magical compulsion** — **not charmed**, but overpowered by the
figure's own will, and that if that will broke it would simply do as it pleased.

**[[dunton|Dunton]].** The passage runs about **400 metres**, curving gently left and descending, and
opens into a cavern — far smaller than the gleaners' chasm — holding **a couple of dozen tatty,
composite houses**, a two-storey stone **peel tower** at one end, pathways between the dwellings, and a
**barricade** across the open edge made of the same impossible jumble of things as
[[the-stone-river|the stone river]]. Guards watch it from the inside, facing out. **The party's tunnel
opens well within the barricade**, and closes behind them.

They go to **[[ketters-rest|Ketter's Rest]]**, the one building with an inn sign, and wake the landlord
**[[varen|Varen]]**, who is astonished to see anyone. Told they were sent by the High Priest, he is
nervous — and looks at them **more with pity than with fear**. He begins to disclaim any part in
sacrificing guests, and says plainly that he **cannot hide them from the Provost**, who **will have to
be told**. He breaks off from observing that they are all here in one piece to ask, stricken, whether
they **left anyone**; told they did not, his relief is obvious. He mentions that before this High
Priest came, a healer here *"would have changed everything"*, and that they now lack little in that
regard.

He pours a rough mushroom spirit and puts them upstairs in a room with blankets on the floor.
[[caspian-talon|Caspian]] casts **Alarm** on the doorway; the party sets watches anyway.

**The book.** On his watch, [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] first establishes that
[[codex-mortis|Kiona's infernal notes]] **can be sensed at close range** by anyone attuned to that kind
of magic — then reads them anyway. The section he wanted describes **[[undead-puppetry|making an undead
puppet]]** from a fresh corpse and channelling one's awareness and part of one's magical power through
it, in order to act in the world while staying safe and hidden. **His conclusion is that the figure in
the arena was a puppet for something else** — which means the party has not met whoever they were
actually talking to. He keeps it to himself overnight, *"concerned people ask how I know,"* and shares
the content the next morning **without naming the book**.

The party takes its **first long rest in a long while**, and wakes to a town that has already heard
about them.

## Key facts learned
- **The axe was keeping the minotaur alive.** Strongly magical, unidentifiable even with Identify, and
  confirmed as the cause of its regeneration. Cut free, the body decayed to almost nothing in about
  twenty seconds.
- **The creature regenerated in its den and not in the open** — the observation that made the fight
  winnable. **Chill Touch** stops it.
- **The arena pit is a dead end**: fifteen to twenty feet, easily climbed, with a den off it holding old
  gnawed bones and nothing else.
- **[[the-high-priest|The S2.36 voice was the High Priest]]**, and he confirms it. He opened the arena
  because the party looked to be in difficulty and he thought it would be interesting.
- **The minotaur was his experiment**, run on the axe, the circumstance, and the party's abilities. He
  did not want them dead, *"but of course the possibility was there."*
- **He is a relatively fresh undead** in red-and-gold robes, with a **human face altered to look more
  elvish**, called **"Greyface"** locally, and styling himself **High Priest**.
- **He does not believe in "God."** He says the word in air quotes and treats the Stonehunger as **a
  substance** — fascinating, unpleasant, and discriminating: *"what it doesn't like are things that it
  cannot reshape and make its own."*
- **Protection is a real thing inside the Stonehunger.** The forge *"had a certain sort of energy which
  gives protection"*; **many places within it have not been absorbed and may never be** — and
  disruption by outsiders can change that. He says the party disrupted the forge's. He does not say how.
- **[[the-buried-arena|The arena]] is curated.** The Stonehunger picked the elements that pleased it;
  the patchwork is deliberate, and it will change eventually.
- **Several dwarven sites were taken** when God was *"more expansive in its habits"* — a second,
  independent statement that its range has contracted.
- **He has been up into [[the-ancient-forge|the forge]]** and recovered things from it, and expects
  little left there next time.
- **[[the-arena-elemental|The stone-shaper is a captive]]** — an earth elemental **compelled**, not
  charmed, held by the High Priest's magical will, which it resists until threatened.
- **[[dunton|Dunton]] is real** — the gleaners' rumour from S2.31 made good. A barricaded cavern
  settlement of **humans and halflings**, with children, magical street-lighting, basic weaponry, and a
  **[[the-provost|Provost]]** at its head.
- **Dunton sacrifices somebody.** The party's safe-conduct is that God has decreed **their** sacrifice
  can wait. What the practice actually is went unasked.
- **[[undead-puppetry|Undead puppetry]]** is described in full in Kiona's notes: a corpse up to about
  eighteen hours dead, suffocation recommended, physical qualities inherited, mental ones the
  puppeteer's, perception by magical sensing rather than the corpse's organs, limited range extendable
  by prepared relay points, and a ceiling on the complexity of magic that can be cast through it. **It
  works unlike any magic Caspian knows.**
- **[[codex-mortis|The notes]] can be sensed** by anyone attuned to that magic, but only at close range.
