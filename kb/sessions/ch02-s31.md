## Recap
Continuing the same afternoon beneath [[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]], [[rell-aetris|Rell]] and
[[trevick|Trevick]] slip through the poorly-sealed buried door into the source of the pale glow: a
long hall (~40 by ~25 feet) of a style unrelated to the marble stair. Its **left wall** is a
faintly-glowing **[[the-broken-map|mural-map]]**, smashed and reformed countless times and (Caspian's
Detect Magic) preserved by strong **abjuration**; it proves to be a map of the region in a **far older
configuration** — fewer mountains where the Stonemarch now stands, a much larger Branweald,
settlements where none are today — its names in an **unknown script** that resembles but is not
Draconic. [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] copies it with his enchanted pen; [[trevick|Trevick]]'s Comprehend
Languages glasses cannot parse the unfamiliar names.

Six pillars of dark glass line the hall. Caspian glimpses smoke moving within one; Rell's **Detect
Thoughts** counts **eleven minds** — five party, six pillars — and reads one spirit's bleak **dual
nature**: a predator lying in wait to feed, and beneath it a deep loneliness, longing for its lost
home and "what has happened to it." (Rell fails the save against the contact and is left rattled.)
Caspian's Detect Magic reads **necrotic** magic and, on an Arcana check, marks the pillars as
**undead**; his **Identify** on one finds the glass **shelters** rather than cages the spirit — it is
bound to the pillar but could emerge — and he pulls back in fright as the thing coils to strike. At
the hall's far end stands a ~10-foot **gargoyle** statue, wings wrapped around itself, repaired many
times from mismatched stone, bearing an **enchantment aura** and signs of past movement; the party
takes it for a **golem guardian**. A mirror on a stick shows it clutches a **large key**, laid loose
in its hand. Rell first reads the room as a prison (gargoyle-jailer, pillars-cells), then — prompted
that the predatory mind expects to get its prey rather than chafing at captivity — revises to the
pillars being **guard-dogs / a defence system**.

To reach the corner **stone door** (frequently used, complex but pickable, no trap), Rell shrouds the
gargoyle end in **magical Darkness**, and [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] sets [[theodore|Theodore]] on the
gargoyle to guard the key. Rell picks the lock on the second attempt; the gargoyle does not stir. The
door opens onto a **~25–30-foot drop**, campfire and torchlight below, and the **clank of mining
machinery**; a spiral, library-style metal stair leads down. Voices call up cautiously — small
creatures who heard the noise but cannot see the unlit party.

Caspian sends **Theodore** down to scout. The room below is **dwarvish** — water/air-pumping
machinery (a sound familiar from the [[nerrin-mine]]) with climbable pipes — occupied by two
**pale, goblinoid** figures in mismatched dress, carrying spears, bows, and long **grapnel-tipped
"fishing poles."** Their **soft leather shoes** are not the spiked boots that made the excavation
tracks above (that trail stopped at the marble stair), though gleaning a kill's surface leavings is
exactly the gleaners' trade. A further archway and stair lead
down to a ramshackle room of ~14–15 battered **crates that stink of dead things** — later revealed as
the resting place of **the sleepers** the party must slip past. The party debates
options and settles on magic: Rell activates **innate sorcery** and **twin-casts Suggestion** on the
two — both fail their saves — commanding them to climb up, answer questions, and, at the word
"**sleep**," close their eyes and hum for seven hours.

The pair come up: an assertive **female** ([[turley|Turley]]) and her deferential companion
([[glorta|Glorta]]). Turley, not realising she is charmed, levels her spear, names the party
"**godless**," and — with patronising confidence — offers to let them hand over their valuables in
exchange for being shown out. Under questioning she reveals the shape of this place. The people here
are **gleaners** (see [[the-gleaners]]); they dwell inside their **"God,"** which
[[trevick|Trevick]] names the **[[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]]** — Turley accepts the name as apt.
God, she says, is **alive**: it moves, hunts, **devours the surface and swallows it down**, digests,
and moves on; the party stands **"in his throat."** The gleaners scavenge the scraps of its kills to
earn "**a place,**" lest they be **crushed** when God next goes below; those without a place — the
"godless" — are simply consumed. They **gleaned a kill "a little way back,"** which the party takes to
be Kethran's Fold itself. Pressed by [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] ("You sound guilty!"), Turley admits
the gleaners kept a small catch for themselves rather than sharing it — a persistent disingenuity the
party notes in her manner.

Turley lays out the way deeper: past **the sleepers** (asleep, but they wake at a scent), over the
**bridges** across a **chasm** — a "river" that is in fact **endlessly churning stone** — where a
dozen or two gleaners work and forty or fifty more are near the boss **[[tuffin|Tuffin]]**; then on to
the **[[the-custodian|custodian]]**, a huge-faced "soft old fellow" who guards the gate to the deadly
deep ("Grommer's Path") and may let them through if brought news of the world above. Rell offers
cooking techniques as payment to be escorted, and — to test the trigger — beckons [[glorta|Glorta]]
close and whispers "**sleep**"; Glorta obediently beds down. Turley grows uncharacteristically
**interested** in the party and, though she doubts Tuffin would allow it, agrees to consider leading
them on.

The party convinces the gleaners to guide them below (the sleeping Glorta is roused). Reading Turley
as still scheming, and unwilling to face the ~50 gleaners with her in tow, [[trevick|Trevick]] casts
**Pass Without Trace**; the party creeps past **the sleepers** — the creatures resting among the
stinking crates, who wake at a scent — so silently that the gleaners confess they had feared being
caught. Hearing the endless grind of stone-on-stone ahead, and with Turley again
warning of the crowd beyond, the party puts **both gleaners to sleep** and sends [[theodore|Theodore]]
to look into the cavern: a **web of bridges** spans the river of churning stone, with **many gleaners**
on the bridges and shores. The party splits over what to do — [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] is confident
they could quash fifty goblins; [[rell-aetris|Rell]] favours cutting the bridges one by one while
invisible, nudging gleaners into the churn, or a **Mass Suggestion** to send them all away. Undecided,
the party agrees to take time to work out a cunning plan, and the session ends.

## Key facts learned
- **The Stonehunger is real, and the party is inside it** (gleaner theology, corroborated by being
  physically within a vast underground complex; see [[the-stonehunger]]). The gleaners worship it as a
  living **"God"** that moves, devours the surface, digests, and moves on, crushing those without "a
  place." Its true nature — deity, titan, beast, construct — is **not established**.
- **The gleaners scavenged this village's collapse.** They "gleaned a kill a little way back," which
  the party understands to be Kethran's Fold — tying the gleaners (and the Stonehunger) to the
  unnatural collapse Mags identified in S2.30.
- **The gleaners are squatters in an older complex.** "There were many folks here before us, but we've
  made it ours." The marble stair, dwarvish machine-room, pillar chamber, map, and gargoyle predate
  them; the builders are unknown.
- **The S2.30 excavation is taken to be the gleaners' work** — gleaning a kill's surface leavings is
  exactly their trade — though the two gleaners seen wore soft leather shoes rather than the
  spiked/foreign-steel boots that left those tracks (presumably other gleaners); the foreign steel's
  origin stays unexplained.
- **The pillar chamber** holds six **[[the-glass-smoke|glass smoke]]** — bound undead spirits sheltered
  (not caged) in dark glass, dual-natured (hunger and lonely longing), able to emerge, dangerous — and
  a **gargoyle golem** (enchantment aura, moves, holds a key), plus the **[[the-broken-map|broken map]]**
  of the region in an older configuration.
- **The route deeper:** sleepers → bridges over a chasm of **churning stone** (~40–50 gleaners under
  [[tuffin|Tuffin]]) → the **[[the-custodian|custodian]]**'s gate onto Grommer's Path (the deep). A
  rumoured place, **Dunton**, is said to lie deeper — where "godless who found a place" live.
- **A clock:** Turley says God will "go below" (move on, crushing the placeless) within days or weeks.
