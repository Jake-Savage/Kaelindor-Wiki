## Recap

**Closing on the caves.** The convoy is about an hour and a half from the caves. [[caspian-talon|Caspian]]
finds the way, with [[rell-aetris|Rell]] and [[trevick|Trevick]] keeping watch; [[mags|Mags]], still
shaken by the parasite, is quieter than usual. **Olo Halfkettle** stumbles and hurts her ankle too
badly to walk; [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] gives her **a potion of healing** with a speech about the
physicians of the Bards' College, and she walks on, gingerly. The great wall of the cavern looms ahead.
Trevick takes the next stretch, Caspian helping and Fabian keeping the villagers watching their feet.

**The witch returns.** [[the-witch|The witch]] comes riding back the other way on her tiger and sees
them first. The villagers are terrified — of the tiger, and of her — and some of the children cry; she
ignores them entirely and rides up to the party.

- *"I wondered if you might still be out here. I spoke with Lae'ethorian. He, well, he is leaving."*
  Should they escape too, do they mean to **betray him and reveal where he is**? He is not as
  concerned about that as he should be.
- **Rell** answers that he owes little allegiance to those who make war on Lae'ethorian's people, but
  that Lae'ethorian is wanted for many terrible things, and it might be unwise not to tell the right
  people. If he means to be a power on the surface again, his power has **grown considerably**, and he
  will do far more harm.
- At that she looks back toward [[the-ossuary|the Ossuary]] with a clear **shudder of distaste** —
  more than fear — at the thought of him using and growing that power. Still: she has come to **see
  more wisdom in him than she expected**, and *"I cannot allow you to compromise this latest attempt
  of his."*
- **What she says of him.** She has given up much herself to avoid the path he has walked.
  Lae'ethorian is **a part of what is wrong among her people, a significant part** — but he has given
  up much to argue for what he thinks must be done, and **he goes back now to renew that and make his
  case**. His power has grown, and power is something her people respect, at present. She has been
  down here many years and does not know how matters stand **at court**. She **argued against his
  going** — but **the party's visit galvanised him**.
- **"How can you blame us?"** When Rell says the elves will use any power they can, she turns on him,
  flushed: how can anyone blame them for seeking **the one power that can actually do something** —
  and yet she herself refused to take it up, so perhaps she is no one to speak.
- **His path, as she tells it.** He is hardly the first to walk it; **it was begun years before his
  birth, and before hers**, and **born as he was, he had very little choice** — less than she had. **He
  does not seek to turn their people deeper into the embrace of [[thalparaneth|Thalparaneth]].** For
  all the party saw of him at the temple, **he seeks to rally their people to a battle that the Archon
  would avoid**, as the Archon did years ago.
- **Rell's read.** Rell tells her that nobody seeks out corruption; it is never a choice. He reads her
  completely: she thinks that is **nonsense** — she is certain people seek corruption and choose it —
  and there is **bitterness** in her, as if refusing it has cost her so much that part of her thinks
  she was a fool not to choose it. She means the party no harm, but Rell sees her **hardening**,
  coming to think that to protect Lae'ethorian she may **have to deal with them**.

**Caspian's word.** Caspian speaks up: corruption is not so black and white, and she wants to save
Lae'ethorian as they want to save these people. They will **say nothing of this place or of him**. He
says, quietly, that there are ways to make the villagers never speak of it, and that he knows people
who can **alter memories** and could learn to do it himself. They will all simply leave. He means it —
he wants nothing more than to be gone. She is visibly **relieved**.

- What Lae'ethorian is about is **a complex business** that even the party's authorities would not
  understand. **It will take him some weeks to get back across the border**; after that it hardly
  matters what they say, but he **must have the chance to return and make his case**. It probably will
  not make a difference, but it might. She would not see him caught, *"not after all this time."*
- She doubts they can fight their way to the surface. Lae'ethorian, she agrees, can fight his own way
  out — and, amused and a little impressed, says **he was not happy about the outcome of his attempt to
  retrieve his cattle from them**.
- **Her own way.** She has **her own means** out, which she has already refused to someone else — but
  those considerations hardly apply to the party (she later says she refused Lae'ethorian the road). It is **a shortcut through the Feywild**, not long,
  **warded along the path**, though nowhere in that land is safe now and she has not walked it in some
  time. It comes out not here but in **Kalredor**, on the far side of the mountains — near
  **[[mooncross|Mooncross]]**, by the Ridgeway.
- Trevick asks how not to carry anything back. She warded the path **with Lae'ethorian's help**:
  without it, it would have been impossible, because **the magic Rell called corruption is the only
  thing they know that holds the [[the-uthrel|uthrel]] back**. He provided the means to keep the road
  open.
- **The risk to her.** She does not hide that the party **could destroy her wards and trap her down
  here** — she has no intention of leaving, but being unable to is another matter.
- **The Keep and the gleaners.** She has no love for the Keep's people, nor they for her. Above are **the gleaners**, *"petty, but dangerous in
  numbers."*
- **Her price is their word** not to hunt Lae'ethorian or send others after him. Fabian gives it, at
  length — and **he is lying**: they are only friendly adventurers, grand strategy is not their
  business, and nobody would listen to them anyway. She would prefer that to be true. She will not hinder them if escape is all
  they want, and if they come to her home she will show them **another way clear of this place** — a
  refuge, she warns, not a comfortable one, and unlike Lae'ethorian she has **no need of more company
  down here**.

She gives Caspian directions by the plain's landmarks, west past the Keep, and rides off without
waiting. The party agrees at once. On the way Fabian prepares the villagers with the tale of the
Gorgon and the evil eye: **don't meet her eye, and if she speaks to you, treat her as a queen and take
a knee**. They take it in their stride — it is how they learned to deal with Lae'ethorian, the only
elf any of them has met.

**Grokkens.** On the way they pass a clump of stones in loose dust, and the dust shakes itself off and
becomes **several grokkens**: rock-like pack hunters, mouths of teeth in rows running back into the
throat, which they can turn inside out like a worm. Fabian steps forward — *"Keep away from my
villagers!"* — and blasts three back with **Thunderwave** from [[the-golden-lute|the golden lute]],
alerting everything for a long way. Rell sends two fleeing with a twinned **Dissonant Whispers**;
**Tom Pruett** and Trevick drive another off. But the rest pile onto Fabian: clawed down, bitten,
**hamstrung**, and finally torn and flung aside, **he falls dying**. Olo stands over him with her club.
**Max** the dog runs a wide circle, leaps onto one's back and **bites into its neck**, bringing it down.
Trevick's arrow pins another to the ground; one bites Rell hard; Caspian's **Fire Bolt** kills the
last. The others flee. Caspian finds a potion in Fabian's pocket and pours it into him — *"Fabs, how did you
end up in this stupid position?"*

**The garden.** Close to, they see what the Keep's patrols saw: **a shimmering mirage of a building
and a green space** that does not belong down here, with a light that is not there and then suddenly
bright, plants growing, a roof among them, and a clear boundary. It is **the same place the commander
described**. All of them can tell it is **an illusion** — a good one, but spread thin over a large
area, and so weaker; only Max is completely taken in. Crossing its edge, Caspian in particular feels
that they pass **with permission**; without it, the illusion would simply dissolve and they would be
**somewhere else** — presumably what happens to the Keep's attacks. The villagers think it the most
beautiful, most natural thing they have seen in years.

Inside **a high hedge with an Elvish gate** — a much grander version of the style the party saw at
[[derwyth|Derwyth]]'s, and one Fabian knows from his studies — stands **a single building**, one proper
floor and a roof half covered and half a **roof garden**, amid a flourishing garden of **illusory birds
and insects**. With Detect Magic, Caspian can tell the illusion is not actively maintained: it has been
cast so often over so long that it has **taken root** and keeps itself. Fabian (and later Trevick) try
to see through it and cannot; Rell keeps his head down, not wanting her attention.

**Her house.** The witch comes out, **her staff blazing under Detect Magic**, with no sign of the tiger,
calmer than before. She **surprised herself** with the offer, she says — Lae'ethorian's company has
**changed her perspective** in ways she did not expect; or perhaps she felt guilty for **not offering
him this road herself**. Either way, best they be gone. When Caspian asks to rest an hour, she first
looks at the villagers as **clutter** — Lae'ethorian's **discarded possessions** — but his care for
them reaches her, and she sees them as **people who need help**: *"You're right. I'm sorry. I did not
consider. Please rest."* She brings out **a great kettle of restorative tea**. They rest in her garden;
Fabian rallies them — *haven't we come far?* — recalling **Rell's leap through the bone storm**.

**What she tells Caspian.** She talks mostly to Caspian, whom she seems to find the most fun, and he
asks everything:

- **The road.** Travellers used to **anchor a point in the Feywild** and reach it quickly from where
  they were — something Caspian knew of only in theory, and which nobody does now because the Feywild
  is so dangerous. She used the road often when she first came to the Stonehunger. It has grown
  **worse and needed more warding** over the years, and she has **not walked it herself in the last
  three years**.
- **The Feywild** is **a wasteland overrun by the uthrel**. As far as she knows **the whole of it** is
  covered and **nothing else lives there**, fey included — the great tragedy of the elves. It is
  possible to push the uthrel back in limited ways with enough magic, at great danger.
- **Necromancy holds the uthrel back.** Magic of the kind Lae'ethorian uses **cannot destroy it but
  can hold it at bay**.
- **How she built it.** When she left **Thal'Mirrith** — clearly an **escape**; she ran — she took
  **necromantically charged stones** with her and laid the path with them. They lost their power over
  time, and **Lae'ethorian supplied new ones**. She had resigned herself to the road decaying; when he
  came down, **considerably after her**, she saw a chance to renew it.
- **Why she left.** She **does not practise necromancy**. She was **under pressure** to take it up and
  would not: she wanted to keep **the purity of her druidic practice**. She does not disapprove of it,
  but knows how it **damages the soul**, and is conflicted about her choice.
- **It is forbidden.** Opening private channels to the Feywild is **not authorised**, for fear the
  uthrel comes through. She did it anyway, because she wants to be **in the Stonehunger without being
  trapped in it**.
- **She refused Lae'ethorian the road.** He asked to use it, as he has before; she does not trust him
  not to do something problematic with it. She trusts the party **not to know how to**.
- **On the way through:** stay on the path, move fast, don't stop to sleep, and **bring no trace of the
  uthrel back**. It grows poorly on the Material Plane and needs substantial contamination to start an
  outbreak, but it can. Fire is not her magic; she cannot say how much would destroy it.
- **Elsewhere:** the connection to the Feywild can never be cut entirely, and where there are
  connections there are outbreaks. The **Elenfain** wards every entrance in the lands the elves hold —
  but she speaks with a sense that it is holding back something that will not be held back.
- **Where it comes out:** an **anchor point just west of the Ridgeway Pass**, the route she took when
  she fled Thal'Mirrith, in the woods away from any town, about **a day's walk** along the road. The far
  gate is keyed to having entered by hers, and would be invisible otherwise.
- Caspian offers to shore up any failing wards. She is surprised he thinks he could, and **wonders
  how and why he would have that sort of magic**; he says he likes to experiment. Strengthening the
  path would be welcome, she says, but interfering carelessly could cause **a backlash**.

Trevick reads her: she **plays down the risks**, knowing she should not have built this, and in the
end it is **not her problem** — there is a fatalism in her, whether because she will not be there or
because she thinks an outbreak will come sooner or later anyway. Fabian, Trevick and Caspian put the
fear of the road into the villagers. Then she opens the gate — anchored, needing little fresh magic —
and **Caspian steps through first**.

**The Feywild.** The sky is the same **pinkish purple** as her garden. Everything else is **a powdery
grey, ash-like substance** with lumps in it, stretching away into a haze; the air is thick with
floating spores. **The path is clear of uthrel, but bare** — Caspian judges that the same necromancy
that holds the uthrel back suppresses any other life. The land is flat, its hills still there but every
growing thing flattened, so they can see a long way. Trevick passes **half-buried shapes of buildings
and woods**, and it brings back what he saw at home.

- **The black towers.** Far off to the left, on the horizon, Trevick and Rell spot **large buildings**
  — martial-looking, **towers, two linked by what could be a fort wall**, of some **black stone** —
  too far to see anyone. The witch never mentioned them. They keep moving.
- **The wards.** They cannot be seen, but Caspian works out there is one **every couple of hundred
  metres**, and that **the newer ones are considerably more potent** than the older. At one or two
  points the wards have weakened and the path narrows, but it is open.
- **Detect Magic finds nothing** here at all: the uthrel-ruined Feywild is **a blank**, dead even to the
  background magic of the ordinary world.
- Everyone is worn down; Caspian and Rell are **exhausted** (cleared by the long rest after). The villagers are running on nothing, but
  they keep going. Fabian prods Caspian with his sheathed sword every time he stops.

After a day or so they find **the far gate's glowing outline** where she said it would be. Before
anyone steps through, Trevick checks every child's feet and pockets and the dog's paws and tells the
adults what happened to his home; Caspian checks them too, and Fabian cleans everyone with
Prestidigitation. They are **confident they bring nothing through**. Caspian maps what he can, though
the road **compresses the geography** and could not be used to teleport back.

**The far side.** The anchor is always there but **invisible** from this side; nothing marks it, even to
Detect Magic. Only the witch can open it, or hold it open. It is close to **a one-way door** — but
someone powerful enough who knew roughly where to look **could find it and unpick it**. A small risk,
but a risk.

**Out.** They come out at about **ten at night**, late autumn, cold and dark, in **the foothills of the
Stonemarch**, in wilderness with no sign of habitation — **out of the Mirrowen**. Caspian ritually
casts **Leomund's Tiny Hut** for the children; some villagers fall asleep before the talking is done.
After more than a decade underground, they have grass, trees and stars. **Seventeen villagers and the
dog are out.** The party takes a long rest.

## Key facts learned
- **Lae'ethorian is going back to Thal'Mirrith** to renew his case at court. The witch says it will
  take him **some weeks** to cross the border, that **the party's visit galvanised him**, and that she
  argued against it.
- **What he argues for, per the witch:** not a deeper embrace of Thalparaneth, but **rallying the elves
  to a battle the Archon would avoid**. His path **began before his birth**, and he had little choice in
  it.
- **Necromancy holds back the uthrel.** It cannot destroy it. This is the witch's account, and the
  reason, by implication, her people seek it.
- **The Feywild is a wasteland of uthrel**, as far as the witch knows entirely; nothing else lives there.
- **The witch is an elf druid who fled Thal'Mirrith** rather than take up necromancy, and built a
  private, forbidden road through the Feywild, renewed with Lae'ethorian's necromantic stones.
- **Black towers stand in the ruined Feywild**, seen from her road.
- **The road's far anchor is just west of the Ridgeway Pass** in Kalredor, invisible and nearly
  one-way, but could be unpicked by a skilled mage who knew where to look.
- **Mooncross** is the League's most Elvish city (see [[mooncross]]).
- **The evacuation succeeded**: seventeen villagers and the dog are out of the Stonehunger.
