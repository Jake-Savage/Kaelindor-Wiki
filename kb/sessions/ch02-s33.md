## Recap
The party regroups at the northeastern end of the chasm, which is still deserted after the
[[the-stone-river|stone river]] was cleared of [[the-gleaners|gleaners]] in S2.32, and goes in through
the cleft together.

**The cleft and the courtyard.** A high investigation check reads the opening: the rock here is under
great **stress**, cracked, and in places **compressed to an almost glass-like texture** — something has
been trying to **force the rock closed** and has **not succeeded**. Inside,
[[the-golden-courtyard|the courtyard]] proves to be about forty feet across, its open centre laid with
elaborate, colourfully-dyed **abstract tiles** much like those in the pillar chamber above (some
cracked, most intact, with weaker protective magic on them), and its **arched portico** on three sides
now pressed into and half-swallowed by the rock. The fourth side, where the party entered, has
crumbled away entirely. There are **no other exits** — though there plainly once were, and the rock is
**actively pressing in**, held back by some resistant effect. From the height of the arches, the
builders stood **taller than humans — six to seven feet** rather than five to six. [[mags|Mags]]'s
reading is that the [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] swallowed the courtyard and that its magic is what
keeps it from being digested — a **supposition**, and the party's, not something the fiction states.

**The statue.** The [[the-golden-dragon-statue|golden dragon]] on its plinth is a **naturalistic,
true-to-life** depiction of very fine work, and appears to be gold. [[mags|Mags]] tests it with a
fingernail (Arcana 19): she feels a moment of soft-metal **give**, then a familiar **protective
enchantment** engages and the surface hardens beneath her nail. She is unharmed. Around the base runs
an **inscription in Draconic script** — archaic in form but close enough to modern Draconic to read —
which [[trevick|Trevick]] parses through his [[glasses-of-comprehend-languages|Comprehend Languages
glasses]] and reads aloud:

---

**The Lord of Light greets the dawn of a new age.**

---

No one recognises the phrase; every history check falls short. [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]], examining
whether the statue could be taken, finds instead that the **plinth's upper section can be rotated**,
and that it **has been rotated before** — though the magic distorts any sense of when, and because the
plinth's base does not move there are no marks on the floor. The party reads the statue as the thing
**holding the crushing rock back**, and declines to remove it.

**What the statue is.** [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] ritual-casts **Detect Magic** and, on an Arcana check
(aided by [[rell-aetris|Rell]], who cheerfully feigns proficiency), establishes: the **abjuration** on
the statue is **powerful and working**, though what would happen if it failed is beyond them; and
alongside it sits **evocation** — a capacity to **emit** magical energy — and, separately, a capacity to
**absorb** magical energy. A **perception** check leaves Caspian aware of **something else magical**
nearby that he cannot place, shielded or out of reach; he says so aloud to the group, and Mags
independently senses the same thing and equally cannot fix it.

**The experiment.** A cantrip **Light** cast on the statue visibly **diminishes** at its surface — the
statue drinks a little of it. The party then rotates the statue at length: 180° anticlockwise (from
west to east), a full clockwise circle, a full anticlockwise circle. **Nothing changes**, in Detect
Magic or otherwise, and the statue takes no damage; the mechanism feels **designed** to turn and the
magic makes the weight almost unnoticeable. Rell tries **Sorcerous Burst** (fire, then every damage type it offers) and Fabian **Vicious Mockery**; every one
is **absorbed** with no effect and no mark. Magic does not generally dissipate, so the statue may be
storing what it takes — but no evidence of that appears.

**The hidden cache.** Caspian spends a full ten-minute **Detect Magic** concentrating solely on the
half-sensed signature, with **advantage** and Mags's **Guidance**, and locates it: beneath a
**flagstone under the northern portico**, outwardly indistinguishable from its neighbours. Rell's iron
staff will not lever it — a **strong abjuration** resists mundane force, and the field cannot be mined
around. Caspian offers **Dispel Magic**; the DM warns that this flagstone's abjuration is **bound to
the statue's**, so dispelling one takes the other, and the party drops the idea rather than release the
rock.

Mags then reads the **weathering on the tiles** (survival 22) and recovers the courtyard's **original
orientation**: with the dragon turned to face the flagstone (north by
[[caspian-talon|Caspian]]'s compass) it is facing what was **east — dawn — in the courtyard's original
construction and position**. With the statue so aligned, Mags spends a **Guiding Bolt** on it. The **radiant** magic is
absorbed like the rest, but this time the statue **glows from within** and throws a **beam of light out
onto the flagstone**. While the beam lasts the stone lifts freely by hand.

Beneath is a **chest** of organic material, unlocked, untrapped (Rell checks with his mirror-on-a-stick),
unwarded, and so old it **falls apart as it is handled**. Inside are the remains of **ceremonial
regalia** — the cloth mostly crumbled — and, pristine and untouched by time in the way of magical
things, **two items**: an elaborate, impractical, **many-tiered crown** with side pieces that would
badly muffle the wearer's hearing, and a **hand-piece** of four finger-rings joined by chains to a
bracelet. With them lie **weapons** that are ornate but genuinely functional in design — **two curved
swords** and something that **shot darts** — all non-magical, decayed and unusable. See
[[the-draconic-regalia]].

Fabian's history check (with Guidance) finds nothing in the weapons, but the **costume is cut for
someone taller and more slender than a typical human**, and he recalls legends — not even stories — of a
time **well before the recorded history of the current age**, of people **physically changed by
draconic magic** much as tieflings are changed by Korrathian planar magic (see
[[the-dragon-touched]]). He judges it **plausible** that the regalia and weapons were meant for someone
of that kind. The party takes everything to be identified later, restores the statue to its original
facing and replaces the flagstone, and leaves — conscious that the gleaners have now been gone the best
part of an hour, and that those who left under ordinary persuasion rather than magic will drift back.

**The gate.** The party crosses to the archway. (The DM resets the S2.32 ending here: this counts as
**first contact** with the face, and the question it asked at the end of that session is **scratched**.)
The stone forms into the same large face — interested, amused, unsympathetic, not hostile. Addressed by
Fabian (still small and painted as a gleaner), it opens by remarking that the party's
trick with the **"little scavengers"** amused it — *"the way you sent them forth; I haven't seen someone
do that before"* — and says of itself: **"I am the eyes of God in this part."**

It refuses at first, on grounds of **waste**: the party will very likely not survive Grommar's Path, and
if they do, **"the eyes of God below"** — its **brothers** — will hardly share news of them back to it.
It says they will not be able to return this way. It would rather keep them here and watch them explain
themselves to the gleaners when they come back.

Fabian buys interest with a story instead: his **festival mask** and the pilgrims of
[[shadows-meet|Shadows Meet]] in [[greywatch|Greywatch]], embellished (performance 25, with advantage
and Mags's Enhance Ability). The face becomes visibly animated — *"I had not imagined the petty people
of this age were capable of such elaboration"* — but calls the surrounding country **paltry fare**, and
adds the telling remark that the party is **not of this place**: *"you are too late and too
interesting… travellers from somewhere far more promising, such as I had begun to suspect were no
longer in the world."*

When [[rell-aetris|Rell]] observes that not all of them are from the world above as it sees it, and
that there is **a limit to the lands it may now reach**, the face shows an involuntary **spasm of
frustration** before mastering it. Mags's **natural 20 Insight (27)** reads it plainly: the creature
**genuinely believes** there is now a limit to the lands they can **claim**, is **baffled** by it, and
feels something close to **shame** — the consciousness of a **diminished power** — which it then
deliberately masks.

Rell offers a **wager**: let them pass; if they fail on Grommar's Path, they will leave **a thread the
face can pull** — a means by which word can be passed back; if they succeed, the face is to accept it as
their victory and grant its **acknowledgement** for the descent. Mags, still reading it, is confident
the creature has the power to grant **neither the passage-prize nor the reward** it is agreeing to, and
absolutely will not admit as much. It accepts anyway, saying what it truly wants — *"I would dearly
like to pull those threads that would lead us back to lands more worthy of God's sight"* — and grants
its **acknowledgement and blessing** to be carried below. On a formal acknowledgement from Rell the
stone **ripples aside** and an opening appears.

Beyond is a dark passage sloping **steeply down**, deliberately **hewn** rather than raw. Mags, Caspian
and Rell each recognise the style: the sort of **narrow ceremonial passageway that leads into the more
seriously sacred and secretive dwarven temples of [[calvyr|Calvyr]]**. The party walks down into the
dark, and the door **closes behind them**. The session ends there.

## Key facts learned
- **The gate calls itself "the eyes of God in this part,"** and speaks of **"the eyes of God below"** as
  its **brothers** — so the Stonehunger has watchers stationed through it, not just this one. (Its own
  account of itself; the party has no independent confirmation.)
- **It is watching the cavern.** It saw the S2.32 Mass Suggestion and found it novel and amusing.
- **It believes its kind's reach is shrinking.** Under Mags's natural-20 Insight, the face genuinely
  believes there is now **a limit to the lands they can claim**, is baffled by it, and feels shame at a
  diminished power. It also says it had **begun to suspect that more promising lands were no longer in
  the world** — and what it wants from the bargain is **threads leading back to such lands**.
- **It cannot deliver what it promised.** Mags reads (same Insight) that the creature has the power to
  grant **neither** the prize nor the reward of Rell's wager, and is concealing that. The bargain was
  struck regardless, and it did open the way.
- **The courtyard is not where it was built.** It is **no longer in its original position or
  orientation** (established — the statue's mechanism answers only to the original bearing), the rock is
  **actively pressing in**, and something is holding it off. That the
  [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]] **swallowed** it, and that its **magic** is what prevents digestion,
  are the **party's suppositions** — neither is confirmed. Something has also tried to **crush the cleft
  shut** and failed.
- **The statue's mechanism.** The [[the-golden-dragon-statue|dragon]] **absorbs** magic of every type
  tried (fire, psychic, light, the full range of Sorcerous Burst) without effect — but fed **radiant**
  magic while turned to the courtyard's **original east (dawn)**, it glows from within and **emits a
  beam** onto the hidden flagstone, which then lifts freely. **Both halves are required** — the bearing
  must be east relative to the courtyard's **original construction and position**, not east by present
  compass, which is why the earlier rotations achieved nothing. Its plinth rotates and has been rotated
  before.
- **The inscription**, in archaic Draconic around the statue's base: **"The Lord of Light greets the
  dawn of a new age."** Nobody recognises the phrase or who the Lord of Light is.
- **The abjurations are linked.** The flagstone's ward is **bound to** the statue's —
  dispel one and the other falls (DM warning), which is why the party did not.
- **A cache of ceremonial regalia** lies under the flagstone: a tiered crown and a chained hand-piece,
  both **magical and pristine**; crumbled ceremonial clothing; and non-magical, decayed but functional
  weapons — two curved swords and a dart-shooter. The clothing is cut for someone **taller and more
  slender than a typical human**. See [[the-draconic-regalia]].
- **[[the-dragon-touched]]:** Fabian recalls legends of a time **well before the current age's recorded
  history** of people **physically altered by draconic magic**, comparably to how Korrathian planar
  magic marks tieflings. He judges it **plausible** — not established — that the regalia was made for
  such a person.
- **Grommar's Path begins as a dwarven sacred way.** The passage beyond the gate is a **hewn ceremonial
  passageway** of the kind that fronts the more secret and sacred **temples of [[calvyr|Calvyr]]**
  (Mags, Caspian and Rell concur).

## Threads opened / advanced
- **[[the-stonehunger-below]]** — the party passes the gate by bargain rather than force and enters
  **[[grommars-path|Grommar's Path]]**, which closes behind them. The Stonehunger's structure gains a
  new layer: watchers called the **eyes of God**, and a stated sense of **diminishment**.
- **[[the-korrathian-conspiracy]]** — the [[the-draconic-regalia|regalia]] is the strongest candidate
  yet for the "something that does not belong" the Brood sent Soren to recover, and unlike the statue it
  is **portable**. Not confirmed as their object.
- New: **who is the "Lord of Light"** of the inscription, and what "new age" was being greeted; **who
  built the courtyard** (six-to-seven-foot builders, Draconic script, Calvyr-style passage below — not
  yet one answer); **what the crown and hand-piece do** (unidentified); **why a dwarven temple-way to
  Calvyr** lies behind the Stonehunger's gate; and **why the eyes of God believe their reach has
  shrunk** — kept separate from the Mirrowen's fragmentation until the fiction links them.
