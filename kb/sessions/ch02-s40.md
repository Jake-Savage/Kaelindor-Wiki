## Recap

**The decision not to ask.** The party had agreed in S2.39 to go back to [[the-ossuary|the Ossuary]]
and put the case to [[the-high-priest|Greyface]]. It does not. Talking it through, the party weighs
what it knows about the puppet:

- **While the operator sleeps, the puppet is much less use** — it can carry out behaviour planned in
  advance and little else. **Everything sleeps**; there are magical ways to stay awake, at a cost.
- **The villagers believe the operator himself is in the Ossuary.** He has never been secretive with
  them about it.
- **A puppet's range is not miles**, though a lair can extend it.
- **Detect Magic would register the puppet but not trace the link** back to whoever works it. A
  dispel could in principle strike at the magic animating the body, but **not at the connection**.
- **When the Stonehunger dives cannot be known.** Everyone who has spoken of it has said there are
  signs and that nobody really knows. Five days is a fair middle guess; it could be much sooner.

[[mags|Mags]] casts **Augury**, asking whether, if they leave within thirty minutes, Greyface will
pursue them fast enough to catch the slowest of them. The answer is **weal**. On that, the party
decides to go **tonight, without telling him**.

**Recruiting the village.** It is evening; the hunters are coming back in from [[the-plain|the plain]]
with crab-creatures and mushrooms. [[trevick|Trevick]] goes among them, learning which ground is safe
and which is not, and encouraging people to come.

[[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] draws about two-thirds of the village to [[ketters-rest|Ketter's Rest]] with
a song. It is enjoyed, and inspires nobody. He follows it with a speech: the party has been inspired
by their perseverance, they are close to the top, and **in less than five days — possibly much less —
this place goes back down**. Asked whether they have talked to Greyface and whether he is coming, Fabian
says **yes, they have talked to him, and he says it goes down in five days** — true as far as it goes,
and silent on the question of his blessing. [[the-provost|The Provost]] backs him, a little
uncomfortably.

The others add their own appeals:

- **[[mags|Mags]]** invokes [[naelos|Naelos]]. Some villagers make signs of devotion; others are
  uneasy.
- **[[rell-aetris|Rell]]** tells them they have been living like rats under someone else's
  sufferance, and that this is the chance to take that power back.
- **Fabian** speaks of relatives on the surface and of **children who have never seen the sun**.

Rell notices a young man slipping out partway through. Mags goes with him; he is fetching the third of
the village that did not come, with an account of the meeting that is not very positive.

The appeal **works**. Many are keen. Others will go if everyone goes. Some are **uneasy about leaving
without Greyface's leave**, and **more than one tries to go and tell him**. The party physically
blocks them, and Rell argues that the village has **not yet drawn his anger** and should not invite
it. Most comply, reluctantly. People are told to pack **keepsakes only** and be ready in thirty
minutes; Fabian's passing mention of bringing **arms** makes some of them more anxious, not less.

**Trevick's gifts.** Trevick finds a halfling and gives him one of the **romance novels** he carries
(see S2.29), along with a copy in Torvish, so the two of them might learn the language on the road —
*"my friend's an excellent writer."* He then pulls a **[[nameweave-crackers|Nameweave cracker]]** with
Fabian. It goes off **violently**, singeing both of them; the nervous become more skittish and the bold
more eager. Mags had kept the children back. Fabian lightens the mood with small conjured tricks.
**Two crackers remain.**

**Departure.** **All thirty-four villagers come**, and the old dog. Five argued against it to the end
but would not be left behind. The column includes small children, the elderly, and far too much
baggage; Mags makes the overloaded drop things whenever they fall behind. **No message is left for
Greyface.**

- **Order of march:** the Provost at the front, navigating, with [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] and Fabian;
  Trevick in the middle, keeping **Pass Without Trace** over the whole column; Mags at the back.
- **Light:** a dim, red-tinted, hooded Light spell.
- **Rell runs a false trail** with Mags's lantern and sets a **timed flare** — a candle burning down to
  lamp oil — about **two miles** off in another direction.
- **[[hawk|Hawk]]** flies a lit pebble away in a decoy direction, then turns back to **watch the
  village**, for about two hours.

**Hawk's mind.** Rell forms a telepathic bond with Hawk to keep the scattered party in touch (range
about a mile). It is **much more like bonding with a person than with an animal**: the mind is not
simple, and what comes through feels **stunted** — as though limited, or deliberately holding back.
Rell assumes Trevick knows, and does not mention it. **Trevick does know** (DM-confirmed).

**The tunnels.** The Provost is aiming for a stretch of **porous rock and caves** like those at the
Ossuary's entrance, where there are **steep vertical shafts leading upward**. They are **not natural** —
"tumbled out," as she puts it — and she does not know by whom.

**The march.** Mags keeps the column moving well past the point people wanted to stop. About ninety
minutes in, people are flagging badly; the caves are still **hours** away. The pace is slow, but many
are giving everything they have — the old, and those carrying small children. Trevick renews Pass
Without Trace one last time.

**The alarm.** Hawk comes back **in alarm** and lands on Trevick, pressing close against him, plainly
frightened. Through the bond: he saw **something at the village that scared him badly** and fled
straight back. It was coming **broadly after the party** — though it could as easily have been
following the false trails — and he did not wait to find out. Questioned quietly, Hawk conveys that it
was **something large**, and that **it was not the Stonehunger itself**: no sense of walls caving in.

**The rearguard.** Caspian proposes that he and Mags **drop back** and buy the convoy time; Mags calls
it *"a very moral idea"* and asks him to put his brain to speeding the villagers up as well. Fabian and
Trevick go on with the column. Rell, summoned over the bond, runs back to join them.

**What is coming.** First Caspian alone, then all three, hear it: a **clattering of objects striking
each other and the ground**, like the bones Mags shakes for divination, on a far larger scale, carried
on the air from the direction of the village. Caspian sends [[theodore|Theodore]] up in Hawk's claws
and rides the rat's senses. On the wind is the scent of **old, dry bones** — and of **magic being
maintained at an aggressive level**, which Caspian recognises at once, partly from the past couple of
days and **partly from much older memories**. Theodore shares those memories and **panics**; Caspian
pulls him back into his hood.

Mags casts a **second Augury**: by staying back as a diversion, will they prevent the convoy being
attacked by Greyface's forces? The answer is **weal and woe** — it depends on too much that has not
happened yet.

Rell, looking toward his flare, picks out **darkness against darkness**: a **very large shape —
eighteen or twenty feet tall** — whether one thing or many he cannot tell, **moving quickly** and
skirting wide of the rearguard, **toward the flare** rather than toward the convoy.

**The patrol.** Up front, Fabian spots a **patrol of six** off to the left, far away, carrying no light
and making no attempt at concealment — stomping along. The Provost says they could be **soldiers from
[[the-keep|the Keep]]**, or **priests**, and they must be avoided:

- **According to Greyface, they regard the villagers as parasites living in the body of God.**
- **They tolerate Dunton only because Greyface made some arrangement with them** — convinced them **he
  was one of them**.
- **Out here, they will kill the villagers if they find them.**
- The hunters find them **easy to avoid**, because **they never leave their patrol routes**.

Fabian halts the column for a breather and goes down the line hushing people. Trevick and Fabian rig a
**bottle of lamp oil with a lit wick**, and **Hawk carries it off** — only just able to manage the
weight — to drop it near the patrol and draw them away. The clattering is now faintly audible to the
convoy; Fabian tells them it is the wind in the great empty spaces, and then, when that is plainly not
believed, that there are all sorts of dangers down here and that is reason enough to get moving. The
column gets back on its feet.

**Where it stands.** The thing from the village appears to be heading swiftly for Rell's flare. Mags,
Rell and Caspian hold as a rearguard in a rough triangle between it and the convoy — within striking
distance if it turns, and with time to intercept if it does.

## Key facts learned
- **Greyface's puppet is far less capable while he sleeps** — only pre-planned behaviour. He has to
  sleep like anyone, unless he pays for magic to stay awake.
- **Puppet range is not miles**, though a lair can extend it; **Detect Magic** finds a puppet but not
  its operator, and **a dispel can reach the animating magic but not the link**.
- **The villagers believe Greyface's body is in the Ossuary.**
- **The dive is genuinely unpredictable**: about five days on the middle estimate, possibly far sooner.
- **Hawk's mind is person-like**, not animal — and it feels limited, or deliberately withheld.
- **There are man-made vertical shafts leading upward** in the porous rock and caves the Provost is
  making for, like those at the Ossuary's entrance; she does not know who cut them.
- **Something very large — eighteen to twenty feet — came out of the village after the party**, its
  passage sounding like clattering bone and smelling of **old bones and aggressively maintained
  magic**. It is not the Stonehunger. **It is Greyface's** — the party assumes so, and the DM confirms it.
- **Caspian recognises that magic from much older memories**, and so does Theodore. Those memories
  belong to **what Caspian is** (DM-confirmed; the party does not know).
- **Patrols from the Keep** — soldiers or priests — walk fixed routes across the plain. They think of the villagers as **parasites inhabiting the body of God**, and
  **Greyface persuaded them he was one of them**.
