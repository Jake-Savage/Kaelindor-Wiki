## Recap

**In the Ossuary.** The party is still sitting in the human-scale corner of the bone hall. The puppet
is **lifelike as undead go and not remotely perfect** — its expressions are too exaggerated and the
muscles of the face do not quite move correctly at the right moment, which is **grotesque to deal
with** even though it communicates clearly enough. Its manner is **stranger and less convincing** than
it was the night before.

[[caspian-talon|Caspian]] has just argued him out of his fatalism, and he takes it up:

> *"Well, I certainly did used to think that. And — well, perhaps I've spent rather too much time
> alone. This is a very odd conversation, but it's been a while since I've had any sort of serious
> conversation at all. And I suppose I shouldn't miss the opportunity when I have it."*

**[[thalparaneth|Thalparaneth]].** He gestures at the bone walls. This place is very old, but it
represents **something that still exists**, and in which he has **some skill**. Real necromancy, he
says, is not like other branches of magic — and there are **parts of magic that are no longer
recognised**. This temple was raised in furtherance of **a principle almost completely lost**, or lost
until relatively recently; and he has come to think **it is not the only one**.

> *"There are others. Other strands to magic that we no longer use. And that is a problem. **I tried,
> and I failed, to make a case that they should be recovered, used. I lost that argument.** And this
> is my — well, my place of exile, one might say."*

He lost it to **others in his family** — *"a disagreement of policy, strategy. I have always been a
better scholar than a politician."*

The temple belongs to **Thalparaneth**. Nobody has heard the name. The word **sounds Elvish**, and
[[mags|Mags]], who knows the ordinary Elvish names for all the gods, establishes **by elimination that
it is not one of them**.

Thalparaneth, he says, was **seen as a god for a long time**, and embodied the principle of — death is
not correct; **better to say un-life**. *"It is more of an opposite to life than any sense of death as
its end."* It is **an energy**, working quite differently from the vital energies [[naelos|Naelos]]
represents. **His kinsman rediscovered some of its ways**, practised them and taught them to others,
Greyface included; there has been **a revival of the practice**, and **he was raised to it**.

And the temple **sharpens that influence**: *"to use necromancy here is very much easier than it might
be in other contexts."* It has **enhanced his magic greatly**, enabling things otherwise beyond him.
*"When I came here fleeing, I have found a resource I could not have dreamt of."*

**What it costs.** Unprompted, he gives the party the warning that shapes the rest of the session.
Real necromancy **is not a healthy thing for a living person to do**. It is wielded readily by those
who have embraced undeath — but *"for those who wish to **preserve their mortality, as I do**, it is a
delicate business. **It corrupts the body, and over time it breaks the mind.** Operating through these
proxies lessens the effect somewhat, but I cannot pretend that I am unmarked by it."*

[[caspian-talon|Caspian]] does not hide that he recognises this from
[[codex-mortis|Kiona's notes]]. Greyface notices: *"you know something, I see. That's good."*

**Why he is here.** He came down **about twelve years ago**, found the temple quickly, spent **a great
deal of power** getting the villagers down, and on arrival **replenished it recklessly** — he has
learned to be more careful since. [[mags|Mags]] finds **no deception** in any of it.

> *"I came down here to hide, because I feared my life would not be worth much had I remained where I
> was. But I have found more than a hiding hole. In some ways that is of course a blessing, but in
> others, **knowledge without the power to act on it can be a torture**."*

And then the thing that undoes the "strange coincidence" at [[black-barrow|Black Barrow]]. Asked
whether he knew the creature was here before he came:

> *"Yes. I had heard of it, and I had conducted or commissioned **divinations** to understand **where
> and when it might surface again**. I was lucky, I suppose, in that I didn't have to wait over long.
> There are other routes in, but they were barred to me."*

He will not go back. His face twists at the suggestion: he has **no standing any more**, he made his
play and it was rejected, and *"perhaps I was wrong. It's possible. I, after all, had very little
data."* He adds that **he has made himself a traitor merely by speaking with them** — a traitor to
*"my country, my people"* — and when [[mags|Mags]] asks which country, he looks at her and says **"it
doesn't matter now."** He assumes **the world thinks him dead** and would rather it did.

**[[the-balance|The demonstration]].** He asks [[mags|Mags]] to cast something central to Naelos's
practice. She taps his nose and casts **Guidance**. He makes the thread of it **visible** and casts
something slightly stronger alongside it, which **does not combine with hers but encircles it**. The
two magics are **extremely different** — his is **uncomfortable**, and to Mags, closest, it feels
**hollow in a profound way**, a real absence. Where they meet, **the effect of both is strengthened**.
He holds it a moment before it **collapses**, and is left **visibly drained**.

They are **antithetical poles**, he explains — hard to combine, *"especially when there is no
conduit"*, and powerful for exactly that reason. He tries to sketch a diagram and finds **the puppet's
hands too clumsy**, visibly frustrated; [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] takes it down instead.

What he draws is **[[the-balance|the whole structure of magic]]**: **[[itharis|Itharis]] at the
centre**, and **four opposed pairs** around her — **[[naelos|Naelos]] against
[[thalparaneth|Thalparaneth]]**, **[[calvyr|Calvyr]] against [[vraxyn|Vraxyn]]**,
**[[korrathian|Korrath]]'s magic** (which he places by Vraxyn, calling it *"odd — channelled outside
its proper place"*, its principle unnamed), and **[[rhynharran|Rhynharran]]** against **a broken
power**. Nine in all, and **he regards it as complete**. *"The ancients could do things that we could
never imagine. And it's not because they were stronger — it's because they understood that magic is not
separate strands, but woven."*

**[[rell-aetris|Rell]] offers himself up.** He interjects to ask what Greyface makes of magic drawn
from worlds beyond, clears a space and casts **Arms of Hadar**. Greyface watches without alarm and
reads it back: **"drawing power from another source, but reordering things such that they resemble
something which comes from another place — realigning the magic of this plane into a shape less
familiar."** Rell accepts it. Asked its purpose he says **survival and defence**, which draws a
dissatisfied look — *"too often our own purposes blind us to reality"* — and then reframes it as
**control**: easier to bend a will away from you than to stand against it. That lands.

> *"There are — or there were — magics which bent that way, toward control, order, defence. I think
> they were practised by people who lived in this land a long time ago. **I have found traces of them
> down here. You may have seen them yourself.** But as a separate school of magic, it has not been part
> of our world for a long time."*

Nobody follows that up. He places Rell's magic opposite Rhynharran, muses that *"perhaps more old
things are surfacing than I realised — the timing could be better,"* and names the **ninth, broken** power — **Sarasthanae**.
[[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] knows it: **an aspect of Rhynharran** covering the **bardic arts,
storytelling and creativity**, usually treated as a subset. Caspian notes that **nine is significant in
magic**; Fabian notes that **the elves, apart from the Archon, are ruled by a council of nine**.

**The offer.** [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] asks to be taught. Greyface's answer is a warning:

> *"You are almost halfway toward being able to practise no other sort of magic in any case. And you
> should know better than most the cost that comes with it… **This power severs bonds, much as Naelos
> strengthens them.** You lose your ability to see others for what they are to you, what they are to
> one another. You lose compassion, empathy, a sense of people as anything other than instruments."*

He has **developed methods of containment** and keeps rules for himself — *"and even I have days when I
cannot recall any of the reasons for the rules I have made for myself. And I have been raised to this
power. **If you choose to use it, you will lose yourself.**"* Told the toll has already been taken, he
answers simply: **"I know."**

He agrees anyway, reluctantly — *"better perhaps it is in your hands than those already lost to other
considerations. I could teach you its principles, I suspect. I am loath to do it"* — and lets slip,
for the second time, that **Caspian's "condition" might offer him some protection**. [[mags|Mags]] and
[[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] both catch the word. Caspian ignores it.

**The price is a living subject**: *"it would not be costless. Not to you, not to me, and **not to the
subject on which we would have to practise**."*

[[mags|Mags]], to everyone's astonishment, **lets him stay** — one day, then back to [[dunton|Dunton]].
Caspian **asks her first**, which she marks as growth. Greyface tries twice more to talk him out of it.

**Back in Dunton.** Out of earshot, Mags explains herself: if they had refused, he would have gone and
done it alone; this way he is controlled and comes back. Her read on Greyface is **"some quite senior
fucker from [[the-bleeding-years|Thal'Mirrith]] who does terrifying dark magic."**

Then she puts the session's real question to the party. There are **thirty-four people** here —
**five young children** too small to fend for themselves, **two incoherent long-term survivors**, and
**one very old dog**. There were forty-two when they came down twelve years ago.

> *"The next time it dives, that's another twelve years. I don't think they'll be here. I think they'll
> all die, including the children. **I think we have to try.**"*

She casts **Augury** on whether [[the-provost|the Provost]] can give practically implementable advice
on reaching the surface, gets an encouraging answer, and goes to find out.

**Two people have already gone up.** The Provost is more troubled than usual: **Greyface asked for two
people, and she sent two** — **healthy young ones**, rather than the two incoherent survivors, because
she **did not want to disrespect the request**. It is too late for [[trevick|Trevick]] to catch them,
and Greyface could make the way harder if he chose. *"He does not kill without reason. He and I have an
understanding."* Told what that understanding has cost: **"If you knew what it was like to be down
here, you would count that mercifully."**

**Caspian comes back with both of them alive.** He looks sad and disappointed and faintly proud. The
village runs out to meet them.

> *"He asked too much of me and I could not give it. So we have left on even terms. I think he was
> perhaps more understanding than I thought he would be."*

He did not ask the way out.

**The Provost's account.** With the evacuation on the table she finally tells her own story — that when
she arrived Greyface **had gone a little mad** and was careless with people's lives, that she
**confronted him and made him see reason**, and that she thinks she **called him back to himself**.
*"There is a man there, not just a monster."* And why she came down at all: **"I came down here
looking for my husband, my son. I found neither."**

She gives them the routes. The way they came is gone; what is left are **paths that are more
contingent**. Over [[the-plain|the plain]] she does not rate — alone they would be picked off, *"but
together, maybe."* And there is another possibility: stories of **another intact area past
[[the-keep|the Keep]]**, in a **honeycomb of tunnels**, by which they might **pass under the plain
rather than over it**. She knows the start of those paths, and that **they lead up**.

She also names the problem. **"Much as we need Greyface — and have needed him these past twelve years —
he needs us too. I don't think he will willingly let us go. Not all of us, at any rate. I think he
must. Or give up whatever it is he is about."**

And then she agrees. *"A small chance of escape, or a decent chance of living another few years down
here… If they were here, and I had a chance to get them out, that's the choice I'd make for them…
Right. Let's see what kind of a plan we can make. It won't come again."*

**Lae'ethorian.** Casting back over everything, [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] puts two and two together.
Greyface never told them anything overtly and was careful throughout — but there is a figure who was
**a relative of [[the-bleeding-years|Archon Thaloriel]]**, part of his family, once **one of the
principal commanders of the Elvish armies**, who **disappeared about twelve years ago**. His name is
**Lae'ethorian**. [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] knows it instantly. West of the Stone March he is
remembered as a monster.

**The split.** The party cannot agree, and the session ends on it. [[mags|Mags]] wants to go now and
take everyone, and would hang back herself to buy time. [[caspian-talon|Caspian]] says they cannot
fight him, that the village realistically cannot be brought out, and that the party should **talk to
him rather than run across land he controls** — and will not say what he would do if that fails.
[[trevick|Trevick]] says **it is not the party's decision to make**: the villagers must choose for
themselves, though the children come first, and excluding Greyface guarantees most of them die.
[[rell-aetris|Rell]] is certain they must try, and proposes **offering Greyface access to the people of
[[the-keep|the Keep]]** instead. [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]] would save them, or failing that the
youngest.

What they agree on is the next move: go back to the Ossuary with the Provost, who insists on speaking
for the village herself, and **ask for everything**. See [[evacuate-dunton]].

## Key facts learned
- **[[the-high-priest|Greyface]] is alive and deliberately so.** For a necromancer of his skill the
  normal course would be to become undead — it makes the magic easier and blunts its effects — but it
  also **destroys the ability to think as a living person does**. He uses puppets instead, specifically
  **to remain in his mortal body**.
- **[[the-ossuary|The Ossuary]] is a temple to [[thalparaneth|Thalparaneth]]**, a god of **un-life** —
  not death, but the opposite of life — whose practice was **almost entirely lost** until **his kinsman
  rediscovered it**, taught it to others and began **a revival**. Greyface was **raised to it**.
- **Mags establishes the name is not among the ordinary Elvish names for the gods**, though it sounds
  Elvish.
- **He is in exile for losing an argument to his own family** about whether the **lost strands of
  magic** should be recovered and used. He fled fearing for his life, has **no standing any more**, and
  considers himself **a traitor** for even speaking to the party. He will not name his country.
- **He chose this place deliberately.** He had **heard of the Stonehunger** and **conducted or
  commissioned divinations to learn where and when it would next surface**. The timing at
  [[black-barrow|Black Barrow]] was not coincidence.
- **[[necromancy|Real necromancy]] feeds on the life energy of others and grows stronger with use** —
  unlike most magic, the practitioner gains from the practice. **It corrupts the body, breaks the mind,
  and severs bonds** — the capacity to see people as anything but instruments.
- **[[the-balance|The structure of magic]]:** Itharis at the centre, four opposed pairs around her,
  **nine in all**, which he regards as complete. Rell's magic is placed as **control and order**,
  opposite Rhynharran; Korrath's sits by Vraxyn, its principle unnamed; **the ninth, Sarasthanae, is broken**.
- **There are traces of a lost school of order-magic down here**, and Greyface says the party **may have
  seen them already**.
- **Caspian's "condition" is referred to a second time**, in front of the party, as something that
  might **protect him** from necromancy's cost.
- **[[the-provost|The Provost]] came down looking for her husband and son**, and found neither. It was
  she who **made Greyface see reason** when he had "gone a little mad."
- **Greyface asked for two living villagers and the Provost sent two healthy young ones.** Living
  requests are **rare now**; the dead go up as a matter of course. **[[caspian-talon|Caspian]] refused
  to kill them and brought both back alive.**
- **Dunton is thirty-four people**, including five small children and two incoherent survivors.
- **The way out may run under [[the-plain|the plain]]**, through a **honeycomb of tunnels past
  [[the-keep|the Keep]]**, where the Provost knows paths that **lead up**.
- **There are soldiers at the Keep**, which the villagers believe are **undead**, and **red-robed
  priests** come up from the lower levels.
- **Greyface is Lae'ethorian** — a relative of Archon Thaloriel and a vanished commander of the Elvish
  armies. Caspian worked it out; Greyface never said it, and does not know they know.
