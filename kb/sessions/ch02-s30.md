## Recap
At dawn on 30 Dusklin the party leaves [[greywatch|Greywatch]] through a small sally-port in the
southern wall, having spent the previous day quietly arranging two **unofficial guides** — a
persuasion that took party gold (largely [[fabian-perennius|Fabian]]'s) and a little pressure from
[[rell-aetris|Rell]]'s shadier contacts, since most guides refuse to cross [[the-mirrowen|the
Mirrowen]] during the [[shadows-meet|Shadows Meet]] festival. The lead guide is **[[nan-prentic|Nan
Prentic]]**, a dour, wiry woman of perhaps 55–60 ("Nan" is taken to be a title, not her name); her
junior is **[[cressan-weller|Cressan Weller]]**, a quiet, twitchy man of about thirty who inspires
little confidence but whom Nan trusts. A donkey, **[[lisa|Lisa]]**, carries their supplies.

The guides navigate the shifting land by memorised landmarks — cairns, rock shapes, distant peaks
lined up as waypoints — since, being unlicensed, they lack the guild's updated maps; Nan last came
this way about two and a half years ago. Pressed by Fabian, Nan calls the errand folly: Kethran's Fold
is "gone," "the land's taken it," and any survivors would have been found weeks ago. Cressan names the
cause the **[[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]]** — a thing that lives underground and swallows whole
settlements from below, leaving the land "still hungry" after — and repeats folk-tales of survivors
who "aren't right," of people who went back under the rubble, and of singing and knocking heard
underground days or weeks later. Nan dismisses all of it as superstition for an ordinary landslip:
"there's too much magic here," and one need only be watchful. Cressan, wary of the region's
identity-mimics, warns that if anyone returns to camp alone, "I know what to do."

The party makes camp in rocky uplands. [[mags|Mags]] sets a **Glyph of Warding** alarm at a
choke-point; watches are shared. On one watch [[rell-aetris|Rell]], with his excellent darkvision,
privately notices that Cressan can **see in the dark better than a human should** — and says nothing.
[[trevick|Trevick]] uses **Speak with Animals** on Lisa, who confirms Cressan is "strange" but that
"Nan likes him," and gives his history: Lisa and Nan once travelled with Cressan's mother; she died,
and Nan took Cressan in a few years later. Mags's attempt to build rapport with Cressan fails; he
wants only to be left alone. In the morning Trevick feeds Lisa an apple and tosses one to Cressan, who
catches it with startling reflexes but is baffled by the gesture.

A second day's travel brings the party, before dark, above **[[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]]** — a
steep, sharp fold in the land, wide enough to have held a village. The collapse reads from above as an
avalanche: a teardrop of loose stone spreading from a point high on the north slope down across the
buried village. Mags, an expert on rock, examines it and determines (high check) it was **not a
natural landslide** — at least some stone **welled up from beneath** rather than tumbling from above,
though it now superficially resembles a landslip. Cressan takes this as proof the Stonehunger struck
from below; Nan is uninterested and eager to leave.

The party picks its way down into the valley (Nan warning them off the unstable rubble) and examines
the slide's edge. There is **far less village detritus than there should be**: crushed building
material, not just valuables, has been **systematically removed**. Boot-scratches mark the rocks —
from **small, gnome-sized** feet, many of them, mostly weeks old with a few fresher — and among them
Fabian recovers, at Mags's direction, a small **silver-coloured nail that is actually very
high-quality steel**: too fine for the job, bearing no maker's mark, of a make impossible in the
Mirrowen and sourced by Fabian to great southern manufactories such as [[crownspire|Crownspire]]. It
has been crudely reused as a boot-grip. Caspian bluntly asks Mags whether her people scavenge such
sites; she rebuffs him, and the party notes the small feet could belong to many kinds of creature.

The tracks converge and lead down to a **deliberately opened gap** into the thick rubble near the old
village centre. Rell drops a light-cast ball-bearing to test it — it falls naturally through the
stone, **not a portal**. The party descends one at a time into a **created passage** (rocks shifted
and balanced, not tunnelled), comfortably sized for Mags but forcing the others to stoop or crawl. It
opens, unexpectedly, onto a **grand, decorative marble staircase** — broad as a formal entranceway,
finely made, of a style Fabian knows from grand buildings, its edges still buried and only a walkable
strip cleared. The party is judged to be **still above the level of the old village**, so the stair
belongs to something beneath or within the site. There are **no lamp niches**, from which Caspian
infers the excavators see in the dark.

The stair descends to a plain, barn-like **wooden door**, planked and metal-banded, with a lock but
poorly sealed. Fabian shoulders to the front to listen but hears nothing useful; Rell, listening, makes
out **voices** and the sounds of **digging** — stone shifted and broken, a hammer's tap — all coming
from **far below**, not just beyond the door. Rell checks the door with a mirror-on-a-stick and finds
no trap, alarm, or engaged lock; it can simply swing open. When the party extinguishes its lights, a
**faint, constant, pale-whitish glow** becomes visible behind the door — not bright enough to see by,
unrecognised by Caspian. The party debates how the daylight-sighted members (Caspian, Fabian) can
descend unseen. The session ends with Rell forming a two-hour telepathic link with the three staying
back and Rell and Trevick preparing to slip through the door — which they have not yet done.

## Key facts learned
- **The Kethran's Fold collapse was not natural** (Mags, expert reading, DM-confirmed): at least some
  stone welled up from below rather than falling from above. The landslip appearance is a consequence,
  not the origin. What caused the upheaval is not established.
- **The ruin has been deliberately, systematically excavated** (confirmed): material — not only
  valuables — has been removed; boot-scratch tracks from **small, gnome-sized** feet show many diggers
  (mostly weeks old, a few fresh); the tracks converge on a **deliberately opened passage** down.
- **A foreign high-quality steel nail** (confirmed anomaly): silver-coloured but true fine steel,
  unmakeable in the Mirrowen, sourced to southern manufactories / Crownspire, reused as a boot-grip —
  its presence here is unexplained.
- **A buried grand marble staircase** lies beneath/within the site, above the old village level —
  something grand was here before or beneath the village (confirmed; nature unknown).
- **Behind the buried door**: a faint, constant, pale-whitish **glow** (unrecognised), and from far
  below, **voices and digging**. Confirmed as heard/seen; their source is unknown.
- **The excavators see in the dark** (inferred from the absence of lamp niches on the decorative
  stair) and are of **small stature** (from the tracks) — but their identity is unknown.
- **The Stonehunger** (attributed folklore, per Cressan; disputed by Nan): a claimed underground
  hunger behind the region's village-swallowing collapses. Recorded as belief, not fact.
- **Rell's private observation** (confirmed as observed): Cressan can see in the dark better than an
  ordinary human. Meaning unestablished.
- **The Underdark is a separate plane** (DM worldbuilding): geographically overlapping the material
  world, accessed via apparent-underground; the Underdark beneath the Mirrowen is itself twisted (less
  than the surface); some cross the Mirrowen through it as a safer route. Planar **nativity** comes
  from exposure to a plane's ambient magic (Rell and Trevick are material-plane natives despite
  extraplanar heritage); long residence in a strongly-magical plane (Feywild, Korrath) naturalises a
  person, while the Underdark's weak ambient magic does not.
