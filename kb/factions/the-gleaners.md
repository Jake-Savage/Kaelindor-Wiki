## Overview
The **Gleaners** are a community of **goblins** the party encounters underground beneath
[[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]] (S2.31). They are markedly **paler** than ordinary goblins,
dress in a mismatch of scavenged things, and carry spears, bows, and distinctive **long
grappling-hook "fishing poles."** They live in the upper zone of the complex, which they call
**"the moor,"** and describe themselves as dwelling **inside their god** — an entity they call
simply **"God,"** which the party identifies with the folkloric [[the-stonehunger|Stonehunger]]
(the gleaner [[turley|Turley]] accepts the name as apt).

Their entire way of life is a **theology of scavenging**, related by [[turley|Turley]] (attributed
to her, not independently confirmed): when "God" takes a place from the surface, the "big bits go
below" to be consumed, but "many little bits need picking through," and "not all of it gets taken
right in." The gleaners' role is to go **above** after a kill and make sure everything of use "has
been made use of," and to pull the leavings up **from the river below** with their grapnel-poles
(per the DM's post-transcript account). By making themselves useful in this way — a contributing
part of the whole — they **earn "a place,"** so that when God next "goes below," they will **not be
crushed**. Those with no place are **"godless"** (their name for the party) and, they believe, are
simply "consumed, crushed... not in a way where they can become part of God, but just gone."

## Structure / ranks
- The gleaners work **"on the margins,"** never **"into the deep"** — "that's not our place." The
  deep beyond them (**Grommer's Path**, spelling uncertain) is where "the bigger folk" and the
  priests, wise men, and holy men who are **"closer to God"** dwell; the ordinary gleaner only
  **reads God's signs** to know when to get clear before God moves.
- Their local boss is **[[tuffin|Tuffin]]** (unseen), down by the **chasm/river** with the bulk of
  the workforce — "forty, fifty maybe" in his vicinity, a "dozen or two" working the river itself.
- They are **not** the complex's original builders — [[turley|Turley]]: "There were many folks here
  before us, but we've made it ours. God has seen fit to grant us a place."
- Beyond this place, gleaners number in the **"hundreds... here in the world."** They recognise the
  name of [[naelos|Naelos]] and other gods but dismiss them all as "made up"; "God," they insist,
  is the only real one ("God is obviously real").

## Relationship to the party
- **S2.31:** Caspian's familiar [[theodore|Theodore]] scouts two gleaners at rest in a dwarvish
  machine-room; the party puts them under [[rell-aetris|Rell]]'s twinned **Suggestion** and
  interrogates them (see [[turley|Turley]] and [[glorta|Glorta]]). Turley, oblivious that she is
  charmed, treats the party as helpless "godless" to be robbed and shown out — but grows
  uncharacteristically **interested** in them and agrees, reluctantly, to consider guiding them
  toward the [[the-custodian|custodian]] in exchange for cooking techniques. The gleaners confirm
  they **gleaned a recent "kill... a little way back,"** which the party understands to be
  [[kethrans-fold|Kethran's Fold]]. The party ultimately puts both gleaners to sleep to slip past
  the rest.

## DM notes
- The gleaners' account of "God" is **their theology**, voiced in-character; record it as belief
  corroborated by the party being physically inside a vast devouring thing (see
  [[the-stonehunger]]), not as settled metaphysics.
- **The gleaners are taken to be the S2.30 surface excavators.** Gleaning a kill's leavings from
  above is their whole trade, which fits the systematic removal of material found at the surface.
  One wrinkle the party notes: the two gleaners *seen* wear **soft leather shoes**, not the
  **spiked/nailed boots** (using **foreign high-quality steel**) that left the tracks, and that trail
  stopped at the marble stair — presumably the work of other gleaners. The identification is the
  party's working assumption; the **foreign steel's origin** is still unexplained.
- The gleaners live under a **clock**: Turley says God will "go below" in "a few weeks, a few days
  maybe, when we've finished" — i.e. the Stonehunger will move on (and crush the placeless) soon.
