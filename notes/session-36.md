# Session 36 - 22/09/2026

30 Dusklin, 1568. Late evening. The party is in the rooms at the top of the shattered carvings, in the ancient dwarven forge above the pool, intending to rest there before going deeper.

## The Forge

The rooms are the remnants of a working forge. Nothing here still functions and very little is recognisable as what it originally was, beyond the broadest outline.

A closer look establishes something not noticed on arrival: other people have camped here. There are the remains of fires lit on the floor and a scattering of low-value debris left behind by previous groups. It is not ancient. It is a matter of months and years rather than centuries. There is no sign of a struggle, no bloodstains, and nothing to suggest the campers left any way but tidily and of their own accord.

The complex at this level is three large rooms, large because a forge needed several smiths working at once.

- The western room is the one the party climbed up into, opening onto the shaft above the pool.
- The southern room holds an open archway onto a spiral staircase leading down.
- The northern room has a hatchway in its ceiling.

Several other doorways survive in the walls, but each is filled with rock that has been pressed forward into the opening and bulges slightly, the way a soft material bulges when something is pressed into it.

The spiral staircase does not match the rest. The masonry is less perfect, plainer and workmanlike, with none of the tiling or decoration of the temple forge around it. It looks as though it was taken from a building elsewhere and positioned here, rather than cut out of this rock.

## The Upper Chamber

Trevick searches what the previous campers left behind and finds nothing of use. He does find the hatchway, which appears to have been concealed at one time and is no longer, and the chamber above it.

The room is about twenty feet by twenty-five, much the smallest of them and by far the most ornately decorated. Its walls carry relief carvings of religious figures in a traditional dwarven style, recognisably Calvyran devotional work. Beneath them the stone is stained where incense has been burnt over a long period. These were not decorations. They were sites of prayer and were used as such.

Something has been taken out of the room recently. There are straight scrape marks across the floor where things have been dragged away from the walls, and they look relatively fresh. There are a few scraps of paper, indicating that scrolls or other written material were stored here and have since been removed. Trevick gathers the scraps and brings them down to Caspian, who examines them and finds nothing written on them at all. They are either magical, and have survived the age of the place, or they are much more recent than everything around them. Nobody establishes which, though it could be established.

## Settling for the Night

The party beds down in the upper chamber on Rell's point that it has only one entrance and is the most defensible room available. Caspian casts Leomund's Tiny Hut over them.

Mags adds a Glyph of Warding on the floor of the hut. She wants it to trigger on any hostile act committed inside the space, which takes some negotiating down to a workable definition of harm, and she loads it with a Guiding Bolt rather than a bare explosion. Having done so she declares herself an old woman, states that setting a landmine is contribution enough, and goes to sleep.

Rell sets up a line of bells and string in the room below the hatch. Trevick raises the possibility of pulling his three remaining crackers, partly on the grounds that he will otherwise forget he has them, and the rest of the party talks him out of it on the strength of what happened last time. They are saved for a safer location.

Watches are set: Caspian, then Trevick, then Rell, then Fabian.

## The Watches

Caspian spends his watch writing up notes on the place. He is confident enough in the hut that he does not realistically keep watch at all, and by his own account is not paying the attention he should. Nothing disturbs him. The glyph is not tripped.

Trevick takes the hatch open and leans against it. Partway through his watch he hears a pattering of gravel and small stones falling from the ceiling onto the stone below, and then, at intervals, one or two of Rell's bells beginning to chime softly. It is not the sound of something tripping over them.

Looking down into the northern room, he sees that its floor is networked with a fine tracery of cracks. It was pristine when the party came up. From the same vantage he can hear heavier sounds of stone grinding and crunching from the western room, which he cannot see into.

He summons Hawk and sends him through with a prearranged signal: one squawk if the western room looks like the one below, two if there is a physical entity in it. Hawk returns and squawks twice.

Trevick then goes down himself, picking his way over Rell's strings without disturbing them, and looks out through the archway.

The western room is worse. The cracks there are substantial, and at the far side, where the party climbed up, the wall is visibly moving, with the cracks spreading outward from that movement. Several blocks have already been pushed well out of place.

At the broken lip of the carvings, where the jagged edges are, something looks wrong inside the stone. There is a crust of stone over it, and beneath that a whitish, almost gelatinous substance that is moving. It is coming up through the stone and pushing the stone aside. It looks alive.

Trevick looks for a separate creature within the rock and does not find one. The impression is that the stone is a kind of skin on the thing, and that its skin cracks open as it moves and adapts beneath. He knows of nothing like it, though he is well aware that things can look stony and be alive.

He wakes Rell rather than the whole camp. The two of them watch it together and agree a threshold: if it gets beyond the chamber below and into the ones above, that is the point at which their options start closing and they move. Rell's position is that it is alarming but slow, and that every watch from here should keep a careful eye on it.

Trevick sits out the remainder of his watch openly caught between not wanting to alert the thing and wanting to touch it. By the end of it he is dusty with stone falling from the ceiling, and the bulges of white substance have begun surfacing and subsiding like something breathing.

## The Collapse

Rell takes his watch down in the forge itself rather than above it, assembling a small campfire near the edge of the chamber on the reasoning that most animals fear fire. He takes down the bells, which have been ringing too often to be useful.

Less than half an hour in, one wall of the western room collapses inward with a very loud noise, waking everyone. For a moment a large patch of the white material is exposed; it begins crusting over with new stonework almost at once.

Rell reports it to the others as some kind of organism slowly consuming the stonework, which dislikes the stone that has been put in place, shoves it out of its way, and hardens its own flesh to replace it. His assumption, stated as an assumption, is that this is the Stonehunger's physical form.

The party argues the situation out.

- Caspian presses hardest for leaving. The room they are in may be safe, but if every other room is destroyed, this is simply the room they die in.
- Rell's position is that it is progressing slowly, that they might endure a little longer, and that the chambers beyond may prove more dangerous than waiting.
- Fabian raises the one attempt at an explanation anyone offers. This room alone shows no cracking, no dust and no movement, and it is also the only room with the devotional carvings in it. He reads that as protection, drawing the comparison to the warded dragon statue in the golden courtyard. The architecture is otherwise no different from the rest of the complex.

They compromise: carry on a little longer, and reassess at the change of watch. It does not hold. Shortly afterwards the other side of the western room comes down in a cloud of dust, leaving the route back to the pool passable only with great difficulty and about to become impassable. The northern room then begins the same process, its floor tiles buckling upward from below and blocks starting out of the walls. Rell raises the alarm and the party moves.

## Studying the Substance

Before leaving, Rell makes a study of an exposed patch in the southern room, where a long crack has opened along most of the length of a wall.

- Looked at closely it is whitish, with a faintly pearlescent sheen, and mostly smooth-surfaced. When it pushes outward the smooth skin splits and something more liquid and elastic shows through. Once it has been still for a minute or two, a stony skin forms over the exposed part again.
- The stony skin is dry to the touch. The exposed moving part is warm and sticky, and traces of it come away on his fingers.
- He takes a smear onto a swatch of leather and seals it in an empty potion vial.
- Heat brought progressively closer produces no reaction at all. Brought into contact, it fizzles, the thing's movement quickens, and the substance spreads in a thin layer over the torch and puts it out.

Mags's reading is that smothering a flame implies intentionality. Rell's is that it could be purely reactive. Neither is settled. The general level of disturbance in that part of the complex increases noticeably afterwards, and Fabian's contribution is to tell him to stop annoying it.

Rell then casts Detect Thoughts on it, searching only for whether there is intelligence there at all, rather than for anything he could read.

He gets a brief brush against it and pulls back immediately. There is certainly something there that could be described as a mind. It is extremely strange, and it is vast and old and not like anything else he has encountered. He can make out no detail about it, and he is left in no doubt that attempting a deeper connection would be a very bad idea. He withdraws with no desire to meddle further, and relates it to the party once they have assembled.

Caspian's response is that if it were not currently eating everything around them, he would like to study it properly.

## The Staircase

The party has had perhaps five hours of broken sleep and nothing like the night it needed. It goes down the spiral staircase from the southern room, with the sounds of continued destruction above it all the way.

The staircase descends a long way, and its style changes at points. It is not one original staircase but several, similar enough to be pitched together and made to serve.

It ends at what appears to be a dead end: a flat wall of raw, uncarved stone, with no evidence of collapse. Tapping it produces no hollow sound. There is no writing, no symbols and no pressure pads.

What Caspian does find is subtler. Only hours earlier the party watched the elemental that controls access to Grommar's Path open and close its gateway, shaping and reshaping the rock to do it, and leaving a distinctive rippling in the stone. The same type of patterning is here, very faint. The same kind of movement has happened to this stone at some point. He points it out to the party.

## The Voice

Mags knocks on the wall and calls out for whoever is there to come and say hello.

A voice answers. It does not sound like the elemental they met at the gate, and it does not sound as though it is coming from within or behind the stone at all. It is clear and bright and seems to hang in the air above them, perhaps a little further back up the stairs. Nothing is visible.

It says: well, well, visitors, you are most welcome. Give me a second.

Several minutes pass and nothing happens. Fabian raps on the wall in a rhythm and observes that where he comes from one is not accustomed to leave visitors waiting at the door. The party adds that they bring news of the outside world.

Then the stone begins to move under Fabian's hand, and the voice speaks again, less declamatory this time.

- "That wasn't so hard. I knew you could do it with the proper incentive."

The stone parts slowly and with evident effort into a plain gateway, much less grand than the arch that admitted them to Grommar's Path. The voice comes more sharply.

- "Come now, you can do better than that."

The gateway widens, and takes on something of the grandeur of the earlier arch, with simulated columns at each side and an arched top. Neither remark is obviously addressed to the party, and who the voice is speaking to is not established.

## The Arena

Beyond is the first lit place the party has found down here. Bright torches burn in braziers around the edge of what is plainly an amphitheatre or arena.

- It is patched together out of several different buildings. Roughly seventy per cent of it is one structure providing the main substance of the place; the remaining thirty per cent is odds and ends collected elsewhere and added to make it grander. Statuary has been crammed in. Not all the seating matches.
- Banners hang around the edges. They are not old and not threadbare, which sits oddly against everything else down here.
- A mosaic of a bull is set into the floor. Nobody recognises the iconography, and Mags is warned off dredging her memory of heraldry for it on the grounds that it would only mislead her.
- A pit opens in the middle of the floor. A grill covered it once; what is left of it is rusted fragments around the rim, most of which appear to have simply crumbled and fallen in, though parts could have been bent by force. The mouth of the pit is probably wider now than it was built to be.
- Above the arena walls there is a rock ceiling, and in places the rock bulges over the top of the structure as though it had flowed across it and settled, cradling the thing. There is no sign of it being active or moving now. Fabian's reading is that the arena may once have stood on the surface and been gone over.
- The floor is muddy and wet, with an unusual oily sheen to it.

The stone door closes behind them. Fabian thanks the voice for letting them through, addresses it as Mr. Voice in the Wall, and asks whether there is anyone here to talk to. There is no reply.

Caspian examines the ground without stepping onto it, trying to establish whether it is mud that will swallow them, oil that will burn, or something else. His conclusion is that the oiliness looks more biological than geological, and that there is something lurking below the mud. He tells the party not to go in there and steps back.

The party then establishes the shape of the trap it is in:

- Mags walks toward the edge of the space and is stopped by an invisible field running along the line of the torches. It is smooth to the touch, entirely invisible, and can be seen through without difficulty. She confirms the same field exists on the opposite side.
- Fabian plays flute notes at it to no discernible result. Anything with a visible component — a Firebolt, a Prestidigitation — splashes off it as though it were a physical wall.
- Tested from several positions, the field encompasses the entire arena, including the staircase out on the far side.
- Mags sends a rabbit across the mud with Minor Illusion. Nothing reacts to it.
- Caspian throws a stone onto the mud while readying a spell. It sinks, which is what a stone would do. Mags, watching closely, sees that the mud rose slightly to meet it, and that it was more engulfed than sunk.

The only way on is through the mud, and the sounds from the pit are getting closer.

Caspian casts Haste on Trevick, on the grounds that he is the only one of them not using magic. Rell uses his scroll of Blink. Fabian puts on the Dragon Crown in case it sharpens his casting. Mags calls down the pit with Speak with Animals and gets no answer.

## The Minotaur

What climbs out is a minotaur, and a very large one, perhaps ten to twelve feet tall. Minotaurs are not common creatures, but they are known to exist and everyone present recognises it.

It is very clearly undead. It carries no weapon and fights with unusually long, sharp horns and claws, lowering its head to charge. Rell judges from the state of its decay that it is essentially mindless, and is right.

Fabian opens by recalling that griffins are infamously the natural rival of the minotaur and conjuring one with Phantasmal Force. The illusion cannot hurt the creature, which has too little mind left in it to be hurt that way, but it can change how it behaves, and Fabian works it for the rest of the fight to keep the thing running at something other than the party.

The fight is slow and unrewarding.

- The minotaur charges Mags. The gore bounces off her; the claw that follows opens her badly. Its second claw goes into the illusory griffin.
- Mags raises Naelos against it. It holds against her, and the light that follows does it no harm at all.
- Rell strikes it with fire and then steps into the mud, where something takes hold of his legs and holds him fast.
- Trevick, hasted, marks it and shoots three times, and sends Hawk in on flybys. Between them they do very little.
- Caspian abandons a plan that depends on the creature's weight once it is established that it outweighs a grand piano, and casts Shatter instead. This drives several more of the creatures beneath the mud briefly to the surface behind the minotaur. They are a form of slime, flat and featureless, without faces, much like the flat things that live on a seabed. They are not undead. They burrow back down.
- Fabian closes and stabs at its face, missing, and tells it to stay back or be struck properly next time. It turns on him.
- Mags examines the thing embedded in its chest and spends a turn on a Thorn Whip trying to drag it out. It does not budge.

The thing in the minotaur's chest is a very large axe of dwarven design, buried in the matted fur and caught in the ribcage, and lodged tightly. The undead skin has grown closed around it, so it is not an open wound. Mags reads it as a holy object, and Calvyran. It is a double-handed weapon, sized for a strong warrior, and she could not possibly wield it. Asked whether it appears to be hurting the creature, she is told that it looks angry and in pain — with the caveat that the party has been hitting it, and the causality cannot be read from outside.

The slime holding Rell drags him down and works to smother him. He kills it with Shadow Blade, and it bursts as it dies, spattering him with something that burns, which frees him. Throughout all of this the minotaur walks through the mud entirely unmolested. It pays the slimes no mind and they pay it none.

Mags raises Naelos against it a second time, and this time it takes. The light burns it, and the creature is left frightened and helpless for a minute, moving as far from her as it can get.

Trevick draws on it, works out for himself that an arrow would break the effect, and lowers the bow. The party holds its fire by consensus. Mags argues for letting it go: her reading is that beating the thing is what will earn them a conversation with whoever spoke at the door, and that the creature will show them a way out one way or another, whether by going back down its hole or by finding something more interesting to headbutt. She raises the possibility of making the hole hard for it to climb back out of.

It runs for the pit and goes back down it.

The party has a minute. Caspian says he has a plan and does not say what it is.
