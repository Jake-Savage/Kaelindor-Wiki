# Session 42 - 03/11/2026

The bones have gone. Their clatter is still rolling away across the plain, fainter by the minute, and in the stillness it leaves behind the convoy is a ruin of crying, bandaging and counting. Mags's Daylight still blazes over them all, a small white sun on the stone, and it is that light that shows them what is coming next. Out of the north-west, spreading as they come, a patrol is walking towards them. There are a couple of minutes, perhaps, before it arrives.

## Making Ready

There is no fortress to retreat to, only the rocks. To the south two great boulders lean together over a crevice, and the children are bundled into it: the toddlers carried, the older ones led, Marianne Swithun with baby Caelum on her hip, and little Daisy Thorngage, who has nobody left, pushed in beside them to be their sister for the night. Old Mothrigg Tosscobble plants himself at the mouth of it with a club in his fist. Max the spaniel is set to watch the other flank, slower on his old legs than the old people. The adults who can hold a bow take one; the rest press themselves into the shadows of the rock.

One of them does not hide. Kithri Munn, who stitched banners for Greyface and whose son Ned went into the swarm an hour ago, walks out to the front with a bow and takes her place beside the party.

Fabian turns to Mags. They took down a storm of bones tonight, he tells her, and these people have to see them be brave, and so they are going to be heroic. The Heroism he lays on her settles like armour. He tells Rell to do the cool thing again. Rell has already called up his mirror images, and they shift about him in the white light.

## Seven

The patrol comes on in a ragged line, seven of them, the last their commander. Some carry heavy crossbows, maces and nets; some carry those and something more, a mark of magic on them; some carry nothing but the magic. The first raises a gauntleted left hand at Fabian and a bolt of death spits from it, and Fabian twists aside. Another runs a hand down his crossbow and it catches in black flame.

Mags looks them over with open contempt. Big men, she tells them, coming armed against unarmed men and women and little children, and she brings Bane down on them in the name of Naelos. Two of the three stagger under it. She dares them to come at her instead. They are not listening. Kithri looses her arrow at the nearest of them and it strikes home, and blood runs.

Then one of them throws a bolt of lightning straight down the party's line, and it cracks through Mags and Rell and the rest like a whip.

## Fire

Caspian waits. He crouches behind his rock while the patrol drifts in from the dark, counting them as they come, and when five of them are close enough together he rises and opens his hand. The Fireball lands in the middle of them and blooms: a roaring orange flower that swallows all five at once, lights the plain for a hundred yards, and leaves them smoking.

Then the commander walks onto the field, looks at what has been done to his men, and answers in kind.

The second Fireball bursts over the party's own heads. For one long moment there is nothing but fire and noise. When it clears, Mags is down, Fabian is down, and Kithri is lying on the stone. A patrolman walks up, levels his crossbow at the woman on the ground, and puts a bolt into her. That is the end of Kithri Munn.

## On the Ground

The patrol presses in. Trevick takes a crossbow bolt and then a mace and keeps shooting. Caspian slips round a patrolman, throws up a shield of force against the blow that follows him, and kneels to pour a healing potion down Mags's throat. She opens her eyes long enough to tell the nearest of them what she thinks of his face. Rell drags himself to his feet and goes in with his blades.

The commander spreads his hands and the air turns to treacle: Caspian and Trevick slow to a crawl, every movement dragged through syrup. A patrolman's mace comes down on Mags's head and she goes down again.

And then Fabian, lying on the stone with fire still in his clothes, simply opens his eyes. He does not know why. He realises he has not eaten in ages, reaches into his pocket, and eats a slab of fruit cake, which turns out to heal like the finest of potions. Then he crawls to Mags and whispers her back to her feet with a Healing Word. Did she just eat his cake, he wonders. It was bloody tasty. They will need more.

Mags raises the symbol of Naelos, and healing pours out of it into Caspian, into Fabian and into Rell.

## The Villagers

One of the casters, the one who threw the lightning, has walked straight into the villagers' rocks. He finds that they bite.

Maddie Pruett, Roscoe's mother, fifty-four and nobody's soldier, steps out and shoots him, then steps back. When he comes on she throws herself at him bodily to keep him off the children, and misses, and does not stop trying. Finnan Highcroft puts an arrow in him. Then Rosie Tryn, eleven years old and armed with a dagger, slips round behind him while he is busy and stabs him in the backside.

Del Zoric has spent the fight cowering, a small voice in his head telling him to stay down. Now he remembers himself, stands, and shoots, and misses, and shoots again, and misses, and keeps on missing, with tears running down his face, and keeps on shooting anyway. Olo Halfkettle, refusing to be outdone by a child, swings his club and misses. Old Bohdan Tarek wades in and cracks the caster over the head. And Max, who has been crouched all this time, waiting for someone to come too close, goes for the robes. He gets hold of the hem and worries it, growling, dragging the caster half off his feet.

The caster has had enough. He puts his hands together and the stone itself shrieks: a Shatter bursting out from where he stands, through himself, through Rosie and Bohdan and Maddie and Olo. Rosie is thrown down, bleeding. Bohdan and Maddie reel. Not one of them dies.

Trevick's arrow takes the caster through the body, and he falls. Max, still with a mouthful of robe, is in no doubt that he did it.

## Laughter

Fabian, back on his feet, strums a chord on the golden lute and starts talking. Isn't it funny, he asks the patrol, that the experts of the deep cannot lay a finger on a dandy like him, while a little girl can stab them practically straight out of the womb? It is funnier than it ought to be. One patrolman begins to laugh, and cannot stop. So does the commander. He laughs until his legs go and he is rolling on the stone, helpless, and his spell falls away from Caspian and Trevick.

Mags lifts the symbol of Naelos again, and this time the power goes out of it like a wall. These things are not living men. Two of them break and flee before it, burning as they go. She knows better than to throw the cold magic of death at them. It would do nothing to them at all.

Rell is everywhere: blades in close, blades thrown across thirty feet of stone, one patrolman after another going down under them. A turned man running from Mags gets a knife in the back as he goes. The other keeps running, out across the plain, and nobody catches him. Caspian, beaten down at last by a mace, is lying among the rocks until Mags reaches him. Fabian stands between the last of the patrol and the villagers, sheathes his sword, takes up the lute, and tells a patrolman to his face that he could not hit an eleven-year-old.

## The Last One

Fabian looks at that last patrolman properly and sees a man who wants very much to be somewhere else. He keeps glancing over at his commander, who is rolling about on the ground in hysterics and paying him no attention whatever, and he does not have the authority to call off the fight. He believes the commander would, if only he could stop laughing. Trevick puts an arrow in him before the question can be settled.

## The Prisoner

That leaves the commander, still gasping with laughter on the stone. Rell kneels over him and binds his fingers together so that he cannot shape a spell, and gags him so that he cannot speak one. When the patrol wanted to leave, it was to this man that the others looked.

## After

The villagers have lost one more, and they are sad for Kithri. Nineteen of them are left, and the dog. She stood in the front line, she drew blood, and she died there. But they are steadier than they were an hour ago. They have fought, and they have mostly won. Fabian sees to them. He promises old Bohdan a sausage when they are out of here.

Trevick wonders whether they could make camp. Rell will not hear of it: one of the patrol got away.

The plan is to ask the prisoner a few questions and then go on across the plain in the same direction. How do they get out of this hellhole? Is there a secret way? Will more come after them? Rell means to put the questions mind to mind, so that the man never has to open his mouth. And he will understand them, after a fashion, because he speaks Draconic, though not the Draconic of the world above. It is something older, close enough for plain questions, likely to drift on anything finer. When Fabian sets the dragon crown on his head, the prisoner, still not recovered, gives him his whole attention.
