# Session 34 - 01/09/2026

30 Dusklin, 1568. The party is a short way inside Grommar's Path, the stone gate sealed behind them. The passage is unlit and slopes gently downward. Fabian takes out a hooded lantern rather than casting Light, on the grounds that a narrow beam will not spoil the darkvision of the rest of the party.

## The Corridor of Saints

Mags and Caspian go ahead about twenty feet to look at the first carving on the wall. It is a bas-relief some eight to ten feet wide and six high, stylised, of a dwarvish figure holding a flask with vapour rising from it and a second flask on a table before him.

Mags identifies it. In dwarvish Calvyr worship there are seven classical saints, all of them either real people or supposedly real people, each an exemplar of a particular craft, and all ancient by the standards of the present era. This is Orvan Smokeglass, the alchemist among the seven. The corridor carries a relief for each of the seven; the party stops to identify five of them. It learns the following about the corridor as it goes:

- The carvings alternate from side to side of the corridor, roughly every ten to fifteen feet, each saint shown in a workshop surrounded by the typical symbols and tools of the craft. They are reverent portraits, plainly intended as devotional works.
- They are not in the style of the engravings at the complex's entrance above. The two are separate work.
- Metal in the corridor is uncorroded, which in a dwarven temple is ordinary rather than remarkable: religious spaces are built with metals treated against corrosion.

Mags raises the possibility of dating the temple by which of the seven saints are present, on the reasoning that a depiction predating a saint's canonisation would have to omit him. The point is granted as workable in principle and never pursued.

Rell interrupts to observe that this is Grommar's Path, said to be an endless trial likely to end in their deaths, and to suggest a slower pace and a check for traps. He is immediately vindicated. Along the side of Smokeglass's carving are a row of very small holes with wires inside them. The trap has been triggered, recently, and to judge by the fresh scrapes on the stone it was triggered by Mags and Caspian walking up to the carving. Its mechanism is no longer operable. Rell cannot determine what the party did to set it off, nor what it was meant to deliver, though from the size of the holes he judges it was needles or a gas.

The remainder of the corridor is a sequence of traps, and the party's progress through it is the substance of the session.

## The Hammer

Rell, moving ahead slowly and watching the floor, finds a patch that has been broken and mended more than once, and mended with more subtlety than the repaired stone the party saw upstairs. Some kind of impact has repeatedly happened here. He infers that whatever falls must retract, or there would be no need for repeated repair.

The party attempts to trigger it from a distance. Mags proposes throwing a piece of hard cheese, which is judged too light; Trevick swings Mags's staff at the spot on the strength of his reach, without result. They edge around the patch instead, one at a time, and then trigger it deliberately from the far side: Rell leans his weight on the staff at the edge of the plate, and, once Trevick and Fabian add theirs, it goes off.

A large, heavy, war-hammer-shaped weight falls from the ceiling on a chain. It crushes Mags's staff, which is destroyed, and puts deep cracks in the floor without breaking through it. Anyone standing there would have been badly hurt. It does not retract.

Rell climbs onto the hammer and up the chain to look at the space it came from. There is no elaborate machinery, only a gear the chain winds around and a space large enough for a person to occupy, cramped. Mags had been hoping for a service passage running past the traps, on the reasoning that Calvyrans believe in repair and would have built a sensible maintenance protocol into the place. There is no such passage.

## The Spikes

Trevick and Rell search the next stretch together, Trevick taking the high line and Rell the low. They find a narrow rectangular opening in the ceiling ahead, with a row of small impact points in the floor beneath it about eight inches apart, and, ten to fifteen feet beyond that, two very slender slits in the walls on either side running from the ceiling down to about two feet above the floor.

The spike line proves easy to cross: it is a single row, not a spread, and can simply be jumped. Rell marks the safe standing spot with a chalk X between his feet before jumping, so the others can place themselves exactly, and calls back instructions. The whole party clears it and bunches up in the gap between the two traps.

## Saint Vero and the Axe

The next relief is of a dwarf whose eyes are picked out in green gems. It is the one carving in the corridor decorated beyond the small gold accents of the others. This is Saint Vero, the Calvyr saint of jewels, lenses and glassware, and of the precision grinding, cutting and faceting those crafts require. Mags is relatively familiar with this saint through the trade in diamonds of different sorts for different divine spells.

Rell asks whether there are any objections to prising the eyes out. Caspian doubts dwarven craft makes gems easy to steal. Mags objects on principle to despoiling temples, though she notes no one has seen this one for a very long time. Rell answers that this temple has been eaten and is no longer functioning as a temple. Fabian suggests the gems are being saved from the next time it is eaten, and the matter is settled with a nominal agreement to donate them to a historical preservation society.

Rell moves in to the side of the carving, judging he can reach the eyes without standing in the projection line of the wall slits, and prises them out. The work is not delicate, and the gems are scratched in the process. They are not one gem per eye but a setting of several: the party recovers a handful of good emeralds and one stone of a darker, purplish colour that nobody can identify.

The trap springs. Two portcullises crash down about twenty feet apart, sealing the whole party between them, and a very large, slender, axe-shaped blade swings out of one wall slit, sweeps across the corridor at floor level and vanishes into the other. It repeats every thirty to forty seconds. The portcullises are grated rather than solid, with gaps large enough for an arm but not a head, and cannot be lifted by hand.

The party's responses run in parallel:

- Trevick tests whether the grate will lift, and it will not. Checking the far side of the mural for a matching slit, he finds none, but notices something else: small rectangular gaps have opened at floor level along the whole section of corridor, where some of the floor stones have sunk slightly. He is confident they were not there before. He watches the floor over subsequent swings to see whether the axe is causing it, and cannot tell. The gaps are never explained.
- Mags reads the swings as building towards something rather than simply repeating, and holds an action to stop the blade with Thorn Whip, which can move a large creature ten feet. Fabian offers to back this with a Shield spell, and the plan is set aside when it becomes clear the caster would have to stand in the path of the axe.
- Rell ignores the discussion and builds a pulley. He hammers a piton into the wall on one side of the axe's arc, ties a rope to the bottom of the far portcullis, runs it up through a loop at the piton and across the corridor, and fixes the loose end to a second piton on the other side of the arc, so the blade must strike the taut rope mid-swing and the momentum can only pull the portcullis end.

It works on the sixth swing of the axe. The blade snags the rope, its momentum is arrested short, and the far portcullis rises far enough for the party to duck and roll under it. Fabian, moving fastest, scoops up Mags, who had committed herself to the Thorn Whip and is slow to break off. Trevick matches him. Everyone gets through.

The axe had been building toward something rather than simply repeating, and the party stopped it in the nick of time: on the seventh swing it would have begun to do whatever it was intended to do.

## Saint Sargrim Hexward

The next relief is Sargrim Hexward, the runecrafter of the seven, known for being equally proficient with curses and with wards against curses. He is conventionally depicted with five runes: two for his hex-setting, two for his ward-setting, and one representing his keeping of the two forces in balance. Which particular runes appear varies between depictions, and a carver may or may not trouble to make the depicted runes genuinely potent. Caspian establishes that this carver did, and that the runes would be worth money and possibly useful if chiselled out.

The party declines to take them. Rell argues there is no time to chisel them out intact, and that gems are easily liquidated where ancient runes are not. Mags declines on the grounds that removing the gems did not make axes swing out of the walls and this might. Caspian notes the inconsistency and lets it go, and the runes are deferred to the way out.

Searching the ground in front of the carving, Rell finds that some but not all of the stones of the floor, and of the wall beneath the relief up to the level of the carving itself, carry very faint marks. They are not cut into the stone; they look more like shadows on it, and read as the trace effect of magic having been cast upon them. He can see a path between them. Rather than talk the party through it, he chalks the route out as he goes. Two of them badly misplace their footing on the way across; the chalked route carries them through regardless.

## The Inscription

The last relief the party examines is of the saint of brewing, shown with vats and tankards. He is the one of the seven most celebrated outside dwarven culture, and is generally taken as a symbol of hospitality and respite. The party finds no trap at this carving.

Cut into the floor beside it is a line of Dwarvish, which Rell reads. It does not match the coherence of the rest of the corridor's work and was carved after it. It is not graffiti scratched in haste: it has been cut with care and attention, and took time.

---

Khazar Vuk Varag — I defy you.

---

Khazar Vuk Varag is presented by every linguistic marker as a proper name, in three parts. It means something on the order of ancient hatred, the oldest of hatreds, or oldest enemy. The words that follow are addressed to it.

The party establishes the following:

- Anyone meeting this line in a text about Grommar, whose classic adversary was death, would almost certainly interpret it as a reference to death.
- It is nonetheless not a traditional dwarvish way of referring to death, and not a Calvyran way either. The reading holds because of Grommar, not because of the word.
- Mags's knowledge of religion turns up no tradition, adversary or entity carrying that name.

Rell's reading is that this is Grommar himself, having come through the sequence of traps intended to kill him, recording that he has defied death at the end of the run. He supports it with a separate observation: the gleaners know Grommar's name, while knowing nothing about any of the other structures inside the Stonehunger, which he takes to imply that Grommar was actually here and they got the name from him.

Mags presses two objections and neither is answered. The first is that "oldest adversary" is not the same claim as "death", and that the party is supplying the equivalence from the Grommar legend rather than reading it off the stone. The second is that a private act of defiance does not need to be patiently carved where others will read it: whoever cut this wanted an inference drawn from it.

On whether trapping the saint of hospitality would be improper, the party is told that it would traditionally be considered poor form, and that on the stories they know Grommar was insane by the end of his life, so how far he felt such constraints applied to him is not a question the tradition settles.

## The Air

The smell worsens steadily as the corridor descends. Mags checks it twice and gets the same answer both times: there is nothing acutely dangerous in it, only a mixture of unpleasant things, though it is not air anyone would want to breathe over a protracted period.

Rell tests it with a flame and finds a faint bluish tinge around its edges, from which he infers a natural build-up of methane, worsening with depth. He has Fabian douse the lantern flame in favour of magical light, and advises the party to watch for laboured breathing or muddled thinking as early signs. Nothing comes of it during the session. Further down, the party begins to hear water: not running water, but an irregular slap, every now and again.

## The Pool

The corridor ends in a right-angle turn to the right and a flight of stone steps descending about fifty feet in an irregular, almost square-spiral. Caspian sends Theo down to scout, and the familiar returns the shape of the chamber below.

The steps open into a large, initially natural chamber holding a pit of water roughly seventy feet across. A ledge runs the whole way around it, wide enough for one person and awkward for two, damaged in places where sections have fallen away, and about thirty feet above the surface. The water is filthy: matter floating in it, oily patches, gas bubbling up irregularly, which accounts for the sound. The chamber is warm and the water itself is giving off heat.

About a third of the way around the ledge to the left of the stairs is a smooth strip of wall some fifteen feet wide, carved, running all the way down into the water and all the way up into the ceiling and beyond it, like a chimney. The carvings continue up out of sight. The party establishes:

- They are in very much the same style as the saints' reliefs in the corridor, but by a different hand.
- They are marred by a great many narrow openings of the same sort as the corridor's trap-ports, added after the carving was done.
- The strip is sheltered by a canopy: four stone pillars rising from projections of the ledge, carrying a sloping roof over the upper part of the carving. The canopy is the work of whoever built the chamber, not of what now lives under it.
- It is the only apparent way on. An exit may lie at the top of the carving, above the canopy roof, and this cannot be confirmed from the stairs.

Dark shapes are roosting in the ceiling beneath the canopy roof, irregular and lumpy across the whole span of it, and not moving.

## The Spiders and the Water

The party spends a considerable time on the stairs rather than on the ledge, probing the chamber at range. The order of what they learn:

Caspian sends Theo under the canopy. Something comes at the familiar almost at once and Caspian dismisses him a moment before it arrives. What came was arachnid, and moved with the clatter of metal rather than the scritch of legs: these are spiders either fully construct or very significantly modified by artifice. They are large for spiders and not large for a threat, roughly the size of a small to medium dog. They attacked on sight, they are very fast, and they descend not by scuttling but by letting themselves down on what appears to be a steel cable. There are no webs anywhere in the chamber.

Rell reads the absence of webs and the immediate pounce together as confirmation that they are not biological: a living spider would stalk rather than rush, and would have webbed the place.

Fabian probes the ledge with an unseen servant, which can be sent about sixty feet from him. It walks two-thirds of the way round making noise, and nothing stirs. It waves a pebble, and nothing stirs, and then throws the pebble hard enough that it skitters along the ledge and drops into the water.

Trevick notices a shift in the patterns on the surface, subtle, and beyond what the falling stone accounts for. The party tests this by having the unseen servant drop rations into the water along an arc, fifty feet in each direction, and the result is consistent: the movement follows where things are dropped, concentrating near them, spread over a wide area but not the whole pool. Nothing is ever seen. The party cannot agree among themselves where in the water it is, and Mags asks directly whether the disagreement indicates that some of them are being magically compromised. It does not: the movement is simply difficult to read.

Mags raises the possibility that Calvyran automata would have an authentication protocol, some way for one of the faithful to indicate they are not hostile. Asked directly, she confirms this is her own reasoning from what automata are rather than anything her character knows about these ones. Rell counters that the place has the character of a sealed tomb, shut with a dead man inside it and never intended to be entered again, in which case the constructs may simply be set to attack everything indefinitely.

Rell then proposes a test. If the spiders are powered by magic, their perception is plausibly some form of divination magic, in which case his amulet of proof against detection may hide him from it. Fabian, thinking it through, can say only that nobody knows how these automata are constructed, and that the theory is intuitively sound. Caspian raises the obvious problem, which is that if the spiders sense by sight as well as by magic the test will be answered by being attacked, and Rell accepts it, holding a casting of Invisibility in reserve.

Rell steps off the stairs onto the ledge, moving carefully to avoid disturbing the water as well as the spiders. Mags holds a cantrip to attack anything that attacks him.

The rest of the staircase immediately snaps flat into a steep slope, and everyone still standing on it shoots down it and over the edge into the pool, a drop of about thirty feet. Rell keeps his footing. He reaches out as the party goes past and catches at Mags, and does not manage to hold her, though he is not dragged in after her either. The party takes minor damage from the fall and ends the session in the water, with the ledge overhanging above them at most points, the carving the easiest place to climb out, and the spiders nesting over the carving.
