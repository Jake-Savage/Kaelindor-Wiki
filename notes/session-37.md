# Session 37 - 29/09/2026

The early hours of the morning. The party is sealed inside the buried arena, the undead minotaur is cowering in the pit where Mags drove it, and the fear holding it there will not last much longer.

## The Arena

The party uses the time to get into position rather than to attack.

Rell picks his way across the mud on the dry ground he mapped the night before, careful to avoid the wet patches where the slimes lie, and looks down into the pit. He reports back:

- It is around fifteen to twenty feet deep, with enough stones protruding from the sides to climb up and down easily. The minotaur has plainly been doing exactly that.
- A space opens off the northern side, but it is a den rather than a corridor. It cannot run far, because the creature would have fled down it rather than stopping if it did.
- There is no way out of the arena through the pit.

He then studies the far wall at length, looking for buttons, switches, levers, runes, glyphs or sigils, and deliberately touching nothing. Nothing shows. His conclusion sets the party's course for the night: he sees no mechanism and no way forward, killing the beast is probably their route out, and whatever spoke to them at the door is looking for entertainment.

Caspian passes his Haste to Fabian. The party rings the pit and waits.

## Killing the Minotaur

Caspian opens with Shatter directed down into the pit. It hurts the creature, and it knocks a quantity of stone slabs off the inside walls, which makes the climb down awkward later but comes nowhere near collapsing anything. Everyone else attacks at once.

What wins the fight is Fabian standing at the lip with Thunder Wave ready, holding it each round for the moment the minotaur begins to climb.

- The first time, the creature springs up the wall with real aggression and Fabian blasts it off, dropping it back into the open part of the pit where everyone can see it.
- The second time, he waits until it is right at the top so that it takes the fall as well as the blast. The cost is leaving himself entirely open; he takes the risk and it pays.
- A third attempt half-lands and does not shift it.

Mags holds Thorn Whip ready to drag it off the wall and never quite manages it. She lands a Toll the Dead late in the fight, and at one point spits in its face and invites it to come and get her if it thinks it is hard enough, specifically to pull it off Fabian. It works. The creature turns and gores at her instead.

It shrugs off a great deal of what is thrown at it, and comes on regardless, with no more thought in it than a thing that has been pointed at a target.

It reaches the top once. It claws Rell from near-full health to the floor in a single round, and he is unconscious and bleeding before anyone can intervene. Mags gets a Healing Word into him. He stands, moves around to put the creature between himself and Mags, and lands the blow that finishes it. It falls backwards into the pit with a crash.

## The Axe

Mags has already worked something out. Comparing how badly hurt the creature looked when it went into the pit against how it looked coming back out, it must have been healing down there — and it was not healing while out in the open.

With it down, Caspian watches the pit closely and sees the damage beginning to fade again. Mags fires Chill Touch into it, which stops it recovering, and settles in to hold it there indefinitely while the others go down.

The den is unpleasant. There are old bones, gnawed, and a quantity of debris, and nothing else. Nothing recent.

Caspian spends his last remaining spell on Identify, directed at the axe buried in the creature's chest. The party learns:

- The axe defies identification. He cannot say what it is.
- It is strongly magical.
- It is the cause of the minotaur's regeneration.

Caspian tries to pull it free and cannot. Fabian moves him aside to show him how it is done and also cannot. Mags observes from above that at some point it stops being about brute force and becomes a question of leverage and intelligence, and is invited to come down and try it herself.

Rell cuts it out instead. The undead sinew is tough, but he knows where to cut, and he works around the blade rather than hauling at it. The moment it comes free the body begins to decay at speed, running visibly through the stages of decomposition, and within about twenty seconds there is almost nothing left of it.

What he is holding is a large, heavy, pristine axe, the sort of weapon a serious warrior would carry.

## Greyface

Mags, alone at the lip of the pit while the others are below, hears footsteps behind her. They are slightly squelching.

She waits, steps sideways, and throws up a cloud of dust and mud with Prestidigitation, intending to catch the outline of anything invisible. She is not as subtle about it as she hoped, and what the dust settles on is not invisible at all.

It is a humanoid figure in heavy robes, red and trimmed with gold, with an elaborate hood that covers most of its face and a staff held without any threat in it. Enough of its flesh is showing to make clear it is undead. As the dust hits it, it brushes off the skirts of its robes.

Mags greets it very loudly, partly to be civil and mostly to bring the others up the rope, and keeps it talking while they climb. It gives her a name of sorts: people in these parts call him Greyface, which he finds a little rude. One does what one can with what one has, he says, but it keeps him humble.

She tells him she is half deaf and would find it easier if she could lip-read. There is a flicker of impatience — he has by now asked the same question twice and been answered neither time — but he pushes the hood off his face. He is clearly undead and clearly relatively fresh: not a skeleton, all his flesh still on him, a deathly colour to it. His face is definitely human, and it has been altered to look more elvish. Nobody present has heard of a people who do that.

Fabian asks him directly whether he is the one who let them in through the door. He inclines his head slowly and deliberately.

- "Yes. You looked to be in something of a difficult situation where you were, and I thought this might be an interesting opportunity. And so it proved."

Then: the axe, if they please.

## The Negotiation

Fabian tries to keep it. The axe is really rather heavy, he says, and it would be quite difficult to get it out of that pit, but they would be happy to leave it down there. He is not lying and he is also not believed.

He tries again, asking as a common courtesy that the exit be unsealed before any weapons change hands. The answer is no, and an instruction to produce the axe before the figure begins to lose its patience.

It climbs down into the pit without using the rope and holds out a hand. Rell does not resist, but makes the point while handing it over: the axe is his to take, but setting that beast on them was hardly friendly, and it very nearly disembowelled him. He would be dead now if his companions had been worse placed.

- "It wouldn't have been my preferred outcome if it ended any one of you, but of course the possibility was there. I thought you actually handled it rather cleverly."

Pressed on why set it on them at all, he says there were certain qualities of the circumstance, of the artifact and of their abilities that interested him. Visitors of any caliber are rare down here. Rell suggests that the next band might last longer if he refrains from disembowelling them, and receives a long flat look that he cannot read at all.

The figure takes the axe up onto its shoulder, scrambles out of the pit without difficulty, and turns it over in its hands as it talks, looking at it with evident interest.

Mags reads him carefully through all of this and establishes two things. He has no intention of attacking them and no interest in killing them imminently — but if he thought it would be interesting or profitable to do so, he would do it without hesitation. Her working policy from that moment is that the party must now be boring. And the axe itself is a relatively unfamiliar object to him in its physical immediacy: he is curious about it rather than triumphant, and does not treat it as something that matters greatly to him.

## What He Says

Fabian keeps him talking, and most of what the party learns this session comes out of it.

Asked whether it is a dwarven axe, he agrees it is — not the most difficult deduction under the circumstances — and of some considerable antiquity. It is an object he is studying and intends to continue studying.

Asked whether he has been up to the dwarven part of the complex:

- "Yes, I've been there. And I think there won't be very much to find the next time I go that way. It's a good thing I recovered what I did."

Asked whether there are more dwarves ahead, he says there are some he has seen, though he has not explored very much deeper than this, and that when God was more expansive in its habits there were several dwarven sites which came under its attention. He says "God" with audible quotation marks around it and makes no attempt to hide that.

Fabian tells him they think they have met this god, and that it was white and amorphous and rather sticky, and did not seem to care much for dwarven architecture. He corrects them:

- It is a substance, and it is fascinating but not particularly pleasant.
- They are wrong about it not caring. It cares very much. What it does not like are things it cannot reshape and make its own.
- That area was resistant for quite some time. It had a certain sort of energy which gives protection.
- There are a considerable number of places within its substance which have not yet been absorbed, and may never be — unless some intrepid adventurers decide to disrupt things, in which case things can change. He credits them with having done so.

Asked whether he is not afraid this room will collapse as well, he gestures at the mismatched statuary and borrowed architecture around them:

- "This place is very much to God's liking. He has picked and chosen the elements that please him, and this is the result. I dare say it will change, but not immediately."

On the trapped temple corridor above, he observes that the dwarves were rather thorough in their idea of security — not, in the end, that it did them any good whatsoever.

## The Door

Fabian finally gets them out by offering Caspian up. Caspian, he explains, is a wiz hand with all things magical and dwarven, and would gladly sit and pore over that axe for hours — if, of course, the figure is not too busy, and if he would be so gracious as to open that door.

Caspian, alarmed, pleads exhaustion: they have all shown some facility for magic, but they are tired and it addles the brain, and they would not be as useful as he imagines. The figure accepts this entirely at face value, and takes the offer of Caspian's future assistance as nothing more than his due.

He turns to an empty stretch of the chamber where there is the faint outline of an archway and says: well, you heard me, open the way quickly now.

There are sounds from the stone. Nothing moves. For the first time he raises the staff in a genuinely threatening way — not at the party, but at the air and the wall — and the sounds increase at once and the archway begins to shape itself much faster. He lowers the staff.

- "That's better. Let's not get any ideas."

The way opens in the same form as the arch that admitted them, onto a passage curving away and downwards. That will take them to Dunton, he says. It is not far.

He will not come. His presence in the town causes something of a stir and he does not wish to alarm or distress people unnecessarily; he will return to the Ossuary, another region of at least relative stability. If they need him, the Provost can tell them the way, though he doubts she will be keen to accompany them.

They should tell the people of Dunton that he sent them. They are not always welcoming to outsiders. They may say that in his capacity as High Priest he has discerned that God would prefer that their sacrifice wait for a more appropriate time. That should satisfy. He does not think they will disbelieve, and besides, even tired as they are, he doubts any of them would really be a match for the party. They are a rather sorry bunch.

Asked about hazards on the way, he mentions the dwarves' thoroughness again, and adds that should his captive give them any trouble, he would make it regret it.

## Afterwards

Out of earshot, Caspian asks what that was.

He does not say what he is actually thinking. Something about the figure rang a bell from the infernal notes he carries, and he keeps it to himself for now, concerned about being asked how he knows.

Mags turns her attention instead to the relationship between the figure and the stone. She establishes:

- Earth elementals, even quite sophisticated ones like the gate that let them onto Grommar's Path, can be magically compelled and dominated.
- This one is definitely being forced to obey.
- It is not charmed. The figure is overpowering it with its own magical will. That is why the first command produced noise and no movement, and the threat produced results.
- If that will were broken, the elemental would not be inclined to obey anyone. It would do as it pleased, and such spirits are not normally cooperative.

## Dunton

The passage runs about four hundred metres, curving gently left and descending without ever getting steep. It opens out here and there into small chambers and then comes out into a cavern.

It is nothing like as large as the cavern of the goblin folk, but it is substantial, and there is a town in it.

- A couple of dozen houses, tatty rather than new, and composite. Some are around eighty per cent of a house with pieces tacked on; others are more scavenged parts assembled. An enormous amount of effort has gone into keeping them standing.
- One much larger structure at one end: two storeys, sturdily built of stone, something like a peel tower. Calling it a fort would be a stretch.
- Pathways rather than streets between the houses.
- A barricade closing the open edge of the cavern — not wood, but that level of structure, made of stuff piled up. It has the same character as the debris in the stone river: things that should not exist in the same place. A couple of people stand on it, looking outward.
- Lights burning in some houses with the steady glow of magic rather than the unsteadiness of candles.

The party's tunnel has opened well inside the barricade, and nobody is watching that piece of cavern wall. Whatever Dunton defends against, it is not on this side. The tunnel closes as soon as the last of them is through. Mags pats the rock and thanks it.

The dwellings are mixed in size, including some that would suit someone of Mags's height. The party heads for the one building with a sign outside it, which is unmistakably an inn sign. It reads Ketter's Rest, in Common.

## Ketter's Rest

The door is not locked. Inside is something that by a loose definition is a taproom: half a dozen tables, a few proper chairs, some stools, and a great many conveniently sized boxes pressed into service as seats. Behind something that does resemble a bar are mismatched bottles without proper labels.

A bleary-eyed, middle-aged human comes down the stairs and looks at them in simple astonishment. Fabian explains that the High Priest recommended the place.

The man's reaction is nervous, and there is fear in it, but he looks at them more with pity than with fear. Mags adds that the priest said their sacrifice did not need to happen right away — and, she suggests, probably did not need to happen at all; later never really needs to come, does it? He holds up his hands in a gesture of helplessness and begins to say that sacrificing guests is not something he would be party to.

He will not promise to keep them hidden.

- "I can't hide you from the Provost. Do you understand that? She will have to be told."

He pours out a measure each of something rough and quite alcoholic, made from mushrooms and whatever else can be mustered, and begins to observe that at least they are all here in one piece — then catches himself, looks stricken, and asks whether they left anyone. Told that everyone is here and that they have a healer, he nods and smiles with obvious relief. Then things are well enough.

His name is Varen, and this is his place. It is a name they might have met in Greywatch; it tells them nothing.

Mags offers her services as a healer to the town. His answer is worth noting:

- "Well, there was a time when a healer in these parts would have, well, would have changed everything. But this high priest came. We've not too much lack in that regard, which is a blessing, of course."

Rell deflects the rest of the conversation to the afternoon, over brunch. Varen shows them upstairs to a room that is really just blankets on the floor and cushions stuffed with rags, and empties his house of pillows when Mags keeps asking for more. He is courteous, thoughtful and as hospitable as his means allow, and he is also going to go and tell the Provost at the first opportunity. He wants no part of whatever is occurring here.

Caspian casts Alarm on the doorway, set to trigger if anybody comes to the door. The party sets watches regardless.

## The Book

Mags takes the first watch and sees nothing but a routine changing of the guard on the barricade. She wakes Caspian by kicking him in the head.

Caspian, alone, has a question he wants answered before he does anything else: does his book give itself away? He establishes that it does, up to a point. Anyone attuned to that sort of magic would sense something in fairly close proximity — close enough that carrying it hidden in a stack of books would not help — but not from any real distance, and not merely from his taking it out and reading it.

He risks it, and reads about the puppet man. The book's influence makes his skin crawl, as it always does, and he sets that aside and focuses. The party learns the following in the morning, when he shares all of it without once mentioning where it came from:

- A practitioner skilled in this kind of magic can make an undead puppet and channel their awareness through it, along with a reasonable slice — though not all — of their magical power. It is used by people who want to stay safe and hidden while still acting and interacting out in the world.
- It does not require killing anyone. The sooner after death the better, so killing the subject and working immediately is most efficient, but a corpse up to around eighteen hours dead will serve.
- Condition matters. A violently or heavily killed body makes a weaker puppet. The book recommends suffocation, since the puppet will not need to breathe and the method does no damage to the faculties.
- The process is slow and costly, so although a puppet is more disposable than its master, losing one is a serious nuisance.
- Physical qualities carry over strongly from the original: a large, strong subject makes a strong puppet much more easily. A sufficiently skilled practitioner can also imbue it with strength or speed during the making.
- Its mental attributes are simply the puppeteer's.
- It does not see or hear through the corpse's organs. It perceives by a kind of magical sensing instead.
- The puppeteer must be focusing on the puppet. They do not passively know what it is doing. Running a puppet and their own magic at once, or two puppets at once, would take an unusual degree of mastery.
- Range is limited, but can be extended a long way by setting up magical buffers or relay points around an area in advance. Out in open country the range is short.
- Simple magic projects through a puppet easily. The more complex the magic, the harder it becomes and the more likely to fail, with a ceiling set by how well the puppet was made.
- A puppet is healed magically rather than mended like a construct, but ordinary healing will not do it. It takes necromantic magic belonging to this same discipline.

One further thing stays with him. This magic does not work like other magic. He is used to learning across the schools and adapting between them, and this feels different in kind from anything he has encountered. He cannot work out why.

His conclusion, which he does not state aloud in those terms, is that the thing they met in the arena was a puppet for something else. Whoever they were actually speaking to in that chamber, they have not met them.

Rell asks whether magical sensing of that kind would have found him while he was flickering in and out of the ethereal plane. The only answer anybody has is that it did interact with him.

## Morning

Fabian takes the next watch and finds the town waking up. The lights come up across the cavern — not daylight, but a gloomy and consistent glow from what is clearly a relatively sophisticated system of magical lights, switched on at a chosen hour. The people of Dunton have collectively decided that this is morning.

- Word has already spread. Nobody gathers or stares or points, but people are casting glances up at the inn considerably more than they otherwise would.
- The weaponry on display is basic: simple swords and daggers, simple bows.
- The population is mostly humans and halflings — ordinary halflings, not deep ones, though everyone is pale from lack of sunlight.
- There are children. Babies carried in slings, and a couple of children running about.

No Provost appears. Fabian makes a point of watching closely enough to recognise faces again later.

Rell takes the last watch, hears people moving quietly about the building below and voices talking in low tones, and decides not to go down and break the peace by falling into conversation.

The party completes a long rest, the first proper one in a long while, and wakes to Fabian putting heart into everyone. Mags asks whether any of them wake up oddly tired. The question is left hanging.

Caspian opens the morning by admitting that he paid no attention at all on his watch and read books instead, which surprises nobody, and then shares everything he learned.
