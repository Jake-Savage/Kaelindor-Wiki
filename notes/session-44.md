# Session 44 - 17/11/2026

## The Last of the Plain

The caves are an hour and a half away, and the great wall of the cavern is starting to loom out of the dark ahead. Caspian finds the way, Rell and Trevick keep watch, and Mags, still shaken by the thing that burrowed into her, is quieter than usual. Olo Halfkettle stumbles and hurts her ankle too badly to walk on it. Fabian kneels beside her with a vial and a speech: he learned from the finest physicians in the world at the Bards' College, and they taught him to distil all that medical goodness into these little vials of wonder. It is a real potion of healing, and Olo gets up and walks on, testing the ankle gingerly. Trevick takes the next stretch with Caspian at his side, and Fabian keeps the villagers watching where they put their feet.

## The Witch Comes Back

The witch comes riding back across the plain the other way, and this time she sees them first. The villagers have never seen anything like the tiger, and they scramble out of its way; some of the children cry. She takes no notice of any of them and rides straight up to the party.

She wondered if they might still be out here, she says. She has spoken with Lae'ethorian, and he is leaving. Should they escape too, do they intend to betray him and reveal where he is? He is not as concerned about that as he should be.

Rell answers that he owes little allegiance to those who make war on Lae'ethorian's people, but that the man is wanted for many terrible things, and it might be unwise not to tell the right people. He has seen Lae'ethorian trying to reclaim some of his humanity, but if he means to be a power on the surface again, his power has grown, and he will do far more harm than before. At that the witch looks back toward the Ossuary with a clear shudder, of distaste more than fear. She will not deny that his conduct has been complicated, she says, but she has come to see more wisdom in him than she expected, and she cannot allow them to compromise this latest attempt of his.

She has given up a great deal herself to avoid the path he has walked, she tells them. Lae'ethorian is part of what is wrong among her people, a significant part. But he has given up much to argue for what he believes must be done, and he goes back now to make his case again. His power has grown, and power is something her people respect, for now. She has been down here many years and does not know how things stand at court. She argued against his going, for what it is worth, but it seems the party's visit galvanised him.

When Rell says that her people will use any power available to them, she flushes and turns on him. How can anyone blame them for seeking the one power that can actually do something? And yet she herself refused to take it up, so perhaps she is no one to speak. Rell asks whether, if Lae'ethorian takes all her people down that path with him, that is a survival worth having. She shakes her head. He is hardly the first to walk it. It was begun years before his birth, and before hers, and born as he was he had very little choice, less even than she had. He does not seek to turn their people deeper into the embrace of Thalparaneth. For all they saw of him at the temple, he seeks to rally their people to a battle that the Archon would avoid, as the Archon did years ago.

Rell tells her that nobody seeks out corruption; it is never a choice. She tries to keep her face still, but he reads her completely. She thinks it nonsense: she is certain that people seek corruption and choose it. And there is bitterness under it, as if refusing has cost her so much that a good part of her thinks she was a fool not to choose it. She means them no harm. But Rell can see her hardening, coming round to the idea that to protect Lae'ethorian she may have to deal with them.

## Caspian's Word

Caspian steps in. He understands better than most, he says, that corruption is not so black and white. She wants to save Lae'ethorian as they want to save these people. They will say nothing of this place, nothing of him, nothing of what they have seen. Quietly, so the villagers cannot hear, he adds that there are ways to make them never speak of it either, and that he knows people who can alter memories and could learn to do it himself. They can all just leave, and be done. He means it. He is tired, he does not love it here, and he wants to go.

The relief shows on her. What Lae'ethorian is about is a complex business, she says, that even their authorities would not understand. It will take him some weeks to get back across the border; after that it hardly matters what they say, but he must have the chance to return and make his case. It probably will not make any difference, but it might. She would not see him caught, not after all this time.

She does not see how they will fight their way to the surface from here. Lae'ethorian will fight his own way out, she agrees, and then, a little amused and impressed, she says that he was not happy with how his attempt to get his cattle back from them turned out. There is another way, she says at last. Not safe, exactly, but safer than this: a shortcut through the Feywild, warded along its length, though nowhere in that sorry land is safe these days and she has not walked it in some time. It does not come out here, but on the far side of the mountains, in Kalredor, near Mooncross and the Ridgeway.

Trevick asks how to be sure they carry nothing back with them. She sees that he knows something of the danger. She warded the path with Lae'ethorian's help, she says, and without it the road would have been impossible: the magic Rell calls corruption is the only thing her people know that holds the uthrel back. She does not hide that they could destroy her wards and trap her down here, and though she has no intention of leaving, being unable to is another matter.

Her price is their word that they will not hunt Lae'ethorian or send others to. Fabian gives it at length. They are friendly adventurers, he says, here to help everyday people like these; grand strategy and hunting down elvish enemies is not their business, and if they did tell anyone, nobody would listen. He does not mean a word of it, and she would prefer all of it to be true. She will not hinder them if escape is all they want, she says, but if they come to her home she will show them another way out. It is a refuge but not a comfortable one, and unlike Lae'ethorian she has no need of extra company down here. She gives Caspian directions by the plain's few landmarks, west past the Keep, and rides away without waiting.

On the way, Fabian prepares the villagers with the story of the Gorgon and the evil eye. If the witch looks their way, they should drop their eyes; if she speaks to them, they should treat her as a queen and take a knee. They take it calmly. It is how they learned to deal with Lae'ethorian, the only elf any of them has ever met.

## Grokkens

They pass a clump of stones in a patch of loose dust, and the dust shakes itself and becomes several grokkens: squat, rocky things with mouths of teeth in rows that run down into their throats, mouths they can turn inside out like a worm. Fabian steps in front of the villagers, shouts at them to keep away, and Thunderwave from the golden lute hurls three of them back and rings out across the plain. Rell's twinned Dissonant Whispers send two scrambling away, and Tom Pruett and Trevick drive off another.

The rest go for Fabian. One claws him off his feet; they bite; one tears his hamstring; one throws him aside, and he lies dying on the stone. Olo plants herself over him with her club. Max, who has been waiting, runs a wide circle round the fight, leaps onto one grokken's back and sinks his teeth into its neck, and it goes down. Trevick's arrow pins another to the ground. One bites Rell hard before Caspian's Fire Bolt burns it, and the others flee. Caspian finds a potion in Fabian's pocket and pours it into his mouth, asking him how in the world he ended up in this stupid position.

## The Garden

Close to, they see what the Keep's patrols saw: a shimmering mirage of a building and a green space where nothing green should be, its light not there and then suddenly bright, plants growing and a roof showing between them, all inside a clear boundary. It is the place the commander described. Every one of the party can tell it is an illusion, a good one but spread thin over a large area and weaker for it; only Max is completely taken in. Crossing its edge, Caspian above all feels that they pass with permission, and that without it the illusion would simply have dissolved around them and left them somewhere else. The villagers think it more beautiful and more natural than anything they have seen in years, and are too tired not to trust it.

Inside, a high hedge with a gate in it is plainly Elvish, a much grander version of the style they saw at Derwyth's, and Fabian knows it from his studies. Beyond stands a single building of one proper floor, its roof half covered and half a garden, amid a flourishing garden full of birds and insects that are not real. Caspian's Detect Magic shows that no one is maintaining the illusion now: it has been cast so often for so long that it has taken root. Fabian stares hard at the gate trying to see what is really there, and later tries again with Trevick, and neither can. Rell keeps to himself; he has no wish to draw her attention.

The witch comes out of the house, her staff blazing under Caspian's Detect Magic, with no sign of the tiger, calmer than before. She surprised herself with that offer, she says. Lae'ethorian's company has changed her perspective in ways she did not expect, or perhaps she felt guilty that she never offered him this road herself. Either way, it is best they be gone. When Caspian asks whether everyone might rest for an hour first, she looks at the villagers as if they were clutter, the discarded possessions of Lae'ethorian's. But as Caspian speaks of them with kindness, something of it reaches her, and she sees people who need help. "You're right. I'm sorry. I did not consider. Please rest." She brings out a great kettle of tea, and it does everyone good. Fabian rallies them all: haven't they come far, and does everyone remember Rell leaping through the bone storm to grab that elf?

## What She Tells Caspian

She spends the rest talking mostly to Caspian, whom she seems to find the most fun, and he asks her everything. The party learns the following:

- Travellers used to anchor a point in the Feywild so as to reach it quickly; Caspian knew of it only in theory, and nobody does it now. She used her road often when she first came to the Stonehunger. It has grown worse and needed more warding over the years, and she has not walked it herself in the last three years.
- The Feywild is a wasteland overrun by the uthrel. As far as she knows all of it is, and nothing else lives there, not even the fey. It is the great tragedy of the elves. The uthrel can be pushed back in places with enough magic, at great danger.
- Necromantic magic of the kind Lae'ethorian uses cannot destroy the uthrel, but it can hold it at bay.
- When she left Thal'Mirrith, and it was plainly an escape, she took necromantically charged stones with her and laid the path with them. They lost their power over time, and Lae'ethorian, who came down considerably later, supplied new ones.
- She does not practise necromancy herself. That is why she left: she was under pressure to take it up and would not, wanting to keep her druidic practice pure. She does not disapprove of it, but she knows how it damages the soul, and she is conflicted about her choice.
- Opening a private channel into the Feywild is forbidden, for fear the uthrel gets through. She did it anyway, because she wants to be in the Stonehunger without being trapped in it.
- Lae'ethorian asked to use the road, as he has before, and she refused him, because she does not trust him not to do something dangerous with it. She trusts the party not to know how.
- On the road they must stay on the path, move quickly and not stop to sleep, and bring no trace of the uthrel out. It grows poorly in this world and needs a good deal to start an outbreak, but it can be done. Fire is not her magic, and she cannot say how much would be needed to burn it out.
- The link to the Feywild can never be wholly cut, and wherever there are links there are outbreaks. The Elenfain wards every entrance in the lands the elves hold, but she speaks of it as holding back something that will not stay held.
- The road comes out at an anchor just west of the Ridgeway Pass, the way she fled Thal'Mirrith, in woods away from any town, about a day's walk along it. The gate at the far end will only show itself to those who came in through hers.

Caspian offers to shore up any ward that is failing. She is surprised he thinks he could, and wonders aloud how and why he would have that sort of magic; he says he likes to experiment. Anything that strengthens the path would be welcome, she says, but tampering carelessly could bring a backlash. Trevick watches her as she talks and sees that she is playing the danger down, knowing she should never have built the road, and that in the end it is not her problem. There is a fatalism in her: if an outbreak comes, it comes.

Fabian, Trevick and Caspian put the fear of the road into the villagers with every horror story they know, the children especially. Then the witch opens the gate, drawing on structures laid down long ago, and they cannot see what lies through it. Caspian decides to believe her, and steps through first.

## The Feywild

The sky is the same pinkish purple as her garden. Everything else is grey: a powdery, ash-like covering with lumps in it, stretching away into a haze, the air thick with drifting spores. The path ahead is clear of uthrel but entirely bare, and Caspian guesses that the same necromancy that keeps the uthrel off it smothers any other life too. The land is flat, its hills still there but everything that ever grew on them flattened, so they can see a long way. Here and there Trevick passes half-buried shapes that were once buildings, or woods, and it takes him straight back to what he found at home. Caspian is simply sad, and for once does not even want a sample.

Far off to the left, on the horizon, Trevick and Rell are the first to see something that breaks the grey: large buildings, martial-looking, towers, two of them joined by what might be the wall of a fort, all of some black stone. They are much too far away to make out anyone near them, and the witch never mentioned them. The party keeps moving.

The wards cannot be seen, but Caspian works out that there is one every couple of hundred metres, and that the newer ones are a good deal stronger than the old. At one or two places the wards have weakened and the path has narrowed, and everyone has to ease past, but it holds. Fabian prods Caspian with his sheathed sword every time he stops to look. To Caspian's Detect Magic the whole place is nothing: not even the faint background hum of the ordinary world, just a blank. Everyone is worn to the bone, and the villagers are running on nothing at all, but they keep moving.

## Out

After a day or so, the glowing outline of the far gate appears where the witch said it would be. Before anyone goes through, Trevick checks every child's feet and pockets and every one of Max's paws, then goes from adult to adult telling them what he saw at Velton. Caspian checks them all again, and Fabian scrubs everyone clean with Prestidigitation. They have kept to the path and made no wild forays, and they are confident they carry nothing out. Caspian maps what he can, though the road folds the geography so that no map could take him back.

On the other side there is nothing at all: no mark, no shimmer, nothing even to Detect Magic. The anchor is still there, but only the witch can open it. It is almost a door that opens one way, though someone powerful enough, who knew roughly where to look, could find it and pick it open.

It is about ten at night, late autumn, cold and dark, in the foothills of the Stonemarch, in empty wilderness with no sign of anyone. They are out of the Mirrowen. Caspian ritually raises a Tiny Hut, and the children are glad of something with a solid roof. Some of the villagers are asleep before the talking is done. They have been underground for more than a decade, and now there is grass under them, and trees, and stars. Seventeen of them and the dog have come out.
