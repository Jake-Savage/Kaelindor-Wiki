# Session 40 - 20/10/2026

Back in Dunton, with the plan from the night before still standing: go up to the Ossuary with the Provost and put the whole case to Greyface. Talking it over again, the party lets that plan go.

## The Case Against Asking

What they know about the puppet decides it. The figure in the Ossuary is far less use to its master while he sleeps; it can carry out what he has arranged in advance and little else, and everyone has to sleep, unless they pay for magic to keep them awake. The villagers believe the man himself is in the Ossuary, and he has never pretended otherwise to them. A puppet cannot be worked from miles away, though a lair stretches the distance. Detect Magic would show the puppet but not the thread back to its maker, and a dispel could strike at whatever animates the body but not at the link.

Nobody knows when the Stonehunger will go down. Everyone who has spoken of it says there are signs and that no one can read them; five days is a fair middle guess, and it could be a great deal sooner.

Mags casts Augury and asks whether, if they leave within thirty minutes, Greyface will come after them fast enough to catch the slowest of them. The answer is weal. They decide to go tonight, and to tell him nothing.

## Raising the Village

It is evening, and the hunters are coming back across the plain with crab-meat and mushrooms. Trevick goes among them, asking which ground is safe and which to avoid, and talking people round one at a time.

Fabian brings about two-thirds of the village into Ketter's Rest with a song. They enjoy it and are moved by none of it. The speech that follows does better. He tells them the party has been inspired by their perseverance and that they deserve to be rewarded for it; they are so close to the top, and in less than five days, perhaps much less, this place goes back down for who knows how long. Someone interrupts to ask whether they have spoken to Greyface, and whether he is coming. Fabian says they have, and that Greyface told them it goes down in five days, which is true as far as it goes and says nothing about his blessing. The Provost backs him, not entirely comfortably.

Mags calls on Naelos, and some of the villagers make signs of devotion while others shift uneasily. Rell tells them they have been living like rats on someone else's sufferance and that this is the chance to take that back. Fabian speaks of relatives waiting on the surface, and of children who have never seen the sun.

Partway through, Rell sees a young man slip out of the door. Mags goes after him and finds he is only fetching the third of the village that stayed away, though the account he gives them of the meeting is not a kind one.

It works. Many are eager, others will go if everyone goes, and a few are plainly unhappy about leaving without Greyface's leave. More than one of them tries to go and tell him, and the party stands in their way. Rell argues that the village has not yet drawn his anger and would be foolish to invite it now. Most give in, reluctantly. Everyone is told to pack keepsakes only and be ready in thirty minutes. Fabian's mention of bringing arms does more to frighten people than to reassure them.

Trevick finds a halfling and gives him one of the romance novels he has been carrying since Greywatch, with a second copy in Torvish so they can learn the language on the road together, and adds that his friend is an excellent writer. Then he pulls one of his Nameweave crackers with Fabian. It goes off with real violence and singes them both. The nervous become more nervous and the bold more eager. Mags had the sense to keep the children back, and Fabian spends a few minutes on small conjured tricks to lighten the mood. Trevick has two crackers left.

## Out Across the Plain

All thirty-four come, and the old dog. Five of them argued against it to the last but would not be left behind. It is a column of small children, old people and far too much luggage, and Mags makes anyone who falls behind under a load put something down. No message is left for Greyface.

The Provost leads and navigates, with Caspian and Fabian beside her. Trevick walks in the middle of the column and keeps Pass Without Trace over all of it, and Mags brings up the rear. They travel under a single dim, red, hooded Light. Rell lays a false trail with Mags's lantern and leaves a candle burning down toward a store of lamp oil about two miles off, to flare when it reaches it. Hawk carries a lit pebble away in a false direction, then turns back to watch the village.

To keep the scattered party in touch Rell opens his mind to Hawk's, and it is not what he expected. It is much more like reaching a person than an animal. The mind is not a simple one, and what comes through feels cramped, as if something is limiting it or as if Hawk is choosing to hold back. Rell assumes Trevick already knows and keeps it to himself.

The Provost is making for a stretch of porous rock and caves like the approach to the Ossuary, where there are steep shafts running straight up. They are not natural; she says they were tumbled out, and she does not know by whom.

Mags keeps people walking long past the point they wanted to stop. Ninety minutes in they are flagging badly and the caves are still hours away. The column as a whole is slow, but the old and those carrying children are giving everything they have. Trevick casts Pass Without Trace for the last time that night.

## Something From the Village

Hawk comes back in a panic, lands on Trevick and presses himself against him. Through the link Rell gets the shape of it: Hawk saw something at the village that frightened him badly, and he fled. It was coming broadly in the party's direction, though it might as easily have been following the false trails, and he did not stay to find out which. Trevick asks him quietly whether it was the Stonehunger itself and gets a no: something large, but no sense of the walls coming in.

Caspian puts it to Mags that the two of them should drop back and buy the convoy time. He is not happy about it but thinks it better than all of them being caught together. Mags calls it a very moral idea, and asks him to bend his brain to getting the villagers moving faster while he is at it. Fabian and Trevick go on with the column. Rell, called over the link, runs back to join the other two.

Caspian hears it first, and soon all three of them can: a clattering of things striking each other and the ground, like the bones Mags shakes for her divinations but on an enormous scale, carried on the air from the direction of the village. Caspian sends Theodore up in Hawk's claws and borrows the rat's senses. There is a smell on the wind of old, dry bones, and of something else Caspian knows at once: magic being held in place at an aggressive pitch. He knows it partly from the last couple of days and partly from much older memories. Theodore has those memories too, and panics, and Caspian pulls him back into his hood.

Mags casts Augury again and asks whether staying back as a diversion will keep the convoy from being attacked by Greyface's forces. The answer is weal and woe; too much depends on what has not happened yet.

Rell looks back toward the place he set his flare and makes out, darkness against darkness, a very large shape, eighteen or twenty feet tall. He cannot tell whether it is one thing or many. It is moving quickly and swinging wide of the rearguard, toward the flare rather than toward the convoy.

## The Patrol

At the front, Fabian picks out a patrol of six far off to the left: no lights, no attempt at concealment, simply tramping along. The Provost says they could be soldiers from the Keep or they could be priests, and either way they must be avoided. Greyface told her they think of the villagers as parasites living in the body of God, and that they put up with Dunton only because he came to some arrangement with them and convinced them he was one of their own. Out here they would kill the villagers on sight. The hunters find them easy to dodge, because they never leave their routes.

Fabian halts the column for a drink and a rest, which people take gratefully, and goes down the line telling everyone they are doing well and to keep their voices down. Trevick and Fabian put together a bottle of lamp oil with a lit wick, and Hawk carries it off toward the patrol, only just strong enough to manage it, to drop it somewhere that will pull them away.

By now the convoy can hear the clattering too, faintly and on and off, and they want to know what it is. Fabian tells them it is the wind moving through the great empty spaces. Nobody believes him, so he tells them instead that there are all sorts of dangers down here and that is reason enough to get back on their feet, and they do.

The thing from the village seems to be heading fast for Rell's flare. Mags, Rell and Caspian hold behind the convoy in a rough triangle with it, close enough to cut it off if it turns.

## Facts Learned

- Greyface's puppet is much less capable while he sleeps, and he must sleep unless he pays for magic to stay awake.
- A puppet's reach is not measured in miles, though a lair can extend it; Detect Magic shows the puppet but not its master, and a dispel can reach the animating magic but not the link.
- The villagers believe Greyface's own body is in the Ossuary.
- When the Stonehunger dives is unknowable; five days is a middling guess.
- Hawk's mind is a person's rather than an animal's, and something in it is held back.
- Upward shafts that someone cut lie in the porous rock and caves the Provost is making for.
- Something very large, smelling of old bones and hard-driven magic, came out of Dunton after the convoy. It is not the Stonehunger.
- Caspian, and Theodore with him, know that magic from a long time ago.
- The Keep's soldiers and priests walk fixed patrol routes across the plain, and would kill the villagers on sight. The Provost says, on Greyface's word, that they think of the villagers as parasites in the body of God, and that Greyface has passed himself off among them as one of their own.
