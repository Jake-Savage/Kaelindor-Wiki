# Session 33 - 25/08/2026

30 Dusklin, 1568. The chasm of the stone river is still deserted after the gleaners were sent up to the surface. The party regroups at its northeastern end, at the cleft in the rock, and goes in together.

## The Cleft

Caspian examines the opening itself. The rock here is under considerable stress: it is cracked through, and in places compressed to an almost glass-like texture. Something has been trying to force the opening closed and has not succeeded. Enough of a gap remains to slip through, tapering towards the top, though the larger members of the party have to turn sideways.

## The Courtyard

Inside is what reads as the outer courtyard of a temple or a villa, roughly forty feet across. The party establishes the following about the space:

- The open centre is laid with elaborate, colourfully dyed tiles in an abstract pattern rather than a picture, of good craftsmanship, much like the floor of the pillar chamber the party crossed on the way in. A few are cracked or broken; most are intact. There is protective magic on them, but weaker than elsewhere in the room.
- An arched portico runs around three sides, now set into the rock, which has come as close as it can. Beneath the arches the paving is broader flagstones, crumbled at the edges where the walls encroach but mostly still flat, about a metre and a half deep. On the fourth side, where the party entered, the portico has crumbled away entirely and only its jagged edges remain.
- There are no other exits, though there is every sign that there once were. The rock has closed over them.
- Judging by the height of the arches, the courtyard was built for people taller than humans, around six to seven feet rather than five to six.
- The rock is actively trying to press into the space and is being held back by something. Mags's reading is that the Stonehunger ate this courtyard at some point and that its magic is preventing it from being digested. This is the party's supposition and nothing in the room confirms it.

At the centre, on a plinth, stands the golden dragon statue. It is a naturalistic, true-to-life depiction of a dragon, of the kind made when dragons were still commonly depicted, and it is very fine work. It appears to be gold. Around the base of the plinth runs an inscription in Draconic script, written in a form not quite like modern Draconic but close enough to be read.

Mags scratches the statue with a fingernail to test whether it is solid gold. She feels a moment of give, as from a soft metal, and then a protective enchantment familiar to her comes to bear: the surface hardens beneath her nail and becomes extremely hard, as a magically protected object would. She takes no harm and no effect falls on her; she reads it as a ward against ageing or damage.

Trevick puts down his Comprehend Languages glasses and reads the inscription aloud:

---

The Lord of Light greets the dawn of a new age.

---

Nobody recognises the phrase. No one can say who the Lord of Light is, whether it refers to a particular dragon, or what age was being greeted.

Fabian, considering the statue's value, examines whether it could be moved. It cannot: it stands some twelve to fourteen feet from the base of the plinth, and about eighteen feet in overall length with the neck extended, and is far too heavy. What he finds instead is that the upper part of the plinth can be rotated, and that it has been rotated before. The base itself does not move, so there are no drag-marks on the floor, and the magic on the statue distorts any sense of how recently it was last turned. The party notes that the statue sits at the centre of the unfilled space, takes it to be what holds the rock back, and on that basis declines to remove it.

Caspian casts Detect Magic and works over what it shows him, with Rell claiming an expertise in the arcane he does not have. The party learns the following:

- The abjuration on the statue is powerful and is working, but what would happen if it failed, and how fast, is beyond them.
- There is also evocation on it: a capacity to emit magical energy.
- Separately, the statue has the capacity to absorb magical energy, a signature Caspian knows from objects that drink magic rather than shed it.

Caspian also senses something else magical in the courtyard and cannot fix on where it is, judging it either shielded or beyond his reach. He says so to the group directly, naming Trevick as the party's finder of things. Mags later senses exactly the same thing on her own Detect Magic and is equally unable to place it.

## Testing the Statue

Mags casts Light on the statue as a deliberately non-hostile test. Where the light falls there is a faint diminution, as though the statue is drinking part of it; nothing else happens.

The party debates whether the ward failing would crush them at once or slowly, and considers Shield of Faith on the walls and Leomund's Tiny Hut as insurance. Neither is used. Caspian's position is that a mechanism built to be turned is unlikely to be a mechanism that brings down the ceiling; Fabian's is that this is precisely what traps do. Mags casts her bones, reads the peril as within acceptable limits, and takes their answer as permission to proceed.

Caspian's compass establishes that the statue is facing west. Reasoning from the inscription that dawn is the direction that matters, and that solar bodies travel anticlockwise, the party turns it. It proves easy: one person alone would struggle, but two or three together manage it readily, the magic carries most of the weight, and the motion feels natural and designed. There are no satisfying clunks.

They work through the rotations with Detect Magic up — a half-turn anticlockwise to face east, then a full circle clockwise, then a full circle anticlockwise. Nothing changes at any point, either to the spell or to ordinary sight, and the handling does the statue no damage.

They then test the absorption directly:

- Rell casts Sorcerous Burst at it, choosing fire for its association with dawn. The magic sinks into the statue with no sign of damage. He works through every other kind of energy the spell can take, to no different effect.
- Fabian casts Vicious Mockery at it repeatedly, demanding its secrets. The statue drinks the psychic magic; nothing else occurs.
- Further castings of Light on the statue, on the portico and on each other suggested target produce only the same faint diminution.

Caspian raises the possibility that the statue is filling like a battery. Magic does not generally dissipate, so it is possible the thing is storing what it takes, but the party can see no evidence of it.

## The Hidden Flagstone

Caspian casts Detect Magic again, this time bending the whole of it on the faint signature he could not place rather than on the room at large. No longer distracted by the statue's far louder magic, and with Mags's Guidance on him, he finds it: beneath one of the flagstones under the northern portico, outwardly indistinguishable from its neighbours. Whatever is there is faint, either buried under a fair depth of stone or inside something that shields magic.

Rell tries to lever the flagstone up with his iron staff. A strong abjuration resists him; ordinary force will not shift it, and the field cannot be mined around. Caspian offers to Dispel Magic the area, and withdraws the offer on learning that this flagstone's abjuration is bound to the statue's, so that dispelling one would take the other. The party will not risk releasing the rock.

Mags turns instead to the tiles, and reads their weathering. The result reorients the whole problem: the courtyard is not lying as it was built, and against its original alignment the dragon, which the compass says is facing north, is facing what was east. The direction of dawn in the courtyard's original setting is also, as it happens, the direction of the hidden flagstone.

With the statue so aligned, Mags casts Guiding Bolt into it — the first radiant magic anyone has tried. The statue absorbs the energy as it has absorbed everything else, but this time it glows more brightly and from within, and a beam of light passes into it and out again to land on the flagstone. The beam does not last long. While it holds, the flagstone lifts freely by hand, with no need of a crowbar.

## The Chest

Beneath is a chest, surprisingly light, unlocked and unwarded. Rell checks it over with a small mirror on a stick and finds no trap. It is made of organic material, is very old and crumbling, and begins to come apart as it is handled; the decay is far past anything Mending would repair.

Inside are the remains of ceremonial regalia. The party recovers the following:

- A crown, magical and accordingly pristine and untouched by time. It has an outer band, a domed section, and above that a smaller tiered rise, in several layers. Pieces hang down at the sides of the head. It is elaborate, impractical and uncomfortable, and would make enough noise in wear to make hearing difficult.
- A hand-piece, also magical and pristine: four finger-rings joined by fine chains across the back of the hand to a bracelet at the wrist.
- Ceremonial clothing, largely crumbled with age, but with enough remaining to be carried away.
- Weapons that are ornate but of genuinely functional design rather than purely ceremonial: two curved swords, and something that appears to have shot darts. These are not magical, are badly decayed, and would not function. They carry no markings the party can place.

Fabian examines the find, with Mags's Guidance on him. The weapons tell him nothing. The costume, however, is cut for someone taller and more slender than a typical human, and on that he recalls what are less stories than legends: that in a time well before the recorded history of the current age there were people who had been physically changed by the magic of dragons, in a manner not wholly unlike the way Korrathian planar magic changes a tiefling. He judges it plausible that this regalia and these weapons were made for someone of that kind, and observes that it lines up with everything else in the room.

The party wraps everything up to carry out, intending to have it identified or appraised in a city rather than spend the time on Identify here. It has been the best part of an hour since the cavern was cleared, and not every gleaner left under compulsion — a good many were merely persuaded, and will drift back. On that reckoning the party leaves the courtyard as it found it, the statue restored to its original facing and the flagstone replaced. Mags stows the crown in her robes.

## The Gate

The party crosses to the archway. The stone forms into a large face as they approach. It is not stern or forbidding; it looks interested and a little amused, and shows no immediate hostility, though nothing about it is sympathetic either. Caspian's earlier reading of it as an earth elemental holds.

Fabian, still small and painted as a gleaner, greets the custodian and asks for access to Grommar's Path. The face turns its attention to him and opens by remarking on what it has already seen: the trick with the little scavengers amused it, and it had not seen anyone do that before. Asked why that should matter, it answers that it is the eyes of God in this part, and that it is suitable that it should be amused.

Its refusal is on grounds of waste. There is very little chance the party would progress along Grommar's Path, and if they did, the eyes of God below — whom it calls its brothers — would hardly share news of them back with it. Told they might return bearing news from beneath, it answers that they will not return by this way. It remarks that coming down from above so long after the surfacing is itself remarkable, and says it would far rather keep them here and watch them try to explain themselves when the gleaners come back, which they will.

Asked what else the party can offer, Fabian takes out his festival mask and tells it about the Itharian festival above and the pilgrims filling Greywatch, embellishing the account and playing up the religion. Mags lends him Enhance Ability, and the performance lands well. The face becomes more and more animated as he talks, and says it had not imagined the petty people of this age were capable of such elaboration.

Its interest, however, is not in the surrounding country, which it dismisses as paltry fare. It states plainly that the party is not of this place: they are too late and too interesting, travellers from somewhere far more promising, of a sort it had begun to suspect were no longer in the world. Had they come below weeks ago, it says, that would have been another matter.

Rell replies that not all of the party are from the world above as the face sees it, and that there is a limit to the lands it may now reach. At this the face shows an obvious, involuntary spasm of frustration before mastering itself. Mags watches it closely and reads the expression clearly. She learns the following:

- The creature genuinely believes there is now a limit to the lands they can claim. It is not a rhetorical position.
- It is confused and baffled by that fact, and feels something close to shame about it: the consciousness of a power that is diminished and diminishing.
- The reaction is involuntary. It then makes a deliberate effort to control its expression and present a facade of being in command of the situation.

Rell extends a wager. If the party is given leave to pass and does not succeed on Grommar's Path, they have means by which word can be passed back, and will leave a thread the face may pull. If they do succeed, the face is to accept that as their victory and grant its acknowledgement of their descent as the reward. Mags, still reading it, sees a look of frustrated impotence pass over the face and then a flash of cunning, and is confident of two things: that the creature has the power to grant neither the prize nor the reward it is being offered, and that it absolutely does not want to admit this.

It accepts regardless, and names what it actually wants: it would dearly like to pull those threads that would lead them back to lands more worthy of God's sight. It repeats that the party will fall and fail, but says that if they do not, then its acknowledgement and its blessing are theirs to carry below. Rell gives the formal acknowledgement the creature is evidently waiting for, and asks it to let them pass.

The surface of the stone ripples and an opening appears, the face shifting aside beneath the rock. Beyond is a dark passage sloping downward, clearly running much deeper into the earth. It is deliberately hewn rather than raw rock. Mags, Caspian and Rell each recognise the style: this is the sort of narrow ceremonial passageway that leads into some of the more seriously sacred and secretive dwarven temples of Calvyr.

The party walks down into the dark, and the door closes behind them.
