# Session 41 - 27/10/2026

Out on the plain, in the dark, the convoy has stopped. The villagers have put down what they are carrying and sat, with a few hooded lanterns among them, while Trevick's last Pass Without Trace runs down. Behind them is the thing the party now calls the bone swarm: a vast clattering, like a storm driving sticks and debris down a street, magnified beyond anything they have heard, and a smell of dry, dusty bone. Ahead and to one side, a few hundred feet off near some ruined buildings, the Keep's patrol walks its route under a faint reddish glow. Mags, Caspian and Rell are the rearguard, Hawk is aloft, and Rell's telepathy holds them all together.

## Waiting in the Dark

Rell's plan is to set the two against each other. Mags puts together the firebomb, a bottle of lamp oil with a wick, and Hawk carries it off with instructions passed over the link: drop it closer to the patrol than to the swarm, and off to the side, so the patrol gets there first and the bones arrive after. Rell tries to make a couple more of the same for himself, and most of the oil leaks away in the attempt.

At the convoy all lights go out. Fabian keeps them out, snuffing any that flicker on, and goes among the villagers trying to buoy them. People have tripped and fallen all the way here and are not shy of saying so. His practical advice on sore legs convinces nobody, since these people have survived down here a long time and know nonsense when they hear it. A story about a hero leading his people out of oppression and across a desert does a little better: the children listen, while some of the adults begin to think this idiot has led them into a terrible situation. Trevick does what Fabian cannot. Crouching among them, he tells them quietly to stay low and silent, that he knows where the enemy is, and that he has done this before among creatures far larger than them, and the murmuring dies away.

Rell's first flare goes out as the swarm reaches it. Caspian, listening, hears the quality of the noise change, as if for a moment it has stopped moving forward. Then Hawk's bomb flares, a small bright point in the dark and nothing more, since there is nothing out there for it to catch, and the swarm moves on again.

## Something Watching

Fabian feels it suddenly: a prickling sense of being watched. In the dark, among forty people, it is easy to explain away. Mags is sure it is scrying and wants him to use it to feed false directions to whoever is looking, perhaps that they are only a mile from the Keep. Caspian and Rell are unconvinced, since anyone watching would see as if they were standing there, and an elf sees in the dark. Nobody settles it.

Trevick, sharp-eyed, picks the patrol out again, a little further off now, and notices that it has drifted toward where Hawk's bomb fell. Later, from that same place, he hears voices: an exchange of words in a language he does not speak, not friendly but not confrontational either. It seems they were talking to Lae'ethorian.

## What Hawk Saw

Hawk, keeping watch near the dead flare, sees the swarm close up. From above it is a crescent, its wings nothing but loose bones caught in a swirling wind. At its centre is a dark mass that even his eyes cannot see into. Around the bones hangs a penumbra, gloomy and hazy, reaching out beyond them, and where it comes near the air turns very cold and tastes sharply of metal. It almost takes him. He beats his wings hard and spirals up and away, and it is very plain to him that if he had been drawn in he would have been beaten to pieces.

## Silence

The rearguard begins to pick its way back toward the convoy. Mags goes slowly and carefully, telling Caspian where to put his feet, and Caspian makes himself invisible. When Mags reaches the villagers she casts Silence over them so they can gather their things and stand without a sound, and so that whoever may be listening through Fabian hears nothing. Rell stays loose, circling east to get ahead and lay another timed flare. Trevick scouts a way north-east and finds it choked with rocks, debris and ruined buildings, no road for forty people.

Caspian, outside the silence, hears the bones getting louder, and fast. The air is getting colder.

## The Argument

Caspian says what he has been thinking to Mags. As reluctant as he was to bring these people out, if Greyface kills them out here he will only grow more powerful from their deaths. They have made a terrible mistake, he says, and people are going to die because of it. His proposal is to let Greyface take the villagers back: they will live, and it is a problem that can be returned to. Mags tells him the told-you-so has been noted, and that nobody is coming back to it.

Rell argues it the other way. Greyface has eked these people out one at a time for twelve years and has never wanted to consume them all at once; if he wipes out the village now he loses his supply. So he will come for the party and leave the villagers be. Caspian thinks that is wishful: he has sat with the man and knows he is not just a guy. In the end, for different reasons, they agree to stand with the villagers rather than send them on alone, and to tell them the truth: fight with us, or run, or go back to the village if they will.

## The Stand

Mags lets the silence drop and the noise of the bones is suddenly right on top of them. People start to panic. Fabian makes the speech of his life about the monumentous decision they have made, their place among the great men and women of this subterranean civilisation, and standing between fear and their loved ones. In the pitch dark he cannot see his audience, his heart is not in it, and as he speaks people are breaking, running and bursting into tears.

Mags casts Daylight. The villagers, who have not seen anything like it in years, are blinded and lost. And in the light they all see it: an enormous wall of bones, twenty or twenty-five feet high, floating a couple of feet above the ground, splitting in two. The western half, with a dark pulsing orb at its heart, comes straight at them; the other breaks away and swings round from the south. Neither shows any sign of slowing.

Mags slams her hands together, raises the symbol of Naelos and tells it that it shall not pass. There is none of the fear she knows from ordinary undead, nothing she would call feeling at all; it is like throwing something hard against a brick wall. But some of the ancient bones crumble to dust.

Then the western swarm rolls over Mags and Fabian and the villagers around them. Mags and Fabian stay on their feet, and so do a couple of others, bloodied. Most of the people beneath it are killed or knocked senseless and drawn up into the swirl, and it does not let them go. They become part of it. Someone fires a crossbow at it from behind cover.

Caspian takes the dark orb for a magical effect and tries to dispel it, and to his own astonishment it goes. It was high magic. Inside, on a platform of bones about fifteen feet up, sits a figure untouched by the storm around him, looking about for whatever just did that. Caspian steps back behind a rock. Trevick marks the figure and shoots, and the bones turn his arrows.

Rell, invisible, runs. He springs off a villager's shoulders and dives head first through the bones, taking cuts on the way, and lands on the edge of the platform. The figure is an elf. It is not the grey-faced puppet. Rell throws his whole weight at him without a thought for defending himself, and the two of them tumble backwards off the platform. They do not hit the ground. The swarm carries them, turning over and over, inside it.

Fabian, still caught in the bones, lies back and blasts Thunderwave from the golden lute at the elf and Rell as they swing past. It shatters bones into shards around him and opens a gap, and he scrambles out and drinks a potion.

Mags calls up Spirit Guardians as great shining moths of Naelos with chitinous wings to beat the bones away from her. Inside the swarm the two powers meet head-on, the very thing Greyface showed them in the Ossuary when he wound her Guidance into his own magic, and now it is volatile and crackling and she has to push back against it with everything she has. She holds it. Then something happens that she did not expect. When the body of a villager the swarm has taken drifts into the circle of her guardians, she feels a gathering-in, a sheltering, of someone only just dead whose soul has not yet had time to pass on, and who would otherwise be prey to the swarm's necromancy. She gives up going after the elf and spends herself moving through the bones to gather them in, and she shelters seven.

The elf makes no attempt to get free of Rell. He clings to him and pours necromancy into him, without finesse, simply pushing power in. Rell has been badly hurt before and this is not like that. It is a profound cold and a profound isolation from everything around him, and he knows that if it goes on much longer the damage will be very hard to undo. He is brought right to the edge, and the link alone keeps him awake. The elf takes command of the swarm again and thickens the bones about himself. His astonishment has turned to fury, and there is something righteous in it.

Caspian sends Magic Missile at him and it does not break his hold. Trevick, from behind his rock, puts an arrow through ten feet of whirling bone and a magical shield the elf throws up against it, and the flow of necromancy into Rell is cut. Rell, going under, locks his grip tighter, and then a bone takes him across the neck and he is out.

The southern swarm comes in and runs straight into Mags's guardians. This time she cannot hold them. They buckle at the edges and fail. But the souls she has gathered flow toward her, nothing anyone could see, and she knows she is carrying them within herself for as long as she stays whole, conscious and faithful. The southern swarm takes three more villagers who had not got far enough away.

Fabian waves the children in behind the big rock, points at the elf and declares that by turning on the people he swore to protect he has become cursed. The curse does not take hold. He gives Trevick heart instead. Mags, battered, calls on Naelos, god of order and of everything in its right place, to hold the elf where he is, and he shrugs it off.

## He Leaves

The elf has been knocked from his throne, stripped of his darkness and stung, and he weighs what he came for against how far he has got. The swarm reverses and begins to sweep back across the plain. As it turns, Caspian picks out the exact patch of it beneath Rell and dispels it, and Rell drops out onto the stone. Mags reaches him and steadies him, and then heals him fully.

Twenty of the thirty-four villagers are alive, and the dog. Fourteen are dead. Every one of the children lived, though six of them have lost a parent and little Daisy Thorngage has lost both. Varen the innkeeper is gone, and Kithri's son Ned, and old Oshin Half-toes, one of the two who had been down here longest. And the Provost was among those consumed, and with her went the only knowledge of where the tunnels are. Whether she is one of the seven Mags carries, Mags cannot tell.

Fabian gathers the survivors before they scatter. Many of them have lost someone, and there is a great deal of grief, but a good number of them have decided that Rell is a hero. The old dog has come through it. The patrol is still out there somewhere, and the bones are still audible, moving away.

## Facts Learned

- Greyface came himself. The swarm is ridden by an elf on a throne of bones, Lae'ethorian in his own body and not the puppet.
- The swarm is a floating crescent of bones twenty to twenty-five feet high, with a magical darkness at its heart and a cold, metallic penumbra around it. It can split in two, it holds what it catches, and it takes the dead and the senseless up into itself.
- The power of Naelos does not frighten it, but it does destroy some of its bones.
- Mags's guardians and the swarm's magic are opposed, and meeting head-on is volatile.
- Spirit Guardians can shelter the souls of the newly dead from necromancy. Mags carries seven.
- His necromancy, felt from inside, is cold and isolating and does lasting harm.
- Daylight does not trouble him, and he can shield himself.
- The Keep's patrol carries a faint reddish magical light and speaks a language Trevick does not know.
- Fourteen villagers are dead, the Provost and Varen among them. Twenty survive, every child among them, and the dog.
