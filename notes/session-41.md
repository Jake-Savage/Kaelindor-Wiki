# Session 41 - 27/10/2026

Out on the plain the dark is total, the kind of dark that presses on the eyes. The convoy has stopped. Thirty-odd villagers have set down their bundles and sunk onto the stone, a few hooded lanterns leaking slivers of light between their knees, while the last of Trevick's Pass Without Trace thins around them like mist burning off. Somewhere behind them is the thing the party has started calling the bone swarm. It cannot be seen. It can be heard: a vast, rolling clatter, like a storm driving sticks and wreckage down a street, swollen to the size of a landslide, and under it a smell of dry, dusty bone. Off to one side, a few hundred feet away among the shells of ruined buildings, the Keep's patrol walks its route inside a faint reddish glow. Mags, Caspian and Rell hold the rear; Hawk is somewhere overhead; and Rell's telepathy runs between all of them like a taut wire.

## Waiting in the Dark

Rell's plan is to throw the two hunters at each other. Mags twists a wick into a bottle of lamp oil, and Hawk takes it up in his talons with his orders passed along the link: drop it nearer the patrol than the swarm, and off to the side, so the patrol arrives first and the bones arrive after. Rell tries to make more of the same for himself, and most of the oil runs out through his fingers and onto the rock.

At the convoy every light goes out. Fabian polices the dark, pinching out any lantern that flickers back to life, and moves among the villagers trying to lift them. They have tripped and fallen the whole way here and are not shy of saying so. His advice on sore legs convinces nobody; these people have survived down here for years and know nonsense when they hear it. A story of a hero who led his people out of bondage and across a desert does a little better. The children lean in to listen, while some of the adults begin to think that this idiot has walked them into a terrible situation. Trevick does what Fabian cannot. Crouched among them, voice barely above a breath, he tells them to stay low and silent, that he knows where the enemy is, that he has hidden before from creatures far larger than these. The murmuring sinks away to nothing.

Far off, Rell's first flare dies as the swarm reaches it. Caspian, listening, hears the great noise change its note, as though for one held moment the whole thing has stopped to look at what it found. Then Hawk's bomb blooms in the distance, a single fist of fire in an ocean of black with nothing around it to catch, and the clatter rolls on.

## Something Watching

Fabian feels it all at once: a prickle at the back of the neck, the sense of eyes on him in a place where nobody could possibly see. Among forty people in the pitch dark it would be easy to shrug off. Mags is certain it is scrying, and wants him to turn it to their advantage by murmuring false directions, perhaps that they are only a mile from the Keep. Caspian and Rell are less sure; anyone watching would see as if they were standing there, and an elf sees perfectly well in the dark. Nobody settles it, and the feeling does not go away.

Trevick picks out the patrol's red smudge again, a little further off, drifting toward where Hawk's fire fell. Later, from that same spot, voices carry across the plain: an exchange of words in a language he does not know, cold rather than friendly, but not the sound of a quarrel. It seems they are talking to Lae'ethorian.

## What Hawk Saw

Hawk, circling above the dead flare, sees the swarm up close, and nobody else sees it like this. From above it is a crescent, a great curved horn of loose bones carried in a wind that blows nowhere else. At its centre sits a knot of darkness that even his eyes slide off. Around the bones hangs a penumbra, a haze of gloom that reaches out ahead of the bones themselves, and as Hawk dips toward it the air turns bitterly cold and tastes of metal, like a mouthful of old coins. It begins to pull at him. He hammers his wings and spirals up and up and out of its reach, and he knows exactly what would have happened if he had faltered: the bones would have taken him apart.

## Silence

The rearguard begins to pick its way back toward the convoy. Mags goes slowly, whispering to Caspian where to set each foot, and Caspian fades out of sight altogether. Reaching the villagers, Mags draws Silence down over them like a sheet, so they can gather their things without a sound, and so that whoever may be listening through Fabian hears nothing at all. Inside it, people move like figures in a dumb-show. Rell stays loose, circling east to get ahead and set another slow-burning flare. Trevick scouts north-east and finds the way choked with rubble and the broken bones of buildings, no road for forty people.

Outside the silence, Caspian hears the clatter swell. It is getting louder, and fast. His breath is starting to show.

## The Argument

Caspian says to Mags what he has been holding back. Reluctant as he was to bring these people out, if Greyface kills them out here he will only grow stronger on their deaths. They have made a terrible mistake, he says, and people are going to die for it. Let Greyface take them back: they will live, and the problem can be returned to. Mags tells him the told-you-so has been noted, and that nobody is ever coming back to this.

Rell argues it the other way. Greyface has spent twelve years eking these people out one at a time; he will not burn the whole village in a single night and leave himself nothing. He will come for the party and leave the villagers be. Caspian thinks it wishful. He has sat across from the man and knows he is not just a man. In the end, for different reasons, they agree to stand with the villagers rather than send them on alone into the dark, and to tell them the truth: fight with us, or run, or go home if you will.

## The Stand

Mags lets the silence fall, and the noise arrives all at once, as though a door has been kicked open on a storm. People start to panic. Fabian makes the speech of his life about the momentous choice they have made, their place among the great men and women of this buried civilisation, standing between fear and the ones they love. But he cannot see a single face, his heart is not in it, and even as he speaks the crowd is coming apart, people breaking and running and bursting into tears.

Mags casts Daylight.

For people who have not seen the sun in years it is like being struck. They cry out and cover their eyes and stagger. And in that sudden brutal noon the party sees what has been coming for them: a wall of bones twenty-five feet high, floating a hand's breadth above the ground, scraping and rattling and kicking up dust, and it is splitting in two. The western half, a dark orb pulsing at its heart, rolls straight at them. The other half peels away and comes sweeping round from the south. Neither slows.

Mags slams her hands together, lifts the symbol of Naelos high, and tells it that it shall not pass. Against ordinary undead she knows the feel of this: the flinch, the terror. There is none of that here, nothing she would call feeling at all; it is like hurling a stone against a brick wall. But the wall cracks. Some of the ancient bones crumble to powder and blow away.

Then the western swarm comes over them like a breaking wave. Mags and Fabian are swallowed in a blizzard of hurtling bone and somehow keep their feet; so do a couple of others, bloodied. Most of the villagers beneath it do not. They are struck down, dead or senseless, and lifted up into the whirl, and it does not let them go. Their bodies turn slowly among the bones, part of the storm now. From behind cover, someone looses a crossbow bolt into it.

Caspian studies the black orb, judges it for what it is, and unpicks it, and to his own astonishment it comes apart in his hands. It was high magic. Where the darkness was, a platform of bones hangs fifteen feet in the air, and on it sits a figure, perfectly still at the centre of the storm, untouched by any of it, turning his head to find whoever just did that. Caspian ducks behind a rock. Trevick marks the figure and looses, and the wall of bone swallows his arrows.

## The Charge

Rell is already moving.

Nobody sees him go. He is invisible, a disturbance in the dust and nothing more, and he is running flat out toward a twenty-five-foot wall of whirling bone that has just swallowed the people he came to save. The ground blurs. The clatter fills the world. There is no plan in it beyond the next stride. Ahead, a villager stands frozen on the edge of the storm. Rell hits his shoulders at a dead sprint, uses him like a step, and launches.

For one heartbeat he is in the air, arms forward, a spear thrown at the heart of the thing. Then he punches head first into the bones.

It is like diving into a mill. Splinters of rib and shoulder-blade tear at him from every side, slicing through cloth and skin, the cold of the penumbra biting into his lungs, the metallic taste flooding his mouth. He does not slow. He bursts through the inner face of the storm and lands, hard, on the edge of the bone platform, fifteen feet above the stone, in a pocket of terrible calm.

The figure on the throne is an elf. It is not the grey dead face the party has spent days talking to. It is the man himself, and he is turning, tensing, aware that something has come through his storm.

He has one second. Rell gives him nothing to do with it. He throws his whole body at the elf, no guard, no footing, no thought of what comes after, arms locking around him, and the two of them topple backwards off the throne and out into the open air.

They never hit the ground. The swarm catches them. They tumble over and over inside it, held up by something stronger than gravity, the bodies of the dead turning past them in the gloom.

Fabian, still trapped somewhere below in the bone-storm, lies on his back, sights up through the chaos, and as the two of them come wheeling past he strikes the golden lute and blasts Thunderwave straight up into them. Bones shatter into a spray of shards. A gap tears open, and Fabian claws his way through it and out into the daylight, and drinks a potion with shaking hands.

Mags, deep inside the storm, claps her hands again and calls up Spirit Guardians, and they come as great shining moths of Naelos, wings like burnished chitin, beating the bones back from her. Here the two powers meet face to face, the very clash Greyface showed them in the Ossuary when he wound her Guidance into his own magic until it broke, and now it is no demonstration. The air crackles and spits between them, and she has to lean her whole will against it to keep the light from being crushed. She holds it. Then something happens she never expected. As the body of a villager the swarm has taken drifts into the circle of her moths, she feels a gathering-in, a sheltering: someone only moments dead, whose soul has not yet had time to leave, and who would otherwise have been fed to the swarm. She abandons the hunt for the elf at once and spends herself wading through the storm after the dead, drawing them in one by one, until she has sheltered seven.

## In the Grip

Inside the whirl, the elf does not try to break free. He clings to Rell as tightly as Rell clings to him, and he pours necromancy into him. There is no finesse in it, no craft; he simply opens a sluice and lets it flood. Rell has been hurt badly before, and this is not like being hurt. It is a cold that starts at the centre of him and spreads outward, and with it a terrible isolation, as if every person he has ever known is being drawn away down a long corridor until he is utterly alone. He knows, with total clarity, that if this goes on much longer what is being taken will not come back. He sinks to the very edge. Only the link itself keeps him conscious, a lantern held on for someone else's purposes. Around them the elf gathers the swarm back to his will and thickens the bones about himself like a shell. His astonishment is gone. What has replaced it is fury, and there is something righteous in it, as though Rell has trespassed against the natural order of things.

Caspian sends Magic Missile streaking into the storm at him, and the darts strike home, and the elf's grip does not loosen. Then Trevick steps out from behind his rock and draws. Between him and his target are ten feet of whirling bone and a man wrapped around a man, and as the arrow flies the elf throws up a shimmer of magic to turn it. It goes through all of it. Bone, shield and all, it finds him, and the flood of necromancy into Rell is cut off like a slammed door.

Rell is going under. With the last of his strength he locks his arms even tighter, a dead man's grip, so that if he goes he takes the elf with him. Then a bone cracks across the side of his neck and the world goes out.

The southern swarm sweeps in and crashes straight into Mags's moths. This time she cannot hold them. They buckle at the edges, flicker, and fail. But the souls she has gathered do not scatter. They flow toward her, nothing anyone could see, and settle within her, and she knows she will carry them for as long as she stays whole, conscious and faithful. The southern swarm takes three more villagers who had not run far enough.

Fabian waves the children in behind the big rock, then rounds on the elf, flings out an arm and declares that by turning on the people he swore to protect, he has become cursed. The curse slides off him. Fabian gives Trevick heart instead. Mags, battered and bleeding, calls on Naelos, god of order and of all things in their proper place, to pin the elf where he hangs, and he shrugs it off.

## He Leaves

The elf has been dragged off his throne, stripped of his darkness, shot through his own shield, and he weighs what he came for against how far he has got. The swarm stops. Then, with a groan like a ship coming about, it reverses and begins to pour back across the plain the way it came. As it turns, Caspian picks out the exact patch of storm beneath Rell's limp body and unmakes it, and Rell drops out of the sky onto the stone. Mags gets to him, steadies him, and pours healing into him until he is whole again.

In the merciless daylight the cost is laid out on the ground. Twenty of the thirty-four villagers are alive, and the dog. Fourteen are dead. Every one of the children lived, though six of them have lost a parent and little Daisy Thorngage has lost both. Varen the innkeeper is gone, and Kithri's son Ned, and old Oshin Half-toes, one of the two who had been down here longest. And the Provost was among those taken, and with her went the only knowledge of where the tunnels lie. Whether she is one of the seven Mags carries, Mags cannot tell.

Fabian gathers the survivors before they scatter. Nearly everyone has lost someone and the grief is raw and loud, but a good number of them have already decided that the man who leapt into the storm is a hero. The old dog has come through it. The patrol is still out there somewhere in the dark beyond the light, and the clatter of the bones grows fainter, rolling away.
