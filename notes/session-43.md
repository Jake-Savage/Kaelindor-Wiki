# Session 43 - 10/11/2026

## The Prisoner

Rell puts the party's questions to the commander mind to mind, so that the man never has to speak. Every question takes a little time to get across, but once understood he answers readily, angry and bombastic, and not at all reluctant. The party learns the following:

- He is baffled to be asked about the surface. He cannot see why anyone would want to go there, and as far as he is concerned there is no way to it.
- He and his company are the acolytes of the Devourer, servants of it in its holy work of destroying everything above. They are limitless, he says, hundreds and thousands of them, and the party can expect to be hunted.
- The plains are constantly patrolled and watched, and any non-believers who pollute the body of the God will be caught and sacrificed. Leaving makes no sense to him: the Devourer devours everything, here or above, and since the party is here they are good candidates for sacrifice.
- He knows the witch, once he understands who is meant. Hers is a place of serious pollution of the body of the Devourer, a problem for a long time. His people can see it from far away, but whenever they mount an attack and get close, it is not there. He is not sure it is real. What he describes as hideous is, Rell slowly realises, a garden: something green growing down here.
- Lae'ethorian is to him a high priest of the Devourer, who came some time ago. He has been behaving very strangely, keeping sacrifices in readiness for a long time rather than sacrificing them, but he is a high priest and must have his reasons. When the patrol spoke to him earlier that night, he told them he would take what he needed and they could have whatever was left.
- He has been down here a long time.

He is humanoid and plainly undead, his skin papery and ancient over the bones, shrunken and shrivelled, and whatever he once was cannot be told from what is left. Rell looks him over and learns nothing more. With nothing else worth the time, and more of his kind surely coming, Rell quietly ends him.

## The Bodies

The six bodies, for one patrolman got away, carry nothing obviously magical. Their robes are a uniform with a common insignia, old and preserved by magic, quite unlike the new robes Lae'ethorian wore. Caspian takes one and suggests everyone does; Mags does. Each of them wears rings as a focus for their spells on the three middle fingers of the left hand, three for the two casters and one for each of the mace-men, and Rell takes those.

Rell studies the commander's face until he could wear it, then mashes the faces of all the dead so that no one will know which patrolman is missing. To the party they all look much alike anyway.

## On the March

The caves are still about three hours off, by where the party believes them to be, and harder to find without the Provost. The convoy moves under low lights turned to the ground, enough to keep people from falling. Mags finds the way with Trevick at her side, Rell scouts ahead, and Caspian watches the rear, half blinded by the Daylight behind them. Fabian's fruit cake, which turns out to heal like a potion, is shared out among the wounded as the night goes on, a bite for Rosie, then for Trevick, Sarah Farlin and Mothrigg, until there is very little left.

The first half hour goes the right way. Then, as the lantern light passes across a dark and empty stretch, the shadows in the lee of a rock to the south-east begin to twist and move of their own accord, and reach out hungrily for the convoy. There are five of them, and their touch is cold. The villagers do not run. May Tegg puts an arrow into one. Olo Halfkettle twists her ankle on the first swing and later charges with a bloodcurdling halfling battle cry. Del Zoric stabs one and shrugs off its touch. Tom Pruett hits himself in the face with his own club. Rell's acid dissolves two, Caspian's Fire Bolt burns two more, the last of them to a bonfire, and Trevick shoots one down. Nobody dies. Del refuses any cake, and Olo paints blood under her eyes.

## Two Lost

The next half hour goes wrong, with Caspian leading, and somewhere along it someone looks round and asks where Bohdan Tarek is. He has been gone some time, and something has taken him. He was the person Max the dog loved best, and the old spaniel whimpers until Trevick takes him in hand; from then on Max keeps to Trevick's heels.

The half hour after goes right again, but Finnan Highcroft drops off the back of the group, and by the time anyone notices, something has taken him. Nobody knows what. The convoy is seventeen now, and the dog.

## The Rider

In the distance something large is moving fast across the plain, roughly away from them, like a rider on a horse but not quite. Rell slips away to look. It is a woman on a great tiger, bent low over its neck to urge it on, with little sparks of light dancing around her. She is alive, she wears a dress entirely unsuited to the place, she carries a staff, and Rell is fairly sure she is a caster.

He calls out to her. She pulls up at once with the staff ready, and he steps out of cover with his hands raised and greets her well met in terrible circumstances. A little impatient at the interruption, she lowers the staff and answers that it is unexpected, but yes, well met.

Rell tells her who they are: fleeing, with civilians under their protection, trying to find a way out of the creature's guts. She tells him to make haste, because she believes the creature is about to return to the depths, and anyone who wants out should go now. Looking from the Keep to the far-off lights of the convoy, she takes them for prisoners escaped from the Keep, which sacrifices anyone it finds down here. Rell corrects her: these people were held by a man who calls himself a high priest. Her eyes narrow, and she says that might explain a few things.

The tiger paces, and she steadies it with a hand. "He let them go? He really is leaving." Told that the party drove him off, she looks openly surprised and asks what caused this change of heart. Rell tells her they did: they would not leave the villagers to be slowly consumed. She is torn between interest and the urge to ride on. Asked simply to point the way, she looks at him with some pity and says that as far as she knows there is no ready way out of here.

Rell opens his mind to hers. She takes the link readily, and it feels strong, but she has to go; she wants to find out what is happening. With a curt nod she urges the tiger on, the lights around her quickening, and she rides away unmistakably toward the Ossuary.

As she goes, Rell learns what he can. She is an elf, and cagey: she gives no name, and will not say how long she has been down here, though she clearly lives here rather than passing through. She is very surprised that Rell knows the name Lae'ethorian. They are allies of a sort, she says, and it is plain that her feelings about him are mixed at best. He has sent her a message saying that he means to return to the surface, and she is going to him to find out what is going on. Rell asks her not to mention the party to him. She hears it, but she makes no promise and raises no objection, and he cannot tell what she means to do. Then he asks whether she is the witch the Keep spoke of. She certainly is, and she finds it very funny that he heard of her from them. She plainly enjoys running rings round them.

The tiger, Rell can tell, is no flesh-and-blood animal but a summoned thing. He brings everything back to the party. Caspian does not like the sound of allies of a sort; Mags distrusts her on principle. Rell points out that she could have killed him and did not, and that she keeps away from the Keep, which Lae'ethorian does not. If Lae'ethorian is leaving, the party reckons, it is because they took his supply, and what he told the patrol he needed was enough life to make the journey. He told them once that he would send no word to the surface at all.

## The Pool

The next stretch goes the wrong way and passes a pool, and Mags has the villagers kept well back from it. Something comes out of the water: large, gross, with something smeared across its hands that it tries to press onto Caspian's face. It misses, bites him instead, then breaks past him toward little Elsie Bowditch, who scrambles away. Its skin has a poisonous sheen that splashes back on anyone who strikes it, and Mothrigg, who clubs it, takes the worst of it. Rell meets it with a Shadow Blade in one hand and his scimitar in the other, and Trevick shoots it dead.

Trevick spots a second lurking at the water's edge. This one presses its goo onto Mags, sticky and burning, and she feels it meld into her flesh and begin to burrow. Old Mothrigg, near death already, throws himself at the creature and goes down under the splash; Mags keeps him alive. Maddie Pruett tries to cut the thing out of Mags and cannot. Del Zoric stabs the spitter in the foot with a cry of triumph. In the end Mags cuts it out herself, with Sarah Farlin helping, and it costs her: inside the goo is a grub, a parasite, which she throws into the pool before Trevick can keep it. Rell finishes the creature and the poison drops him, and Mags brings him round. The last of the cake goes to Mothrigg and Rell, with a stern word to Mothrigg about staying out of fights.

## Laughter

For all of it they are still about two hours from the caves: an hour gone the right way, and an hour the wrong. Trevick leads the next stretch with Mags beside him, and it goes the right way. And somewhere along it, for the first time on the whole journey, the villagers are laughing. Trevick gave one of them a copy of Caspian's book, and they are reading the cleverest parts aloud to each other.
