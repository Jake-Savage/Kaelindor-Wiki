# Session 35 - 15/09/2026

30 Dusklin, 1568. The party is in the water at the bottom of the pit, where the collapsing staircase left them. Rell alone is on the ledge, thirty feet above the surface.

## The Pit

The party establishes the shape of the problem before anything moves:

- The water is about forty feet deep, warm, and thoroughly opaque. The whole chamber is warm and humid.
- The walls of the pit are wet and slick. They are not impossible to climb, but they are very difficult, and there is an overhang at most points.
- The carved section of wall is much easier, because the carvings themselves give footholds. It is also where the metallic spiders are nested, beneath their canopy.
- At the top of the carving there is a hole in the ceiling. Climbing it is the only apparent way out of the chamber other than back up the ruined stair.

Mags proposes Water Walk. Something strong enough could still drag a person under, but the buoyancy would hold: the moment it let go, they would come straight back up, and it would have to fight the water to keep them down at all. Rell, in heavy armour, will be slow in the water as long as he keeps it on.

## The Hydra

A jaw closes on Trevick's ankle almost at once and drags him beneath the surface. The water is too deep and too muddy for anyone to see what has him.

It surfaces momentarily to snap at Caspian, and that is the party's first sight of it: a hydra, with five long necks and lizard-like, snake-like heads full of jagged teeth, pale and unhealthy-looking. It is large, strong and very hard to injure. It bites with one head at a time, because it is not in a hurry.

Mags moves five feet so that the whole party is within range, ducks under the water to locate Trevick at the cost of an opportunity attack, and casts Water Walk. Everyone is lifted onto the surface of the pool except Trevick, who is held, though he feels the pull upward and knows it will be easier to break free now.

Caspian casts Spider Climb, takes Rell's rope on his way past, and gets out of the water entirely and onto the ledge. Fabian discovers he has no rope in his pack, fails to throw one thirty feet up to his unseen servant, and casts Mirror Image instead. Rell, above, anchors a rope using his climber's kit and throws the remaining twenty-five feet down into the water.

Trevick is submerged for most of the fight, and is a moment from drowning by the end of it. His firebolt is spoiled by the water; his attempt to twist free fails. Three things get him out:

- Rell casts Dissonant Whispers at it again and again. Each time it recoils and flees as far from him as it can, which drags Trevick further but breaks its hold on the position it wanted.
- Mags walks forward fifteen feet, points straight down, and casts Daylight into the pool. Forty feet of opaque water becomes something in which shapes can be made out. The party can now see the hydra's silhouette, and Trevick's.
- Caspian places a Fireball underwater, aimed closer to Trevick than he would otherwise dare, judging accurately how the fire will spread down there. Magical fire does catch underwater, though it does not billow out as far as it would in air, and it does not boil the water around it. The water gets warmer; nothing in it is cooked.

The Fireball forces the creature to release Trevick. Fabian follows it by ducking his head, and all three of his mirror images, under the filthy water, strumming his lute and speaking a single Command: drop. He surfaces with the observation that of five heads not one of them could be considered beautiful.

Trevick pops to the surface prone, the Water Walk carrying him. He summons Hawk to watch the water and makes for the rope. Mags heals him with Healing Word. Rell drives the hydra back down twice more as it tries to come up under people, and it eventually dives out of range and stays there. It is not killed.

## The Bottom of the Pool

With the Daylight still burning below the surface, Trevick pieces together what he saw while he was down there. The party learns:

- At one side of the chamber floor, debris and detritus have accumulated, as though being drawn toward a point. This is taken to be a drain.
- Among the muck around it are one or two shiny objects, visible as glints in the mud, and bones. People have died in this pit before, and had good things on them when they did.
- At the opposite side is an area considerably clearer than the rest of the floor, which the party reads as the point where water comes in.
- There are no strong currents, and the water has the character of being largely still. It is stagnant now, which does not mean it always was.

Trevick offers to dive for the shiny things if someone keeps the hydra occupied, and Mags agrees to cover him with Chill Touch — though it is pointed out that Chill Touch will not drive the creature off the way Rell's whispers did, and is unlikely to trouble something that size at all. Both of them talk themselves out of it, and the party leaves the drain alone. Whether there is a mechanism controlling the water or simply a blockage is not resolved; Rell's suggestion is that clearing the drain might empty the pool entirely.

## The Climb

Rell climbs the carved section of wall. Two-thirds of the way up, a mechanism triggered by his presence on the climb itself whirs into life, and blades emerge from the narrow slits cut into the carving.

Not all of them still function. Enough do to make the climb lethal. Rell has kept track of where the openings were as he went and places himself accordingly, and a couple of the blades graze his skin without any of them piercing him. He is conscious afterwards of having been lucky: climbing this without being caught is a matter of chance more than judgement.

Two things are learned on the way past the spiders:

- They are rusting. Flakes of rust come down as they shift, and they are plainly not in good repair after a long time in a damp chamber. There are a great many of them, piled on one another past counting, and they are becoming more wakeful.
- Seen close up they are very intricate, and the way they move is naturalistic. Rell is not aware of constructs being made to that standard as a rule.

Most significantly, they do not react to Rell at all. When the blades open they skitter and shift as though looking, but they never home in on him. This proves his theory: their means of detection is magical rather than ordinary sight, and his amulet of proof against detection defeats it.

## The Forge

Rell climbs over the lip into the space above. It is a room clearly of dwarven origin, and clearly part of a forge: trays of the kind molten metal is poured into to be worked, and a great many tools scattered around.

- Everything is in considerable disarray, and the place is very ancient. Almost anything not made of stone crumbles when touched.
- Parts of the walls have buckled inward. There is pressure on the room from outside.
- Nothing in the room is operable. Most of what it held is damaged or has been crushed away under fallen wall.
- The construct parts laid out here are, even at a glance, sophisticated.
- Two doors lead further into the complex, both matching the architecture the party has already seen. A significant crack in one wall opens into natural fissures in the rock.

Rell's judgement, relayed down the telepathic link, is that dwarven temples commonly contain forges and that the party is now in the temple complex proper. He also establishes that the corridor above, the pit, and this room were all originally part of the same structure, pushed slightly out of shape since.

## The Regalia

The party stops to rest, and Caspian works Identify over both magical pieces recovered from beneath the golden courtyard.

The hand-piece, which the table settles on calling the handlet:

- It was made for a protector or bodyguard. It is not obviously martial; it is ornate and decorative, and its purpose is defensive.
- Its use is this: when something is worked against a person standing beside its wearer, the wearer may take the force of it onto themselves instead, and if they can shrug it off, the person beside them is spared it too. The risk is real. If the wearer cannot shrug it off, they have simply taken the thing onto themselves as well.
- It must be attuned to before it will work.

Mags takes it, on the grounds that she is better at shrugging such things off than anyone else in the party, and on the memory of being turned into a pincushion in the shifting library. Like most magical items of its kind, it begins far too large for her hands and adjusts itself to fit.

The crown, which Fabian names the Dragon Crown:

- It carries traces of enchantment magic such that in some circumstances it may enhance charisma-based checks, though not necessarily charisma-based magic. The effect is circumstantial and will not trigger in every instance, and what triggers it is unknown.
- It is magical enough to stay on the head despite being absurdly proportioned.

Caspian passes it to Fabian with the observation that he would benefit from it and would suit it more than anyone present. Fabian spends the rest wearing it and trying to keep it balanced, and it proves magical enough to stay on his head regardless. One further effect surfaces as he wears it: he catches his thoughts beginning to express themselves in Draconic, the Draconic word rising to mind ahead of its equivalent.

## What They Came For

With a decision to make about pressing on, the party restates what it is actually doing here, and corrects itself in the process. Fabian's version is that they were chasing cultists and there is something culty below. Rell corrects it: they intercepted the Brood's communications, and the instruction given was to go into the space beneath the disappeared village and find something that should not be there. That instruction never named the Stonehunger itself. Rell's reading is that the Stonehunger has consumed things from across its range and across history, and that the object the Brood want may be one of them rather than the Stonehunger itself. Mags counters that the courtyard they have already found may be the thing that should not be there.

Everyone elects to press on. Whatever route back to the surface exists, it is somewhere ahead: the gate above sealed behind them, and the Stonehunger closes behind what leaves it.

## Clearing the Canopy

The plan agreed is to destroy the spiders before setting the rope, since the rope would not survive the spell. Caspian turns his study during the rest to Shatter.

The cost is understood and accepted in advance: it will not be possible to shatter the constructs without also shattering large parts of the carving beneath them.

Caspian moves in under the canopy and directs the spell upward. The stone is old and relatively brittle, and the constructs are old and brittle too. The effect on them is devastating. Metal, stone and dust rain down, the canopy itself collapses into the water with a large splash, and the spiders come down with it, a few of them scrabbling briefly in the water where they land. Caspian jumps clear of the falling masonry.

The costs are noted:

- A great deal of the upper carving surface is very badly damaged. Chunks have fallen away and much of the rest is crumbly. Climbing the carving itself is now very difficult.
- The slots the blades emerge from are in some places more exposed than before, but the mechanism itself shows no sign of damage. The blades will still trigger.

Rell lowers a rope and sets the climber's kit so that it hangs well clear of the wall, out of reach of the blades, and ties a series of loops into it to make it something closer to a ladder. Trevick starts up, begins to swing badly, grabs the wall to steady himself and is stabbed for his trouble, then drops back to the platform to start again. Fabian fares little better and jumps off to restart. Mags goes up cleanly. Trevick and Fabian both make it on their second attempts, ungainly but successful.

The party is in the forge. It is about eight or nine in the evening; they went down to the landslide in the late afternoon and entered the complex in the early evening. The stated intention is to take a long rest here, with Caspian's Leomund's Tiny Hut and Rell's strings and bells, and then go on through one of the two doors.
